## Top of mind

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair at 10am ET today, and the stakes are unusually high: the 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.31% on August 17 (its highest since 2007), the Treasury Department stepped into the bond market on August 19 to try to cap long-end borrowing costs, and strategists are calling today's speech a "key risk event" for both bonds and the dollar. That backdrop lands right after a Thursday session that looked strong on the surface — the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both closed at highs on Nvidia's and Salesforce's earnings — but the equal-weight S&P actually fell, and Marvell dropped 7.6% after hours despite record revenue, a sign the market is getting choosier about which "AI trade" names it rewards.

That narrowing sets up an outsized reaction, in either direction, to whatever Warsh says at 10am.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote at 10am ET
**Source:** CNBC, NPR, Bloomberg
**So what:** This is the single biggest catalyst of the day. A CNBC survey found 45% of economists don't expect Warsh to give explicit forward guidance on rates — he has argued policymakers should speak less, not more — but 80% of surveyed economists want more insight into his economic thinking regardless. Traders are pricing a 66% chance the Fed holds rates steady in September and a 34% chance of a hike; strategists warn that if Warsh doesn't address the bond market's anxiety directly, the 30-year yield's run toward 5.5%+ could resume.

### 2. Marvell falls 7.6% after hours despite record $2.74 billion revenue
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, TradingKey
**So what:** Revenue rose 37% year over year and beat consensus, EPS topped estimates, and management raised its fiscal 2027 and 2028 outlooks — yet the stock still sold off. The likely culprit: a roughly 90-basis-point sequential dip in the non-GAAP gross-margin midpoint and uncertainty about the timing of revenue from Marvell's Google custom-silicon deal. It's a reminder that "beat and raise" isn't automatically enough when a stock has already rallied hard into the print.

### 3. Nvidia and software earnings power a record close, but breadth is thin
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance, FXLeaders
**So what:** Nvidia closed up roughly 8-9% on its revenue beat and guidance, while Salesforce (+22-23%, helped by a deepened Anthropic partnership), Okta (+28%), and CrowdStrike (+20.5%) posted some of their best days of the year on their own earnings beats. But the equal-weight S&P 500 fell 0.3% the same day — a reminder that a handful of mega-cap prints, not a broad economic signal, are doing the heavy lifting on the index level.

### 4. Jobless claims fall to 203,000, keeping the labor market resilient
**Source:** Department of Labor, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Initial claims came in below the 208,000 forecast and down from the prior week's 207,000, with the four-week average holding near 205,500. A labor market that isn't cracking gives Warsh less cover to lean dovish today, reinforcing the CNBC survey finding that a majority of economists still expect hikes, not cuts, over the next year.

### 5. Gold pulls back, bond and dollar markets "on edge" into the speech
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Gold eased to around $4,590/oz Thursday, its biggest one-day pullback in a week, as investors de-risked ahead of Warsh. The more important context is the Treasury's own August 19 intervention to cap long-end yields — an unusual step that has effectively made the Fed's communication today, not just its policy rate, the thing markets are trading around.

## Ideas — long-term core

### MRVL — Marvell Technology
- **Thesis:** Record quarterly revenue, a 37% year-over-year growth rate, guidance raised for both fiscal 2027 and 2028, and a specific, quantifiable growth driver (the Google custom-silicon partnership) make this a name where the after-hours selloff looks more like a valuation reset than a fundamental problem.
- **Valuation note:** Worth checking the stock's post-drop multiple against its own three-year average and against Broadcom's, since both trade on the same "custom AI silicon" thesis — a 7.6% single-day repricing on a beat-and-raise quarter usually means the stock got ahead of itself, not that the business did.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The after-hours drop is exactly the kind of "good quarter, stock still falls" setup long-term buyers should research rather than chase — patience through Friday's full cash-session reaction is more useful than trying to catch the exact bottom.
- **Risks / bear case:** The margin compression (90 bps sequential) and the market's specific concern about *when* Google revenue converts to cash flow are real, not just sentiment — if next quarter shows further margin erosion without a clear timeline on the Google ramp, the bear case strengthens.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### TLT (20+ Year Treasury) — Warsh's Jackson Hole reaction trade
- **Catalyst:** Warsh's 10am ET keynote is a binary-ish event for long-duration bonds — strategists have explicitly framed it as the swing factor for whether the 30-year yield stabilizes or resumes its push toward 5.5%+.
- **Time horizon:** Hours to a few days — this is about the immediate market reaction to the speech and the following session, not a multi-week bond call.
- **What would invalidate:** A speech seen as dodging the bond market's concerns (per the 45% of economists who expect no forward guidance) without offsetting clarity on his economic view would likely extend the long-yield selloff, invalidating a long-duration long; a clearer, more measured tone would do the opposite.
- **Risk note:** Long-duration Treasury products are highly rate-sensitive by design — size any position with the understanding that a single speech could move it several percentage points in either direction today.

### CRM — Salesforce post-earnings follow-through
- **Catalyst:** Wednesday's report and the Anthropic partnership drove a 22-23% single-day pop, one of the stock's best days on record; the question now is whether that holds through a full week of trading or gives back gains once the initial reaction fades.
- **Time horizon:** Days — watching whether Friday and next week's sessions confirm the move or fade it.
- **What would invalidate:** A retracement of more than half the earnings-day gain on average-or-higher volume within the next week would suggest the initial reaction overshot; sector-wide software weakness (unrelated to Salesforce's own numbers) would also undercut the thesis.
- **Risk note:** A 20%+ single-day move already priced in a lot of good news — this is a momentum/follow-through trade, not a fresh valuation thesis, so size it accordingly and don't treat it as a long-term entry point.
