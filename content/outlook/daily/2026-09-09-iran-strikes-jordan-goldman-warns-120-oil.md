## Top of mind

The US-Iran war widened again Tuesday: US forces destroyed five more Iranian tankers tied to the IRGC, and Iran retaliated by firing 18 ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan (Jordan's air defenses intercepted all but two, which landed in unpopulated areas). Oil pushed higher into Wednesday — WTI near $94.66, Brent near $99.44 — and Goldman Sachs is now flagging a risk case where Brent tops $120 if Hormuz disruptions persist into 2027 (its base case is still $80). Layer on a 10-year yield that briefly cleared 4.8% Tuesday, the highest since October 2023, and Friday's CPI print becomes the week's real swing factor for both stocks and the September 16 Fed decision.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. US destroys five more Iranian tankers; Iran strikes US base in Jordan
**Source:** Washington Post, NBC News, Al Jazeera, CBC
**So what:** CENTCOM says it struck five IRGC-linked tankers Tuesday (four in the Gulf of Oman, one near Kharg Island) after Iran attempted to attack a US warship. Iran retaliated with a barrage of 18 ballistic missiles at Al Azraq base in Jordan; Jordanian defenses intercepted all but two. This is the same escalation pattern that hit Jordan and the Gulf states earlier this month — each exchange resets the market's assumption about how long the disruption to Gulf shipping lasts, which is the whole ballgame for oil and diesel prices.

### 2. Goldman Sachs flags $120 Brent as a risk scenario, not base case
**Source:** CBS News, OilPrice.com, TradingKey
**So what:** Goldman's base case still assumes gradual de-escalation and ~$80 Brent in Q4, but it's now explicitly modeling a scenario above $120 if Hormuz-area attacks intensify and Gulf export flows — already reportedly below 45% of pre-war levels — stay impaired into 2027. The gap between Goldman's base case and risk case is itself the signal: nobody has real conviction here, so expect the oil complex to keep trading headline-to-headline.

### 3. AI-disruption fear hits enterprise software: Salesforce, Intuit down ~4%, ServiceNow ~5%
**Source:** Motley Fool, 24/7 Wall St.
**So what:** The sell-off was tied to renewed concern that OpenAI's newest model could encroach on tasks specialized software platforms currently charge for. Salesforce shares are still up sharply over the past month on strong Q2 AI-linked results, so Tuesday reads more like a single-day repricing of competitive risk than a thesis-breaking move — but it's the kind of headline that can compound if it recurs.

### 4. Canada's retaliatory tariffs on $27.6B of US goods took effect Tuesday
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Washington Times, Canada.ca
**So what:** Ottawa's counter-tariffs (15-50% across steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp/paper and electronics) followed the collapse of trade talks after the US's Section 232/338 tariffs on Canadian goods took effect August 22. It's a second macro headwind layering onto the Iran story — narrower in scope than the oil shock, but another source of margin pressure for US exporters and importers with Canadian exposure.

### 5. Chewy reports before the bell today; Oracle and Adobe still ahead Thursday
**Source:** Investing.com, Simply Wall St, Bloomberg options data
**So what:** Chewy reports fiscal Q2 before the open with consensus at $0.36 EPS on $3.32B revenue; Bloomberg options pricing implies a swing of roughly 10% on the print. GameStop's Tuesday report (revenue beat, adjusted EPS in line, collectibles up 57%, but total revenue down ~19% YoY on the loss of the Switch 2 tailwind) is a reminder that a top-line beat doesn't guarantee a rally when the underlying trend is still declining — worth keeping in mind heading into Chewy's own print.
