## Top of mind

Friday's July jobs report showed employers cut 23,000 positions against a forecast 83,000-job gain, plus a combined 103,000-job downward revision to May and June — and stocks rallied to a fresh S&P 500 record (7,757.64, +0.62%) as traders cut the odds of a September Fed hike to roughly 42% from about 58% the day before. The catch: unemployment's drop to 4.1% came partly from a shrinking labor force, not more hiring, and it lands three weeks after the Fed's most divided vote since 2016 (9-3, with three sitting regional presidents dissenting in favor of a hike) — which means Wednesday's CPI report, not Friday's jobs number, is the print that actually decides whether this rally holds. Underneath the index-level calm (VIX 14.90), single stocks are moving 24-37% on earnings in a single session — Atlassian and Twilio both posted blowout beats Friday — a reminder that dispersion, not the VIX, is where this market's real risk lives right now.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. July payrolls shrink by 23,000, blowing past forecasts of an 83,000 gain
**Source:** BLS, CNBC, NBC News
**So what:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics also revised May and June payrolls down by a combined 103,000 jobs, and the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% from an expected-unchanged 4.2% — but largely because the labor force participation rate fell, not because more people found work. Rate futures cut the odds of a September Fed hike to roughly 42-44% from 57-58% a day earlier, and the 10-year yield fell to about 4.65% from 4.67%.

### 2. Atlassian rockets roughly 37% on a blowout Q4 beat and AI-driven guidance
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, StockStory
**So what:** Atlassian's fiscal Q4 EPS of $1.87 beat estimates of $1.50, and revenue of $1.766 billion beat the $1.66 billion consensus, with first-quarter revenue guidance also topping Wall Street's estimate on the strength of AI features layered into Jira. Shares closed near $149-151, up from Thursday's $110.17 close — one of the single largest one-day earnings pops of the year for a company this size.

### 3. Twilio jumps roughly 24% as a Q2 beat and raised guidance validate its turnaround story
**Source:** ts2.tech, StocksToTrade
**So what:** Twilio's adjusted EPS of $1.47 beat the $1.32 estimate and revenue of $1.5 billion (+22% year over year) beat the $1.43 billion consensus; the company also raised full-year organic growth guidance to 13-13.5% and guided Q3 revenue and EPS above Street estimates. Shares closed at $239.34, up from $193.20 Thursday, with several sell-side firms lifting price targets into the $240-260 range on the print.

### 4. A historically divided Fed raises the stakes for Wednesday's CPI
**Source:** CNBC, TechTimes
**So what:** The Fed's July 29 decision to hold rates at 3.50-3.75% passed 9-3, with Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, and Dallas's Lorie Logan all formally dissenting in favor of a hike — the most fractured hawkish vote since September 2016. With three sitting regional presidents already on record wanting tighter policy, a hot CPI print Wednesday has a much shorter path to actually moving the committee than it would with a unanimous, dovish-leaning Fed.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### MSFT — Microsoft
- **Thesis:** Azure has crossed a $100 billion annualized revenue run rate and Copilot has scaled past 30 million paid seats, giving Microsoft two compounding, high-margin growth engines layered on top of an already-dominant enterprise software franchise — a rare combination of AI-cycle exposure and cash-generative durability.
- **Valuation note:** Trading around a 25-26x forward P/E against roughly 32% earnings growth and a ~47% operating margin — not cheap, but not stretched relative to the growth rate, and below its own 12-month average P/E.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. Shares are already up more than 25% over the trailing week on the back of the Q4 report — this is a name to accumulate on pullbacks into the CPI/Fed-driven volatility ahead, not chase at the highs.
- **Risks / bear case:** After that scale of a run, a lot of AI-capex optimism is already priced in; any sign of Azure growth deceleration, disappointing AI monetization, or a broader capex-ROI scare (a theme that's already hit chip and memory names this month) would compress the multiple quickly.

## Ideas — opportunistic

### TEAM — Atlassian, post-earnings momentum watch
- **Catalyst:** A ~37% one-day pop on a clean beat-and-raise quarter with AI-driven Jira adoption as the specific, quantifiable driver — not just a sentiment move.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a couple of weeks, watching whether the gain holds or fades as the initial earnings reaction gets digested.

### TWLO — Twilio, guidance-validated breakout watch
- **Catalyst:** A ~24% pop on a Q2 beat plus raised full-year organic growth guidance (13-13.5%) and multiple sell-side price-target hikes — evidence the "Rule of 40" turnaround story has real numbers behind it now, not just a promise.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks, through the next quarter's guidance update, watching for follow-through buying versus a fade.
- **What would invalidate:** A slide back below the low-$200s would signal the market doubting whether the raised guidance sticks; watch broader software-sector tape for confirmation or contradiction.
- **Risk note:** Communications-platform names have a history of sharp reversals on modest guidance disappointments (Twilio itself, and monday.com in past quarters) — this is a momentum trade, not a re-rating to hold blind.
