## Top of mind

Markets had their best day in about a month on Thursday after Fed Governor Waller signaled he'd support holding rates steady if inflation keeps easing — but that relief rally runs straight into this morning's August jobs report, the last major data point before the September 16 FOMC decision. Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole turn and a war-driven oil spike toward $92 have already pushed hike odds above 55%, so a hot payrolls number reopens the debate Waller just tried to close, while a soft one (echoing July's outright loss of jobs) argues the Fed should stand down. Underneath Thursday's bounce, market breadth and RSI have been quietly deteriorating since mid-August — a reminder the rally's foundation is narrower than the index level suggests.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. August jobs report is today's swing factor for the Fed
**Source:** CNBC, Kiplinger
**So what:** Consensus looks for roughly 53,000-58,000 jobs added and unemployment holding at 4.1%, following a July report that showed an outright loss of jobs against expectations for a gain. A moderate rebound supports the "labor market cooling, not cracking" case the Fed wants to see; another weak print muddies a September decision that's already close to a coin flip.

### 2. Waller's dovish comments spark the best rally in a month — but he's not the one calling the shots
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Reuters coverage
**So what:** Fed Governor Waller said he'd back holding rates steady if price pressures keep easing, and long-dated yields fell as the dollar weakened, lifting all three major indices. But Waller is one voice on the committee — Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks were the hawkish outlier that moved hike odds from the 30s into the 60s in the first place, so today's data carries more weight than any single governor's comments.

### 3. Oil extends its war-driven surge toward $92, on pace for its biggest weekly gain since July
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** WTI is up more than 9% this week as renewed US-Iran hostilities and fears of prolonged disruption through the Strait of Hormuz keep a geopolitical risk premium baked into crude. That's the inflation wildcard the Fed has to weigh against a cooling labor market — a scenario where growth slows and energy costs rise simultaneously is the least comfortable one for policymakers.

### 4. Market breadth and RSI have quietly deteriorated even as the index sits near highs
**Source:** Investing.com, StockCharts technical analysis
**So what:** The share of S&P 500 stocks trading above their 50-day moving average has dropped below 50% after topping 70% in mid-August, and the index's RSI has slipped from above 65 to just above 50 over the same stretch. Narrowing participation while the headline index holds up is a classic late-rally warning sign, not a reason to panic on its own — but it's worth watching alongside today's data.

### XOM — Exxon Mobil
- **Thesis:** A structurally higher, geopolitically-driven oil price (WTI near $92, up 9% this week alone) supports free cash flow for the majors while disciplined capex and buyback programs remain intact — Exxon's integrated model captures upside in both upstream and downstream without the single-basin concentration risk of smaller E&Ps.
- **Valuation note:** Trades at a valuation reasonable relative to its own history when oil sits in the high-$80s to low-$90s range, with a dividend still well-covered by free cash flow even at lower oil prices than today's.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The current setup is timely — an active war keeping a risk premium in crude gives near-term earnings a tailwind — but the thesis should hold up even if the premium fades, since the underlying business doesn't depend on the conflict continuing.
- **Risks / bear case:** A swift de-escalation or an OPEC+ supply response could send oil back toward $75-80 quickly, compressing near-term earnings estimates even if the long-term thesis is intact.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### KRE — Regional banks, jobs-report rate-path trade
- **Catalyst:** Regional banks are among the most rate-path-sensitive corners of the market — a soft payrolls print today that pulls hike odds back down would likely lift the group, while a hot number that reinforces the case for a September hike would pressure it.
- **Time horizon:** Hours to a few days around the print and the immediate market reaction.
- **What would invalidate:** A print that lands squarely at consensus with no clear directional read on Fed odds — in that case the trade doesn't have a clean catalyst and should be skipped rather than forced.
- **Risk note:** This is a binary, headline-driven setup tied to one data release — size small and know the exit before the 8:30am ET number, not after.

### NEM — Newmont, gold momentum on dollar weakness
- **Catalyst:** Gold has climbed above $4,400/oz as the dollar index falls to its lowest since May and Treasury yields retreat, a combination that's historically supportive of gold miners' margins and multiples.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks, tied to how the dollar and real yields behave into the September FOMC.
- **What would invalidate:** A hawkish surprise from today's jobs report or a Fed pivot back toward "higher for longer" language that snaps the dollar and yields higher again.
- **Risk note:** Miners carry operational leverage to the gold price in both directions — a reversal in the metal moves the equity by more than the commodity itself.
