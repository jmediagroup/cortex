## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Oil jumps toward $82 WTI / $88 Brent as Hormuz reopening talks stall on Trump's new demands
**Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg, Al Jazeera
**So what:** Trump told Axios the US is "low-keying it" on fresh strikes but is demanding Iran compensate victims of the conflict before backing a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — a corridor that normally carries about a fifth of global oil supply but has seen only 8-15 vessel transits in early August versus roughly 130 before the war. Iran's Foreign Ministry says the US naval blockade must lift first. Analysts flagged a possible "tipping point" in OECD oil inventories around the start of Q4 if the deadlock persists.

### 2. Intel sells $15 billion in stock — its first offering since the 1971 IPO — shares fall 4%
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, Intel Newsroom
**So what:** Intel is raising capital to fund AI and custom-silicon growth after last month's fastest revenue growth in nearly 15 years and a capex guidance hike to $20 billion; underwriters have a 30-day option on an additional $2.25 billion. The stock's -4% reaction to dilution came despite shares still up 175% year-to-date — a reminder that even AI-cycle winners aren't immune to supply/demand mechanics in their own shares.

### 3. CoreWeave and Super Micro report tonight — the first real AI-infrastructure earnings test since the capex-ROI scare
**Source:** TipRanks, Yahoo Finance, FX Leaders
**So what:** CoreWeave (Street estimate: $2.55B revenue, -$1.23 EPS) carries a $99.4 billion backlog and a $31-35 billion capex plan, but its adjusted operating margin collapsed to 1% from 17% a year ago — the options market is pricing roughly a 15.5% post-earnings swing. Super Micro (guided $11.0-12.5B revenue, ~91% YoY growth) has fallen for three straight months amid a DOJ investigation and unresolved internal-control weaknesses. Both are direct reads on whether AI capex demand is still outrunning the concerns.

### 4. Zoetis and ResMed slide premarket on guidance cuts and analyst caution
**Source:** Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Zoetis fell nearly 6% after cutting full-year 2026 revenue and EPS guidance, and ResMed dropped about 5% on lowered analyst price targets — a reminder that even with the index barely moving, single-name dispersion remains wide heading into a week stacked with earnings and inflation data.

### 5. Target hits a 52-week high as consumer spending holds up even as tech wobbles
**Source:** Reporting on Monday's session (Yahoo Finance, CNBC)
**So what:** Target shares climbed to a 52-week high of $152.02 after Q1 results showed a 6.7% increase in net sales, outpacing Amazon, Walmart, and Costco on the day — a data point for the "consumer is fine" side of the ledger even as mega-cap tech (Nvidia, Apple) dragged Monday's tape lower.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### XOM — Exxon Mobil
- **Thesis:** As the Hormuz standoff keeps a geopolitical risk premium in oil, Exxon's integrated upstream/downstream model and disciplined capital allocation let it monetize higher crude prices without the operational risk of pure exploration plays — a business already compounding free cash flow and returning it via dividends and buybacks.
- **Valuation note:** Trailing P/E around 26.4x but forward P/E near 12.5x, reflecting expected earnings growth; dividend yield about 2.7%. Average analyst rating is "Buy" with a 12-month price target roughly 10% above the current price.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Timely but not urgent — shares are already up nearly 40% over the trailing year. The Hormuz premium is a tailwind, not the core thesis; this is a name to accumulate on any pullback tied to a Hormuz de-escalation headline, not chase into today's spike.
- **Risks / bear case:** A negotiated Hormuz reopening (even a partial one) could unwind a meaningful chunk of the current oil-price risk premium quickly, and Exxon's earnings are still ultimately a commodity-price call regardless of balance-sheet quality.
