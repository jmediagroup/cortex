## Top of mind

July PPI came in flat (0.0% m/m) Thursday, well below the +0.2% consensus, and CME FedWatch odds of a September rate hike collapsed from roughly 55% a week earlier to somewhere in the low-to-mid 30s%. The S&P 500 rallied to a fresh record close of 7,798.99 (+0.65%) on the news. But the headline number hides a wrinkle worth watching: the PPI decline was almost entirely energy (gasoline -5.7% m/m), while core services rose 0.2% and construction costs jumped 2.2% — the kind of detail that could complicate the "Fed stays on hold" narrative once the August 26 PCE report lands. Underneath the record, the tape was picking distinct winners and losers: Applied Materials fell after hours despite an EPS beat, and Workday spiked as much as 25% on take-private talk with Silver Lake.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Flat July PPI crushes September rate-hike odds — but the core details are messier
**Source:** CNBC, Reuters-sourced coverage, TechTimes
**So what:** Headline final-demand PPI was flat m/m (annual rate down to 4.7% from 5.5%), missing the +0.2% consensus, driven almost entirely by a 3.1% drop in energy prices (gasoline -5.7% m/m). Service prices still rose 0.2% and construction costs jumped 2.2%. CME FedWatch showed September hike odds falling from around 55% a week earlier into the low-to-mid 30s%. Directionally consistent across sources even where the exact percentage varies — but the energy-driven composition is a reason not to over-read the headline as clean disinflation.

### 2. Workday spikes as much as 25% on report Silver Lake is in talks to take it private
**Source:** Reuters (via wire), Bloomberg, CNBC, Benzinga
**So what:** Shares were halted for volatility after surging as much as 25% intraday, closing up roughly 18% at $206.45 — adding about $8.6B in market cap and erasing the stock's year-to-date losses. Reuters reported private-equity firm Silver Lake is in talks to acquire the HR-software company, which carried a roughly $43B market value before the report broke; no price has been disclosed, and analysts have modeled a 25% takeover premium (implying ~$219/share) as one reference point. This would be one of the largest tests yet of PE appetite for enterprise software in the AI era.

### 3. Applied Materials beats on EPS, misses on revenue — stock falls despite the beat
**Source:** Quiver Quantitative, Blockonomi, TradingKey, Vantage Markets
**So what:** AMAT posted fiscal Q3 EPS of $3.50 (vs. $3.45 consensus) but revenue of $9.115B missed the ~$9.176B estimate by roughly $60M. Shares fell about 3.9% after hours despite a fifth consecutive quarterly EPS beat — echoing this week's Cisco pattern where a headline beat wasn't enough to move a stock that's already priced in AI-capex strength. AMAT closed near $551 in Thursday's regular session (trailing P/E ~51.5, forward P/E ~33.1) and remains well off its 2026 high.

### 4. Oil extends its slide as the Hormuz standoff drags on
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNN, Al Jazeera
**So what:** Brent and WTI each fell more than 2% Thursday even as a drone strike hit an outbound tanker near the Strait of Hormuz Friday and the IEA warned global oil stockpiles are being drawn down rapidly. The Strait has been effectively closed by Tehran's self-declared "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" since February; periodic attacks and no resolution mean the current pullback looks like a demand-side read, not a sign the supply risk has eased.

### 5. July retail sales top consensus, a resilient-consumer signal ahead of Fed decisions
**Source:** US Census Bureau, National Retail Federation
**So what:** Advance July retail sales rose roughly 0.3% m/m, in line with the +0.3% consensus and marking a tenth consecutive monthly gain — a data point the Fed will weigh alongside this week's cooler CPI and PPI prints as it heads toward September. Preliminary University of Michigan consumer sentiment for August follows at 10am ET (consensus 54.1, down from 55.2), the next read on whether that spending resilience is showing up in how consumers feel about the economy.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### AMAT — Applied Materials
- **Thesis:** A core wafer-fab-equipment supplier to the AI-capex buildout, with a fifth straight quarterly EPS beat even as the market punishes the stock for a modest revenue miss — a name whose fundamentals keep outrunning its stock reaction this earnings season.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E around 33x versus a trailing P/E near 51.5x — the gap implies the market already expects meaningful earnings growth, so this isn't a deep-value entry, but it's also not stretched relative to other AI-infrastructure names.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The stock remains well below its 2026 high on China export-restriction overhang; a post-earnings dip that stabilizes over the next few sessions is a more attractive entry than chasing the name into a print, but a continued slide argues for patience.
- **Risks / bear case:** If wafer-fab-equipment orders roll over as AI-capex growth normalizes, or China restrictions widen further, there's no near-term beat-and-raise catalyst left to lean on, and the stock could keep re-rating lower alongside peers like Cisco this week.
