## Top of mind

Thursday erased Wednesday's Fed-driven selloff in one session: the S&P 500 rose 1.7%, the Nasdaq jumped 2.8%, and the Dow added over 600 points, powered almost entirely by Microsoft's record-breaking earnings reaction — a 16% surge that added roughly $450 billion in market value, the largest single-day gain by any stock in market history. Amazon's AWS growth reaccelerated to its fastest pace in 18 quarters, sending shares up over 9% after hours, while Apple slid after weak China sales and soft guidance despite beating on headline numbers. Overnight, that split carried into Asia: South Korea's KOSPI surged nearly 18% — its biggest single-day gain ever — as SK Hynix and Samsung roared back from this week's chip-anxiety selloff, and futures pointed to a higher US open.

**Read-through:** This was a full risk-on reversal — every major index rallied together, volatility collapsed, and the move was driven by concrete earnings evidence (Microsoft's Azure growth, Amazon's AWS reacceleration) rather than sentiment alone. But the reversal wasn't universal: Apple's post-earnings slide on China and guidance is a reminder that this market is still pricing AI-capex names on a case-by-case basis, not rewarding the group indiscriminately. Asia's overnight follow-through (KOSPI's record surge, Nikkei +4%) suggests the relief has legs into Friday's open, but a rally this concentrated in a handful of megacap earnings reactions is also a rally that can reverse just as quickly if the next data point disappoints.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Microsoft posts the largest single-day market-cap gain in history
**Source:** Bloomberg, U.S. News
**So what:** Shares jumped about 16% after Microsoft reported 43% Azure growth — its fastest pace since early 2022 — adding roughly $450 billion in value and pushing its market cap to about $3.35 trillion. This eclipses Nvidia's prior record one-day gain and is the clearest single data point this earnings season that AI infrastructure spending is translating into accelerating cloud revenue, not just cost.

### 2. Amazon's AWS growth accelerates to an 18-quarter high; capex guidance jumps to $220B
**Source:** CNBC, company earnings release
**So what:** Q2 revenue hit $200.6 billion (vs. ~$196.5B expected) with AWS revenue up 36.7% to $42.2 billion, beating estimates near $40.5 billion. Shares gained nearly 4% in regular trading before adding another 9%+ after hours. Management raised 2026 capex guidance to roughly $220 billion (from $200 billion), but investors accepted the higher spend because the AWS acceleration validated it — echoing Microsoft's reaction the same evening.

### 3. Apple slides on weak China sales and soft Q4 guidance despite beating estimates
**Source:** Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Apple posted Q3 revenue of $109.42 billion (+16.4% YoY) and beat on EPS, but shares fell roughly 6-7% after hours on Greater China revenue of $18.8 billion (vs. $19.6B expected) and Q4 guidance of 9-11% growth — short of the ~$114.9 billion consensus. Component bottlenecks and rising memory-chip costs were cited as headwinds heading into the fall lineup. This is the same earnings week that made Microsoft and Amazon look unstoppable; Apple's reaction shows the market is still discriminating, not just chasing anything AI-adjacent.

### 4. Asian chip stocks stage a historic overnight rebound
**Source:** Nikkei Asia, TradingKey, Reuters-sourced coverage
**So what:** South Korea's KOSPI surged nearly 18% overnight — its largest single-day gain on record — with SK Hynix up over 25% and Samsung up over 20%, as this week's chip-demand anxiety (triggered by a weak SK Hynix report earlier in the week) reversed sharply on the back of Microsoft's and Amazon's cloud/AI capex commentary. Japan's Nikkei added roughly 4% after the Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1% in an 8-1 vote. The speed of the round trip — from "AI capex anxiety" Wednesday to a record rally Friday morning in Asia — underscores how sentiment-driven this trade remains.

### 5. Friday's data slate: Employment Cost Index, Chicago PMI, and final consumer sentiment
**Source:** Econoday, Kiplinger
**So what:** The Q2 Employment Cost Index (8:30am ET), July Chicago PMI (9:45am ET), and the final University of Michigan consumer sentiment read for July (10am ET) round out a heavy data week that already included Wednesday's Fed decision and Thursday's GDP/core PCE prints. With the bond market still digesting a hawkish Fed hold, a hot Employment Cost Index reading — a direct wage-inflation gauge — could reopen this week's rates story even as equities ride Thursday's earnings-driven relief.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### AMZN — Amazon
- **Thesis:** AWS's reacceleration to 36.7% growth — its fastest in 18 quarters — is the clearest evidence yet that Amazon's cloud and AI infrastructure investment is compounding rather than just diluting margins. Retail and advertising remain durable cash generators underneath the AWS growth story.
- **Valuation note:** Shares are up sharply post-earnings (regular-session +3.9% plus a further 9%+ after hours), so the multiple has already re-rated on the news; this isn't a name to chase at the open.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The raised 2026 capex guidance ($220B, up from $200B) is a real near-term cash-flow headwind — patient accumulation on a pullback from the post-earnings pop looks more sensible than buying the gap.
- **Risks / bear case:** If AWS growth decelerates from this quarter's pace, or if the higher capex doesn't keep translating into accelerating cloud revenue, the market has already shown this week (via Apple and, earlier, Meta) that it will punish spending-without-payoff optics quickly and hard.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AAPL — post-earnings dislocation
- **Catalyst:** A 6-7% after-hours drop despite a revenue and EPS beat is a large repricing driven specifically by China weakness and soft Q4 guidance — not a broad demand miss. Whether the stock stabilizes near the post-earnings low or continues lower as analysts digest the guidance is the tradeable question over the next few sessions.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, watching how the stock behaves once the initial reaction settles and analyst notes come out.
- **What would invalidate:** Further downward guidance revisions or confirmation that the China and services softness is broadening (rather than a one-quarter blip) would argue the drop is a fair repricing, not an overreaction. Continued memory-cost inflation commentary would also weigh on the setup.
- **Risk note:** This is a bet that the market overreacted to guidance on an otherwise-beat quarter — size small, and note the added variable of the previously-flagged Cook-to-Ternus CEO transition landing in the same period.

### MU — semiconductor relief-rally follow-through
- **Catalyst:** Micron was one of the hardest-hit names in Wednesday's chip-anxiety selloff; Thursday's Microsoft/Amazon capex commentary and the overnight KOSPI/SK Hynix/Samsung surge suggest the "AI demand is slowing" narrative that drove this week's chip selloff may be unwinding as fast as it built.
- **Time horizon:** Days, through Friday's US session and into next week — this is a momentum/reversal trade, not a fundamentals re-rate.
- **What would invalidate:** A failure of US semiconductor names to follow through on Asia's overnight strength, or fresh reporting corroborating the demand-slowdown story that triggered Wednesday's drop, would suggest the Asia rally is a local overreaction rather than a durable turn.
- **Risk note:** This whole group has now moved sharply in both directions within the same week — treat any position as a short-dated, high-volatility trade and size accordingly.
