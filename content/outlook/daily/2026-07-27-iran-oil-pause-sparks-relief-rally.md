## Top of mind

Stock futures jumped Monday morning — Dow futures up about 408 points (+0.78%), S&P 500 futures +0.87%, Nasdaq 100 futures +1.46% — after the US and Iran quietly paused their strike campaign over the weekend, sending Brent crude down as much as 7% before it pared about half that drop. The obvious read is relief: cheaper oil eases the inflation scare that had pushed the 10-year toward 4.70% and forced Delta and United to warn of billions in extra fuel costs. The less obvious risk is that this is the same de-escalate-then-re-escalate pattern that's whipsawed oil all year, and it's landing right before the week's real tests — Wednesday's Fed decision and Wednesday/Thursday earnings from Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon, any one of which could overwhelm today's relief bid.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. US and Iran quietly pause their strike campaign; oil craters
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, Euronews
**So what:** After roughly two weeks of nightly US strikes on Iranian military infrastructure, the US paused the campaign late Friday without a formal announcement, and Iran said it halted retaliatory strikes while engaging Oman on Strait of Hormuz de-escalation. Brent fell as much as 7.4% toward $87-90 before recovering about half that move to the low $90s; WTI dropped roughly 5% to the mid-$80s. This is the single biggest driver of Monday's futures rally, and the single biggest risk to it if the pause doesn't hold.

### 2. TSMC's capex hike triggers a chip-sector selloff that's still being digested
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Benzinga, Seeking Alpha
**So what:** TSMC beat Q2 estimates (revenue $40.2B, +33.7% year-over-year; ADR EPS +74%) but raised 2026 capex guidance to $60-64 billion from $52-56 billion, and its stock fell roughly 5% on the news. The read-through hit the whole sector Friday — chip names sank as much as 4.4% and Intel gave back a large piece of Thursday's earnings-driven rally — on worry that heavier, front-loaded overseas capex delays the cash payoff of the AI buildout rather than derailing it.

### 3. Delta and United are absorbing billions in extra fuel costs — right as oil rolls over
**Source:** Benzinga, U.S. News, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Delta has guided to roughly $4 billion in incremental 2026 fuel costs and United to about $6 billion, after jet fuel crack spreads spiked alongside the Iran conflict; both stocks fell 3-5% intraday last week on the margin worry. Monday's oil pullback is a direct — if fragile — offset to that pressure, and airline shares are the cleanest read on how durable traders think the strike pause is.

### 4. The Fed meets Wednesday with the hold/hike debate genuinely unsettled
**Source:** CME FedWatch-derived estimates via multiple outlets, IndexBox, TradingKey
**So what:** The FOMC meets July 28-29 and is widely expected to hold the fed funds rate at 3.50%-3.75%, but estimates of the hold probability range from about 61% to 65% (implying a 33-34% chance of a hike) — unusually contested for a meeting most economists still call a hold. Monday's oil pullback modestly eases the inflation case for a hike, but it came too late to change the data the Fed has already been watching into this meeting.

### 5. Big Tech's capex answer arrives this week, and it will resolve Friday's chip debate one way or the other
**Source:** CNBC, Kiplinger, TipRanks
**So what:** Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon report Wednesday and Thursday this week, with Apple and Qualcomm also on the calendar (sources vary slightly on the exact day for each). Whatever TSMC's capex guide implied about AI spending discipline, these four companies control the actual demand signal — a round of reaffirmed or raised capex without new margin caveats would likely calm the chip complex; cautious framing from more than one would extend Friday's selloff.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor
- **Thesis:** Record Q2 results (revenue +33.7% year-over-year, ADR EPS +74%) and management's own framing — AI demand still outpacing capacity — argue the foundry's leading-edge monopoly position remains intact even as the market fixates on the capex bill. One analyst sees potential for more than 100% upside by 2028 at a reported ~23x forward earnings; TD Cowen raised its price target to $440 from $400 on the print.
- **Valuation note:** The ~5% post-earnings pullback happened despite a beat-and-raise, purely on capex-timing concerns — a reasonable entry discount for investors who buy the multi-year demand thesis, not a valuation red flag on its own.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient framing fits best here: this week's Microsoft/Meta/Amazon capex commentary will either validate TSMC's demand read or add to the margin-timing worry, so a second data point before sizing up seems prudent.
- **Risks / bear case:** Raising 2026 capex to $60-64 billion lifts capital intensity toward roughly 36% of sales (from 33.5% in 2025), and depreciation plus new overseas (Arizona) fab ramp costs could pressure margins through 2027-2028 even if unit demand stays strong. If this week's hyperscaler earnings show any capex hesitation, the whole complex — TSMC included — likely re-rates lower first and asks questions later.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### DAL / UAL — airlines on the oil pullback
- **Catalyst:** Monday's roughly 5-7% intraday drop in Brent/WTI directly eases the fuel-cost pressure that pushed Delta and United down 3-5% last week, after each guided to multi-billion-dollar 2026 fuel-cost increases tied to the Iran conflict.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a couple of weeks, tracking whether the US-Iran strike pause holds.
- **What would invalidate:** Any resumption of strikes or a breakdown in the Oman-mediated Hormuz talks would send oil straight back toward its recent highs and erase the relief — this conflict has already reversed on diplomatic headlines multiple times this year.
- **Risk note:** This is a headline trade on a fragile, undeclared pause, not a fundamentals call. Airlines were already managing the cost shock through fares and capacity cuts before oil rolled over — don't assume a full unwind of the fuel-cost overhang.

### Semiconductors — binary setup into hyperscaler earnings
- **Catalyst:** Friday's sector-wide selloff (chip names down as much as 4.4%, Intel giving back Thursday's earnings gains) tied to TSMC's capex guide sets up a real test: Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon report Wednesday/Thursday and will each disclose their own AI capex trajectory.
- **Time horizon:** Through Thursday's close this week.
- **What would invalidate:** Cautious capex framing or ROI caveats from more than one hyperscaler would likely extend the chip-sector selloff; reaffirmed or raised spending without new caveats from most of them would likely stabilize the group.
- **Risk note:** This is an earnings-headline-driven, binary setup — the group has already moved double digits intraday multiple times this month. Size for volatility, not conviction.
