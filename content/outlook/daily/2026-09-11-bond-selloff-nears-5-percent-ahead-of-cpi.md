## Top of mind

The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed roughly 18 basis points this week to sit just under 5% — a level it hasn't held in nearly two decades — and this morning's August CPI report (8:30am ET) is the last major inflation data point the Fed sees before the September 16 FOMC decision. Markets are pricing something like a 70%+ probability of a 25bp hike, up sharply from a summer spent debating cuts, and that repricing is happening against a backdrop of $100+ oil, a four-session losing streak in equities, and a bond market that's arguably more stressed than stocks are admitting. Today's print is a genuine two-way risk for both asset classes.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Global bond selloff pushes the 10-year to the cusp of 5%
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** This is arguably the more important market story than today's CPI itself — a sustained move through 5% on the 10-year would be the highest sustained level since 2007 and raises the cost of capital across the board, from mortgages to corporate refinancing to the multiples the market is willing to pay for long-duration growth stocks. The move has been building since the Iran war first pressured oil and inflation expectations; today's data either validates it or gives dip buyers a reason to step in.

### 2. August CPI lands this morning as the Fed's last read before September 16
**Source:** Reuters, CNBC
**So what:** Consensus looks for headline CPI at 3.4% year-over-year (+0.4% month-over-month, largely gasoline-driven) and core at 2.4% y/y (+0.2% m/m). Economists flag that core coming in at 0.3% or hotter would likely cement the hike case, while a 0.2% or cooler print could reopen debate about a hold — this is a real fork, not a formality, for a Fed funds futures market currently pricing better than 70% odds of a 25bp move.

### 3. Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure keep the oil risk premium bid
**Source:** Times of Israel, NPR
**So what:** Houthi missile and drone attacks this week on Saudi Aramco facilities in Abha, Najran and Jazan — home to a 400,000-bpd refinery — wounded more than 70 people and forced a halt to some operations. Combined with reports of Saudi crude output falling roughly 1.9 million barrels/day in August, this is a second, independent supply-side reason (beyond the Iran war) for crude to stay elevated even if the geopolitical headlines rotate day to day.

### 4. JPMorgan upgrades Meta to Overweight on its AI agent push
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC
**So what:** Analyst Doug Anmuth raised Meta to Overweight from Neutral and lifted his price target to $820 from $640 (~25% implied upside at the time), citing the early rollout of Meta's "Muse" AI agent and Meta Model API access as a growth avenue beyond advertising. It's a notable sell-side stance change on one of the market's largest AI-capex spenders right as the broader tape is being driven by rates, not fundamentals — worth watching whether the stock can actually decouple from the macro pressure this call implies.

### 5. Kroger reports before today's open — a read on the consumer under inflation pressure
**Source:** Company guidance, analyst previews
**So what:** Consensus looks for $34.68B revenue and $1.05 EPS (fiscal Q2), a modest ~1% EPS increase year-over-year, with options pricing a 5.5–6.34% implied move. New CEO Greg Foran's turnaround progress and the company's ability to hold market share and margins while grocery inflation runs hot make this a useful, if lower-profile, read on consumer resilience the same morning as the CPI print.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### JPM — JPMorgan Chase
- **Thesis:** The scaled, diversified bank best positioned to convert a higher-for-longer rate regime into durable net interest income growth, without the single-name credit or duration risk smaller banks carry. A hawkish Fed repricing (which is exactly what's happening into next week's FOMC) is a structural tailwind for large-bank net interest margins.
- **Valuation note:** Trading around $358 with a trailing P/E near 15.3x and a dividend yield near 1.7% — above JPM's own 3-year average P/E of roughly 12.7x, so the market has already priced in some of the rate tailwind. Not a screaming discount, but not stretched relative to the earnings power a hike cycle implies either.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Reasonably timely — a confirmed hike (or even sustained hike odds) firms up the NIM thesis concretely, but the stock's already-elevated multiple versus its own history argues for building a position over time rather than sizing up fully into today's CPI print.
- **Risks / bear case:** If a cool CPI print reopens the door to a hold (or even cuts later), the NIM tailwind narrative weakens quickly; JPM's premium multiple versus history also leaves less cushion if credit costs normalize higher or loan growth disappoints.

## Portfolio-level guidance

*Allocation and risk observations. Not specific buy/sell calls — those depend on a full picture this report doesn't see.*

- **Concentration check:** A four-day losing streak in equities alongside a bond market making a near-two-decade high in yields is a reminder that stocks and bonds can both be under pressure at once — a portfolio's "safe" fixed-income sleeve isn't automatically offsetting equity drawdowns in this kind of regime.
- **Rates positioning:** With the 10-year knocking on 5% and today's CPI a live catalyst in either direction, this is not the week to be extending duration to "lock in yield" — the level itself is attractive on a multi-year view, but the near-term path is unusually uncertain into a Fed decision.
- **Cash & dry powder:** Between a live Middle East oil-supply shock, a coin-flip-to-favored-hike Fed decision, and a CPI print that can swing either way this morning, keeping some dry powder rather than being fully invested into today's data remains a defensible, non-market-timing stance.
- **Risk regime read:** VIX in the mid-teens against a 10-year near a 19-year high is the same disconnect flagged in recent reports — implied volatility still isn't pricing the stress that bond yields and $100+ oil are already signaling. That gap has been persistent, but it isn't free; a surprise CPI print is exactly the kind of event that closes it abruptly.
