## Top of mind

The Dow and S&P 500 closed at record highs Monday as Big Tech powered a broad rally — Amazon's market cap topped $3 trillion, Meta jumped 6%, and the Nasdaq rose 2.1% — while oil confirmed its steepest one-day drop in weeks after President Trump called off planned Iran strikes, with Brent and WTI both down roughly 5-6% on the session. That rally now has to survive Tuesday's earnings gauntlet: Caterpillar and BP report before the open, while AMD and SpaceX — the latter's first-ever print as a public company — land after the close, with options markets pricing a swing of over $200 billion in SpaceX's market cap alone. Yields eased (10-year to 4.70%) and the dollar fell to a roughly two-month low on coordinated US-Japan intervention, giving the tape a rare everything-rallies setup that today's earnings will stress-test.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Trump calls off Iran strikes; oil confirms a 5-6% drop
**Source:** Forbes Advisor, Trading Economics, Al Jazeera
**So what:** Brent and WTI both closed Monday down roughly 5-7%, confirming the intraday plunge that began after Trump said he canceled planned strikes on Iran and that talks would resume. OPEC+'s previously announced 188,000 barrel-per-day September production increase compounds the pressure. Nothing here is confirmed diplomacy yet — treat the de-escalation as directional, not settled.

### 2. Amazon crosses $3 trillion as megacap tech drives record closes
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Amazon rose 4.6% Monday, pushing its market cap above $3 trillion for the first time, while Microsoft (+4.9%), Meta (+6%), Alphabet (+4.9%), Nvidia (+2.9%) and Tesla (+3.5%) all rallied together. A move this broad and this synchronized across megacaps is more a sector re-rating than stock-specific news — worth remembering before treating any single name's gain as an idiosyncratic thesis confirmation.

### 3. Dollar falls to a two-month low on coordinated yen intervention
**Source:** Trading Economics, Bloomberg
**So what:** The DXY extended its slide to a fifth straight session after Japan confirmed coordinated yen-buying operations with the US, pushing the dollar to its weakest level since mid-June. A softer dollar is a tailwind for multinational earnings translation and commodities, but it also signals policymakers are actively intervening rather than letting markets clear on their own.

### 4. Alibaba jumps on Qwen 3.8-Max launch, reviving the China AI story
**Source:** Seeking Alpha
**So what:** Alibaba shares jumped Monday after the company unveiled its Qwen 3.8-Max model, pitched as delivering top-tier performance against rivals. It's a reminder that the AI-infrastructure trade isn't a purely US phenomenon, and that Chinese tech names carry both the upside of a genuine model breakthrough and the usual regulatory overhang.

### 5. Tuesday's earnings wave: AMD and SpaceX headline after the close
**Source:** FX Leaders, Benzinga, Investing.com
**So what:** Caterpillar and BP report before Tuesday's open, with HSBC, McDonald's and Spotify also on the slate. AMD (consensus ~$1.61 EPS, ~$11.3B revenue, +47% y/y) and SpaceX — its first-ever earnings report as a public company, with options implying a swing of roughly $204 billion in market cap — report after the close. This is the day's real catalyst; Monday's rally set a high bar for these prints to clear.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### AMZN — Amazon
- **Thesis:** AWS growth acceleration, expanding retail margins, and a widening AI-infrastructure buildout underpin a multi-year growth case that's more durable than any single session's move — Monday's rally just happened to be the day the market cap cleared $3 trillion.
- **Valuation note:** A stock up 4.6% in a single session on top of an already-elevated multiple is pricing in a lot of AWS/AI good news already; worth comparing forward P/E and AWS-growth-adjusted multiple against cloud peers (MSFT, GOOGL) before sizing anything new.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. This is a name to watch for a pullback entry, not to chase into a record close that came alongside five other megacaps rallying in lockstep.
- **Risks / bear case:** A rich valuation leaves little room for a soft AWS growth print or a broader AI-capex digestion trade, and much of Monday's move reflects sector-wide rotation rather than Amazon-specific news — a reversal in megacap sentiment would hit AMZN along with the group.

### BP — BP plc
- **Thesis:** A global integrated major with upstream, downstream, and trading operations that can partially offset a lower crude tape; energy majors have traded at a persistent discount to the broader market for years while paying above-market dividend yields.
- **Valuation note:** Monday's 5-7% oil drop hit energy-sector sentiment broadly; if BP shares sell off further into and after Tuesday's print, the discount to free-cash-flow generation at current oil prices would widen further and is worth tracking against history.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient — BP reports Q2 results Tuesday before the open. The print, not the oil headline, will show whether trading and refining strength offset lower crude realizations. Wait for the number before sizing.
- **Risks / bear case:** If OPEC+'s September supply increase and a genuine Iran de-escalation keep oil range-bound in the mid-$70s to mid-$80s for an extended period, upstream cash flow compresses further and tests the dividend/buyback framework majors have leaned on.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMD — data-center print into Tuesday's close
- **Catalyst:** Reports after Tuesday's close; consensus ~$1.61 EPS on ~$11.3B revenue (+47% y/y), driven by data-center AI chip demand.
- **Time horizon:** Through Wednesday's session reaction.
- **What would invalidate:** A miss or soft guide on data-center/AI-chip demand would puncture the "AI capex keeps accelerating" narrative that's been driving the whole semiconductor complex, and likely pressure peers along with AMD.
- **Risk note:** Implied volatility into the print is elevated after Monday's broad tech rally already priced in optimism — a "good but not good enough" number could still sell off on positioning alone.

### SPCX — SpaceX's first public earnings report
- **Catalyst:** SpaceX reports its first-ever earnings as a public company after Tuesday's close; consensus is roughly $6.8B in revenue and a $0.29 per-share loss. Options markets are pricing a swing of roughly $204 billion in market cap on the print.
- **Time horizon:** Days — through the initial reaction and the following session.
- **What would invalidate:** A miss on Starlink subscriber growth or launch cadence, or commentary suggesting Starship and other capex is running well ahead of revenue, would challenge the growth premium already priced into the stock.
- **Risk note:** This is the company's first print as a public entity with no earnings-reaction history to anchor expectations — size for outsized volatility in either direction.

### BABA — Qwen 3.8-Max momentum
- **Catalyst:** Alibaba jumped Monday on the unveiling of its Qwen 3.8-Max AI model, described as delivering top-tier performance versus rivals.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks — through any follow-on commentary on adoption or monetization.
- **What would invalidate:** If the launch doesn't translate into cloud/enterprise revenue traction within a quarter or two, or gets overshadowed by renewed China regulatory headlines, the momentum trade unwinds quickly.
- **Risk note:** China ADRs carry headline and regulatory risk on top of normal equity risk — size accordingly and don't treat a single product launch as a full re-rating.
