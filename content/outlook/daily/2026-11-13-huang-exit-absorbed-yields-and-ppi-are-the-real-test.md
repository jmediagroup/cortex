## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO
**Source:** Nvidia newsroom, NBC News, CNN, CNBC
**So what:** The board said Huang stepped down as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, and named Harvey C. Jones its first chair. Reports tie the resignation to his personal relationships, and three House committees have asked him for a closed-door interview on December 1. I am not repeating the allegations. For investors, the leadership question is now settled for the short term, and attention shifts to whether the business can keep its momentum without its founder.

### 2. Nvidia's rivals take the money: AMD and Broadcom gain nearly 6%
**Source:** Motley Fool, Yahoo Finance, Benzinga
**So what:** AMD and Broadcom led S&P 500 gainers as Nvidia fell 4.34% to $166.19. Investors stayed in AI hardware and moved money away from the single name with key-person risk. That is a sign the AI-spending thesis is intact, even as Nvidia's premium is questioned. Melius Research reportedly downgraded Nvidia to Hold and cut its target to $185 from $315.

### 3. Applied Materials beats and guides above consensus, and the stock still slips
**Source:** Applied Materials IR / SEC 8-K, Benzinga, ad-hoc-news
**So what:** Record fiscal Q4 revenue of $10.41 billion (up 53%) and adjusted EPS of $4.16 beat the roughly $4.05 consensus, and fiscal Q1 revenue guidance of $11.1 billion (plus or minus $500 million) is above the roughly $10.9 billion estimate. One early European quote had shares down about 2.5% before the U.S. open. A beat-and-raise that still slips is the same pattern AMAT showed in August, and it suggests the good news was already priced.

### 4. Cisco posts its fastest growth in more than 15 years and doubles its AI target
**Source:** Cisco newsroom, CNBC, Seeking Alpha, Investing.com
**So what:** Revenue rose 24% to $18.5 billion, adjusted EPS of $1.40 beat the roughly $1.33 estimate, and full-year revenue guidance rose to $74.8–$75.8 billion. Cisco now expects about $9 billion in AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers this fiscal year, more than double fiscal 2026. After-hours reaction reports ranged from flat to about +4%. Morgan Stanley had downgraded the stock to Equal-weight before the report, citing pull-forward risk.

### 5. Long yields push toward 5.1% as bonds reprice CPI
**Source:** Forbes Advisor, Seoul Economic Daily, FRED
**So what:** Bonds were closed for Veterans Day on Wednesday, so Thursday was the first chance for the long end to react. FRED shows the 10s-2s spread narrowing to about 24 bps. CME FedWatch reportedly prices a December hike fully, with about 13% odds of 50 bps. A 10-year above 5% is the main reason small caps and long-duration growth stocks lagged.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### NVDA — Earnings under an interim CEO
- **Catalyst:** Fiscal Q3 results are reportedly due Tuesday, November 17 after the close, per Nvidia's own prior call. Confirm on Nvidia's IR page. This will be the first report under interim CEO Colette Kress, who was the CFO.
- **Time horizon:** Days, through the November 17 report and the November 18 session.
- **What would invalidate:** Any cut to guidance, customer commentary suggesting orders are shifting to AMD or Broadcom, or new disclosures that widen the investigation beyond Huang personally. Any of these would turn a governance discount into a fundamental one.
- **Risk note:** The stock moved 14% intraday on Thursday. Earnings, congressional testimony on December 1, and a CEO search all add headline risk. Sizing should be small, or wait for the earnings reaction before acting.

### AMAT — Beat-and-raise, sold anyway
- **Catalyst:** Thursday's after-hours results; the regular-session reaction today confirms whether the early European weakness holds.
- **Time horizon:** Days to weeks.
- **What would invalidate:** For a watch-and-buy-on-weakness setup, a follow-through decline on heavy volume through today's session means the market is pricing a cyclical peak in equipment spending. Step aside rather than average down.
