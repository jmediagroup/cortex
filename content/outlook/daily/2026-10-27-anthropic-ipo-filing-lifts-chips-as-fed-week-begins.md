## Top of mind

The S&P 500 rose 0.70% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.82% on Monday, October 26, as Anthropic's IPO filing revived the AI trade and chip stocks bounced about 2.8%. The same day, Meta said it plans to raise up to $95 billion in equity for AI infrastructure and its shares fell about 1.9%. For a long-term plus opportunistic investor, the takeaway is that the AI-capex funding question is still open, and the Fed decision on Wednesday is the next test.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Anthropic files for an IPO
**Source:** CNBC, TheStreet, BigGo Finance, KuCoin recaps
**So what:** The prospectus reportedly points to a Nasdaq listing under the ticker "ANT," about $16.3 billion in first-half revenue and a net loss of roughly $80.7 billion that the filing attributes mainly to non-cash fair-value changes. Price and size were not disclosed. A large public AI lab gives investors a direct way to price AI economics, and the filing lifted the chip and AI supply chain on Monday. Note that this is a filing, not a listing, and the numbers come from secondary coverage.

### 2. Meta plans an equity raise of up to $95 billion
**Source:** Techflow, TradingKey, other recaps
**So what:** The proceeds are earmarked for data centers and AI infrastructure. Shares slipped about 1.9% on dilution and return-on-spend concerns, following a roughly 7% drop on Thursday. One outlet printed "$950 billion" in a headline, but its own body text and other outlets give $95 billion, which is the figure used here.

### 3. Fed meets Wednesday with a hold priced in
**Source:** Fed funds futures as cited in market recaps
**So what:** Futures reportedly put about a 90% probability on the Fed holding at its current 3.75%-4% range. With the 10-year near 5% and the 2-year at 4.71%, the market is more likely to react to the press conference tone than to the decision itself.

### 4. Oil pulled back
**Source:** TheStreet, HDFC Sky recaps
**So what:** Recaps cite falling oil as a supportive factor after last week's energy-driven jump. I could not confirm the size of the move, so treat it as direction only.
