## Top of mind

Alphabet posted 82% cloud revenue growth and a $514 billion backlog Wednesday night, but investors fixated on the bigger number: a hiked 2026 capex guide to $195-205 billion (from $180-190 billion), with more increases flagged for 2027, sending the stock down nearly 5% after hours. Tesla delivered a second gut-check — a revenue beat overshadowed by a margin miss, with operating margin sliding to 1.4% from 4.1% a year ago — and fell about 4%. Both stories are unfolding as oil extends its climb on fresh Iran-conflict escalation, with Intel's earnings tonight the next data point on whether Wall Street's patience with AI-fueled spending is fraying.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Alphabet's capex hike overshadows a blowout cloud quarter
**Source:** CNBC; Seeking Alpha
**So what:** Revenue rose 24% to $119.8 billion and Google Cloud revenue jumped 82% to $24.8 billion with operating margin more than tripling to 35.6%, but free cash flow turned deeply negative (-$5.9 billion) on record $44.9 billion quarterly capex. Management raised full-year 2026 capex guidance to $195-205 billion and warned 2027 spending will rise "significantly" again — the kind of open-ended spending commitment that makes even a genuinely strong quarter read as a risk to some investors.

### 2. Tesla's record revenue can't offset a margin collapse
**Source:** Electrek; StockTitan
**So what:** Revenue hit a record $28.24 billion (+26% year-over-year, ahead of estimates), but operating margin fell to 1.4% from 4.1% as opex jumped 47% on AI, Optimus and robotaxi spending, and adjusted EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.53 consensus by a wide margin. Free cash flow margin turned negative. Cybercab production has started in Texas and robotaxi service expanded to seven metros, but the company gave no firm robotaxi deployment timeline — the same ambiguity that's dogged prior quarters.

### 3. Oil extends its climb as the Iran conflict widens
**Source:** CNBC
**So what:** WTI rose to roughly $87.72 and Brent to $95.61 — a six-week high — after a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile off Saudi Arabia and Houthi forces hit two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea with missiles and drones, the first direct strikes on that waterway in this conflict. President Trump additionally threatened further strikes on Iranian infrastructure. This is now a second live catalyst alongside earnings season, and one that feeds directly into the inflation and rate-path debate.

### 4. Intel's 18A ramp faces its highest-stakes earnings call in years
**Source:** Yahoo Finance; Blockonomi; Alphastreet
**So what:** Consensus expects $0.21 EPS (versus a $0.26 loss a year ago) on $14.42 billion revenue, with 18A process yields reportedly around 85%, up sharply from 65% last quarter. Analyst opinion is split — Citi keeps a Buy with a $130 target while Rosenblatt maintains Sell — making tonight's print, after Alphabet's and Tesla's mixed reactions, a real test of whether the market still rewards AI-adjacent capex optimism or has turned more skeptical this week.

### 5. Asian chipmakers rally on AI-spending optimism even as US tech wobbles
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** The Kospi rose about 2.8% and Samsung and SK Hynix both gained more than 3% as investors bet regional chipmakers benefit from AI infrastructure spending, and the broader MSCI Asia Pacific gauge climbed roughly 1%. The divergence from Wednesday's US tech weakness is notable — Asia is reading the same capex story as durable demand for memory and foundry capacity rather than a spending risk.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### GOOGL — Alphabet
- **Thesis:** Google Cloud grew 82% to $24.8 billion with a $514 billion backlog and operating margin that more than tripled year-over-year — evidence the AI infrastructure buildout is converting into durable, high-margin revenue rather than just capex. Search revenue also grew a healthy 17% to $63.3 billion, showing the core business isn't being cannibalized as feared.
- **Valuation note:** The nearly 5% after-hours drop is a re-pricing of near-term free cash flow (which turned negative on record capex), not of the underlying business quality — worth watching whether the stock actually opens down that much or partially recovers, since knee-jerk capex reactions have reversed before in this earnings cycle (see Micron's post-earnings path in June).
- **Why now (or why patient):** A selloff driven by "too much spending on a business growing 82%" is a different kind of risk than a demand problem — patient investors may see this as a better entry than chasing the stock at highs, but only if cloud growth and margin expansion continue to justify the spend.
- **Risks / bear case:** $195-205 billion in 2026 capex, rising again in 2027, is an enormous and rising bet on AI demand holding up; if cloud growth decelerates or hyperscaler capacity turns out to be overbuilt, Alphabet's free cash flow could stay depressed for years rather than quarters. The stock is also exposed to any broader AI-capex sentiment shift given how correlated mega-cap tech has become.

### INTC — tonight's earnings as the next AI-capex referendum
- **Catalyst:** Intel reports Q2 2026 results tonight, July 23, after the close. Consensus is $0.21 EPS on $14.42 billion revenue, with 18A yield progress (reportedly ~85%, up from 65%) the key metric after Alphabet's and Tesla's mixed after-hours reactions this week.

- **Risk note:** Intel has a wide analyst split (Buy at Citi, Sell at Rosenblatt) and the stock has already rallied hard into this print — binary setup, size for a gap either direction.

### XLE — energy sector momentum on an escalating Iran conflict
- **Catalyst:** Fresh Houthi tanker strikes in the Red Sea and Trump's threat of further strikes on Iranian infrastructure pushed Brent to a six-week high near $96; energy was Wednesday's lone leading sector.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, tracking headline flow out of the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea shipping lanes.
- **What would invalidate:** Any ceasefire progress or de-escalation signal (as happened briefly earlier this month) would likely reverse the oil bid quickly; energy names have round-tripped fast on ceasefire headlines before in this same conflict.
- **Risk note:** This is a geopolitical-headline trade, not a fundamentals-driven one — it can reverse violently and without warning on a single diplomatic development. Size small and define an exit level before entering.
