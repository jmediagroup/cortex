## Top of mind

Walmart fell roughly 9% Thursday to its lowest level in months — not because it missed, but because it beat on EPS and raised full-year guidance while US comparable sales grew just 2.6%, the slowest pace in about six years, as management pointed to shoppers making "trade-offs" amid high gas prices. The stock move mattered more than the headline: it dragged consumer names lower and helped erase most of Wednesday's Treasury-buyback relief rally, with the 10-year back to 4.70% and the 30-year to 5.25%, both roughly back where they started the week. The obvious risk is treating Walmart's slowdown as consumer-wide weakness when Ross Stores and Deere both beat and rallied the same day; the less obvious one is that a bond market this quick to erase a policy-driven rally is signaling the underlying yield pressure — fiscal issuance, not market plumbing — hasn't actually gone away.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Walmart falls ~9% despite an EPS beat and raised guidance, as US comp sales slow to a six-year low
**Source:** CNBC, Investing.com, StockStory
**So what:** Walmart reported adjusted EPS of $0.81 versus $0.74 expected and revenue up 5.9% to roughly $187.9 billion, and raised full-year constant-currency sales guidance to 4%-5% from 3.5%-4.5%. But US comparable sales grew just 2.6%, down from 4.1% last quarter and below the roughly 3.8% consensus, with management citing gas-price-driven trade-offs. The market treated the deceleration as the more important signal than the beat — a reminder that "beat and raise" doesn't guarantee a positive reaction when the underlying growth trend is the story investors are watching.

### 2. Treasury yields snap back higher, erasing most of Wednesday's buyback-driven rally
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** The 30-year yield climbed six basis points to 5.25% and the 10-year erased its Wednesday losses to close at 4.70%, both recouping most of the decline sparked by Treasury's surprise announcement to more than double its long-bond buyback operations. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC the buyback program could ultimately be larger than the $4 billion per operation announced. That the rally reversed in a single session suggests markets view the buybacks as addressing bond-market liquidity, not the underlying fiscal issuance pressure that pushed yields to multi-year highs in the first place.

### 3. Ross Stores and Deere both beat and rally, a contrast to Walmart's reaction
**Source:** PR Newswire (Ross Stores release), StockTitan, Investing.com
**So what:** Ross Stores posted second-quarter EPS of $2.66 (including a roughly $0.60 tariff-refund benefit) against guidance of $1.85-$1.93, with comparable sales up 10% on traffic and operating margin up 205 basis points even excluding the tariff benefit. Deere beat with EPS of $5.10 versus $4.69 expected and revenue up 5% to $12.61 billion versus $10.81 billion consensus, sending shares up more than 3% premarket. Both prints argue against reading Walmart's stock reaction as evidence of broad consumer or industrial weakness.

### 4. Oil extends its climb as Trump escalates economic pressure on Iran
**Source:** CNN Business, Trading Economics
**So what:** WTI crude rose about 2.1% to roughly $86/bbl as the US pushes a sanctions-heavy strategy — Trump has threatened "tremendous economic consequences" for any country aiding Iran — while the naval posture around the Strait of Hormuz keeps shipping traffic a fraction of pre-conflict levels. Iran's economy is reportedly contracting sharply (IMF estimates around 6% this year, inflation near 54%), but analysts remain skeptical Tehran capitulates quickly, meaning the oil-price floor from this standoff is unlikely to disappear soon.

### 5. Jobless claims fall to 206,000, beating expectations and showing a still-resilient labor market
**Source:** Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, RTT News
**So what:** Initial claims for the week ending August 15 fell 6,000 to 206,000, below the roughly 210,000 consensus, though the prior week was revised up to 212,000 and the four-week moving average rose to 204,000. Continuing claims ticked up to 1.799 million. The headline beat argues the labor market isn't cracking, but the upward revision and rising continuing claims are worth tracking heading into the Fed's Jackson Hole symposium next week.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### ROST — Ross Stores
- **Thesis:** Off-price retail's structural advantage — buying excess and closeout inventory cheaply and passing savings through — showed up clearly this quarter: comparable sales up 10% on customer traffic (not just price), with operating margin up 205 basis points even stripping out the one-time tariff-refund benefit. That's evidence of real operating leverage, not just an easy comp.
- **Valuation note:** EPS of $2.66 blew past $1.85-$1.93 guidance, though roughly $0.60 of that was a non-recurring IEEPA tariff refund — investors should back that out when comparing to forward multiples rather than anchoring to the headline beat size.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The traffic-driven comp growth (not just ticket size) is the more durable signal, and it's showing up in the same week Walmart flagged consumer trade-offs — suggesting value-conscious shoppers are rotating toward off-price rather than pulling back broadly.
- **Risks / bear case:** The tariff refund inflates this quarter's margin picture and won't repeat; guidance for the back half remains conservative relative to the beat, which management may simply be sandbagging or may reflect genuine caution about the same gas-price pressures Walmart cited.
