## Top of mind

A senior US official told reporters Monday that the Strait of Hormuz is "fully open and under US Navy control," the result of a previously secret four-month operation that cleared roughly 80 Iranian naval mines from shipping lanes. Iran's response: its Supreme National Security Council says it will announce a new maritime exclusion zone outside the strait "in the coming days," with any vessel that enters facing Iranian sanctions. The two claims are hard to reconcile, and the tape is siding with Tehran for now — Hormuz traffic fell to its lowest level since May over the weekend, Brent is near a six-week high around $97.50, and US diesel sits at a record $5.85 a gallon. Equities return today for their first full session since Friday's hot jobs report, with CPI Friday and the September 16 Fed decision still ahead.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. US says it secretly cleared Hormuz of mines; Iran vows a new exclusion zone
**Source:** Al Jazeera, Breitbart, PBS
**So what:** A senior US official said a four-month covert Navy mine-clearing operation removed an estimated 80 Iranian mines and that Hormuz is now "fully open" under US Navy control. Iran's Supreme National Security Council countered that it will announce a new restricted maritime zone beyond the strait within days, placing any vessel that enters on a sanctions list. Both statements are posturing ahead of a real test — actual transit volume — and until traffic normalizes, the market has no reason to believe either side over the price of oil.

### 2. Weekend tanker strikes: US disables three Iranian vessels, Iran hits tankers and fires on US warships
**Source:** Axios, CNBC, NPR
**So what:** US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers Saturday, destroying one, after the IRGC fired ballistic missiles at two US Navy warships (neither was hit). Iran retaliated by striking tankers using a rerouted path around Hormuz and several US-linked vessels. This is the most direct US-Iran naval exchange since the war reignited and is the immediate cause of both the traffic collapse and the fresh oil bid.

### 3. Diesel crack spread hits an all-time high of $102.20/bbl
**Source:** Forbes, CNBC, Option Beast
**So what:** The diesel crack spread — the margin refiners earn turning crude into diesel — hit a record Monday as diesel stays in short supply even with crude prices comparatively contained. US retail diesel is at an all-time-high national average of $5.85/gallon, up roughly 55% since the war began in February, squeezing truckers and any business paying for freight while minting record margins for refiners. This is arguably a cleaner read on physical scarcity than the crude price itself.

### 4. Friday's jobs beat keeps a September 16 hike very much in play
**Source:** CNBC, Forbes (CME FedWatch)
**So what:** August payrolls grew 162,000, roughly triple consensus, pushing CME FedWatch's implied odds of a 25bp hike at the September 16 meeting to around two-thirds as of the last clean read before the print — and the hotter-than-expected number likely pushed that higher still. The Fed's blackout period started September 5, so there's no fresh commentary to calibrate against before CPI Friday and the decision itself.

### 5. Oracle and Adobe headline a loaded earnings week
**Source:** Seeking Alpha, Parameter
**So what:** GameStop reports today after the close, Chewy follows Wednesday, and Oracle and Adobe both report Thursday — Oracle's infrastructure-as-a-service growth (one analyst projects 25% sequential, 116% year-over-year) is the market's next read on AI-datacenter capex demand, while Adobe reports under new leadership for the first time. Kroger closes out the week Friday with a read on the consumer.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### VLO — Valero Energy, record diesel crack spread
- **Catalyst:** Monday's all-time-high diesel crack spread ($102.20/bbl) and a national diesel price near $5.85/gallon are direct margin tailwinds for refiners; Valero's realized refining margin has roughly doubled year over year, and the stock has more than doubled since January alongside peer Marathon Petroleum.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks to the next earnings print — tied to how long the Hormuz-driven diesel shortage persists.
- **What would invalidate:** Any easing of the physical diesel squeeze — more refinery throughput coming online, a genuine Hormuz de-escalation, or demand destruction from record prices — would compress the crack spread quickly.
- **Risk note:** CNBC has already flagged this as a "nearly unprecedented" run for refiner stocks with history suggesting mean reversion. This is a momentum trade on an already-extended move (VLO and MPC have both more than doubled YTD), not a value entry — size accordingly.

### AVAV — AeroVironment, defense-spend catalyst
- **Catalyst:** Continued escalation (weekend tanker and warship strikes) keeps loitering-munition and precision-strike demand elevated; the company has landed multiple US Army Switchblade contracts this year, including a $186 million order under a five-year, $990 million ceiling contract.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks, tied to contract-award news flow and the war's trajectory.
- **What would invalidate:** A credible ceasefire or de-escalation would remove the urgency behind expedited defense orders; a miss or delay on an expected contract award would also hit the stock.
- **Risk note:** Small-cap, contract-dependent name — headline risk cuts both ways, and defense names can gap on rumor as easily as on fact.
