This is a day where the inflation print and the earnings prints could move markets more than the other.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Iran war grinds on; Hormuz flows still a fraction of pre-war levels
**Source:** Bloomberg, Times of Israel
**So what:** Iran says it has no intention of backing down against the US naval presence and will escalate strikes if attacked further, keeping the risk premium in oil bid. The EIA estimates only about 4.9 million barrels/day of oil and petroleum liquids are moving through the Strait of Hormuz currently, down from 21.6 million before the conflict — a stark number that explains why Brent has held above $100 even as headline escalation events come and go.

### 2. Rate-hike odds jump ahead of today's PPI and Friday's CPI
**Source:** CME FedWatch (as of Sept. 7), Investing.com
**So what:** Markets had spent most of the summer debating rate cuts; CME FedWatch now shows meaningfully better-than-even odds of a 25bp hike at the September 16 FOMC meeting, driven by the oil-price shock feeding into inflation expectations. Today's PPI (consensus +0.4% headline, +0.2% core) and Friday's CPI are now the two data points that decide whether that repricing holds into the meeting — a genuinely two-sided outcome for both bonds and equities.

### 3. Oracle and Adobe report tonight — Wall Street tells two very different stories
**Source:** Benzinga, TipRanks, Yahoo Finance/Morgan Stanley research
**So what:** Oracle options are pricing an ~11% implied earnings move (versus a 16% average over its last four reports, including a 40% overnight surge exactly a year ago) on consensus of $1.73 EPS / $19.13B revenue, with cloud backlog and AI capex funding the focus. Adobe, by contrast, was downgraded to Underweight by Morgan Stanley with a $240 target (~10% downside) on concerns AI erodes Creative Cloud's pricing power, layered on a CEO/CFO transition. Same earnings night, opposite narratives.

### 4. Apple's foldable iPhone Duo debuts to a shrug, not a rally
**Source:** CNN, MacRumors, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Apple's first major launch event under new CEO John Ternus unveiled the iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max and the foldable iPhone Duo, plus new Watch and AirPods models — but AAPL closed down slightly (-0.28%) as investors focused on a $100 price hike on the 18 Pro line and margin pressure from chip cost inflation rather than the novelty of the foldable form factor.

### 5. Sector breadth was ugly Wednesday even with oil's tailwind
**Source:** Investrade, market-data aggregators
**So what:** Energy was the only S&P sector to close higher (roughly +1%), with most of the other ten sectors negative — Consumer Discretionary and Consumer Staples were among the weakest, each down more than 1%. Broad-based selling on a day when the "obvious" trade (long energy on higher oil) barely worked is a signal that the yield/inflation story is driving the tape more than any single commodity move.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### XOM — Exxon Mobil
- **Thesis:** The cleanest large-cap way to hold direct exposure to a structurally higher oil-price regime (Hormuz disruption, OPEC+ discipline) without single-basin or single-project risk — integrated refining and chemicals provide some offset when crude spikes squeeze margins elsewhere in the energy complex.
- **Valuation note:** Trading near $161 with a P/E around 20.5x and a dividend yield near 2.6% — the stock is already up roughly 40% year-to-date on the oil rally, so this is not a cheap entry relative to its own history (energy majors have more typically traded in the low-to-mid teens P/E).
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient, not urgent. The macro tailwind (Hormuz flows still at a fraction of pre-war levels) is real, but a name up 40% YTD with a 20x multiple argues for waiting for a pullback rather than chasing here — this is a watch-list-on-weakness situation, not a today trade.
- **Risks / bear case:** A large chunk of the thesis depends on the Iran conflict staying disruptive; any credible de-escalation could unwind the oil premium quickly, and XOM's multiple has little room to re-rate further if crude retreats toward Goldman's $80 base case.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### ORCL — Oracle, post-earnings volatility setup
- **Catalyst:** Q1 FY2027 results after tonight's close; options are pricing an ~11% move on consensus of $1.73 EPS / $19.13B revenue (~28% revenue growth implied, which would be a record pace). The market's read on cloud backlog conversion and AI-capex funding needs matters more than the headline beat/miss.
- **Time horizon:** Overnight through the next few sessions as the print is digested — binary, short-fuse setup.
- **What would invalidate:** Backlog growth or cloud revenue guidance coming in below the 58-64% range management has guided to, or fresh detail on financing/capex that spooks the market on funding risk given Oracle's scale of AI infrastructure commitments.
- **Risk note:** Oracle's own history shows swings from 8.5% to 40% on earnings — size well below the full position you'd hold in a lower-volatility name, and expect the options-implied 11% move to be a floor, not a ceiling, on realized volatility.

### ADBE — Adobe, contested earnings setup
- **Catalyst:** Also reports after tonight's close, with Morgan Stanley freshly Underweight ($240 target, ~10% downside) on AI disruption risk to Creative Cloud pricing power, against a CEO/CFO transition already underway.
- **Time horizon:** Overnight through the next few sessions; this is a "does the bear case get confirmed or pushed back" event, not a multi-week hold.
- **What would invalidate:** Creative Cloud net-new ARR or retention metrics coming in stable would directly rebut the Morgan Stanley thesis; conversely, any guidance commentary that validates freemium/AI cannibalization concerns would likely extend the post-downgrade slide.
- **Risk note:** A stock already carrying a fresh bearish analyst call into earnings can gap either direction hard — this is a name to have a real view on before touching, not a default "earnings are usually a coin flip" trade.
