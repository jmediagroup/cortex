## Top of mind

Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter report, released after Wednesday's close, was the number the whole tape had been holding its breath for: revenue of $96.2 billion (up 106% year over year, above the roughly $92 billion consensus) and third-quarter guidance of $105.8-$110.2 billion, comfortably ahead of expectations. Shares popped nearly 5% in after-hours trading, a strong signal that the AI-capex story still has legs even after a choppy August. That reaction now has to survive Thursday's cash session, which also brings Marvell's own high-stakes earnings after the close, a wall of retail results, and the opening day of the Fed's Jackson Hole symposium — three separate tests of whether Wednesday's calm, PCE-driven pullback was just noise or the start of something more.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Nvidia crushes estimates and guides Q3 well above consensus
**Source:** Benzinga, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Data center revenue rose 117% to $89 billion and adjusted EPS of $2.22 beat the $2.09 estimate, but the number that mattered most was forward guidance — $105.8-$110.2 billion for Q3, implying 86-93% year-over-year growth and effectively rebutting the "AI capex is slowing" worry that had been weighing on chip stocks all month. At roughly 8% of the S&P 500's weight, Nvidia's after-hours pop is a market-moving event in its own right, not just a single-stock story.

### 2. Core PCE inflation holds at 3.3%, headline ticks up to 3.7%
**Source:** CNBC, FXStreet, Fox Business
**So what:** The Fed's preferred gauge came in essentially as expected on the core reading but with a hotter headline print, both still well above the 2% target. Combined with Wednesday's Q2 GDP second estimate holding growth at 1.5% annualized, the data gives Fed Chair Kevin Warsh little cover to sound dovish at Friday's Jackson Hole keynote — arguably the more important macro event of the week once Nvidia's number is digested.

### 3. Meta settles state social-media-addiction suits for roughly $16.7 billion
**Source:** CNBC, Forbes, UPI
**So what:** A bipartisan coalition of state attorneys general (California, Colorado, New Jersey, and Kentucky leading a combined case) reached a settlement requiring Meta to pay out over a 10-year period and to adopt product changes — default two-hour daily time limits on Instagram and Facebook, nighttime access blocks, and hidden like counts, among others. The dollar figure is large but spread over a decade and pending court approval; the bigger question for the stock is whether this becomes a template other AG coalitions use against Snap, TikTok, or YouTube next.

### 4. Marvell reports Thursday after the close — the AI trade's second big test this week
**Source:** The Motley Fool, Schaeffer's Research
**So what:** Consensus calls for roughly $2.71-2.72 billion in revenue and about $0.93 in EPS, up around 35-39% year over year, with prediction markets pricing an 88% probability of a beat. Options are implying a roughly 13.6% post-earnings move. Investors will be listening for detail on Marvell's $12.2 billion custom-chip deal with Google — a read-through for whether hyperscaler AI capex commitments are actually converting to revenue beyond Nvidia.

### 5. A wall of retail and software earnings lands Thursday
**Source:** CNBC earnings calendar
**So what:** Best Buy, Dollar General, Dollar Tree, Burlington Stores, and Ulta Beauty all report Thursday, giving a broad read on both discretionary and value-retail consumer health heading into the back-to-school and holiday-planning season. Workday, Autodesk, and Affirm add a software and fintech cross-section to the same day, along with Canadian banks Royal Bank of Canada and TD Bank.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### META — Meta Platforms
- **Thesis:** The core advertising business remains highly profitable and Reels/AI-driven ad targeting continue to support engagement and monetization; Wednesday's settlement removes a specific, quantified legal overhang that had been an unpriced tail risk rather than an open-ended one.
- **Valuation note:** The settlement's roughly $16.7-18 billion cost, spread over 10 years, is a manageable fraction of Meta's free cash flow generation and shouldn't materially move any reasonable DCF, but it's worth confirming how the charge is recognized in the next quarterly filing.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a name to watch for how the market prices "overhang removed" versus "precedent set" over the next few sessions rather than a name to chase on the settlement news alone — the stock's reaction Wednesday was modest, suggesting the market had already partly priced a resolution.
- **Risks / bear case:** The settlement's mandated product changes (usage limits, nighttime blocks) could measurably affect engagement metrics that drive ad revenue, and the bipartisan coalition structure makes it a template other state AGs and other platforms will likely reference in future actions.
