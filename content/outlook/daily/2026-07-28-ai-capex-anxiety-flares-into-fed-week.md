## Top of mind

A Wall Street Journal report that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's planned Ohio data center — part of a project expected to top $500 billion once chips are included — sent Nvidia down nearly 5% Monday and dragged the Nasdaq lower even as the Dow and small-caps held up. The obvious read is a fresh jolt of AI-capex anxiety: investors are increasingly uneasy about circular financing arrangements where the chip supplier also backstops its own customer's spending. The less obvious risk is that this isn't isolated to Nvidia — SK Hynix (-13%), Samsung (-10%) and South Korea's Kospi (-9%) sold off just as hard overnight on a separate but related worry (SK Hynix's own $500 billion HBM4 supply pact with Nvidia), and that same capex-return question now sits directly in the path of tomorrow's Fed decision and this week's Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon earnings.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Nvidia in talks to guarantee ~$250B in financing for OpenAI's Ohio data center
**Source:** Wall Street Journal (via CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance)
**So what:** The guarantee would back OpenAI's lease of a 10-gigawatt project SoftBank's energy arm is developing in Ohio, with Nvidia separately said to be discussing financing up to $350 billion of OpenAI's chip purchases. Total project cost is put at $500 billion-plus. The structure — chip supplier financing its own customer's demand — is exactly the kind of circular-financing setup that makes investors nervous about whether AI capex is being driven by real end demand or vendor-financed commitments; Nvidia fell nearly 5% Monday on the report.

### 2. SK Hynix, Samsung, and the Kospi sell off hard on the same capex-return worry
**Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg
**So what:** SK Hynix slumped as much as 13% and Samsung fell up to 10%, dragging South Korea's Kospi down 9% and pulling Japan's Nikkei and Taiwan's benchmark down nearly 4% each. The proximate trigger was last week's $500 billion Nvidia-SK Hynix HBM4 memory supply pact, but the sell-the-news reaction shows the same "who actually pays for this and does it return the capital" question is now global, not a single US mega-cap issue.

### 3. Oil extends its slide as the US-Iran strike pause holds
**Source:** CNBC, Trading Economics
**So what:** WTI fell another 1.7% Tuesday morning to roughly $82, on top of Monday's roughly 6.5% drop, as the ceasefire-style pause between the US and Iran continued to hold. This is the clearest disinflationary offset to the AI-capex jitters and directly supports the case for the Fed holding rates Wednesday — but it's the same pause that has already reversed more than once this year.

### 4. The Fed's two-day meeting opens today; decision Wednesday at 2pm ET
**Source:** Federal Reserve, CME FedWatch-derived estimates via multiple outlets
**So what:** FOMC's July meeting runs today and tomorrow, with the rate decision and Chair press conference Wednesday afternoon. Consensus expects a fifth straight hold at 3.50%-3.75%, but priced odds run only about 61-65% for a hold, meaning roughly a third of the market is still pricing a surprise hike — unusually contested for a meeting most economists have called a formality.

### 5. Analyst moves: Alphabet upgraded, Tesla and Stellantis downgraded
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Defense World, Ad-Hoc News
**So what:** Alphabet was upgraded to Buy from Accumulate at Phillip Securities (price target trimmed to $425 from $450) — notable because Alphabet runs its own TPU silicon and doesn't carry the same off-balance-sheet financing exposure as Nvidia's OpenAI arrangement. Tesla was cut to a lower price target at Piper Sandler ($450 from $500, shares -1.2%), and Stellantis took a rare double downgrade with its target slashed to $4 — both idiosyncratic, company-specific calls rather than part of the AI-capex theme.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### GOOGL — Alphabet
- **Thesis:** Alphabet controls its own AI compute stack through custom TPUs, giving it a structurally different capex profile than pure GPU buyers exposed to vendor-financing arrangements like the one Nvidia and OpenAI are reportedly discussing. A fresh sell-side upgrade this week (Phillip Securities, Buy) adds to a generally constructive analyst tone even after the price target was trimmed slightly.
- **Valuation note:** Alphabet has generally traded at a discount to peer mega-cap multiples on a forward P/E basis for much of 2026, a gap that's widened further as pure-play AI infrastructure names have run hotter and gotten more headline scrutiny this week.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Alphabet reports later in the earnings calendar than Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon this week, so there's no immediate catalyst — this is a name to watch the other four reports for signal on before sizing anything up.
- **Risks / bear case:** Alphabet is not immune to a broader AI-capex re-rating — its own capital spending has risen sharply, and any market-wide repricing of "does this spending pay off" would likely drag Alphabet down with the group even if its financing structure is cleaner. Regulatory and antitrust overhangs remain a separate, ongoing risk.

### NVDA — binary setup into the Fed and Big Tech earnings
- **Catalyst:** Monday's near-5% drop on the OpenAI financing report leaves Nvidia trading into a week where Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday and Apple and Amazon report Thursday — each will disclose its own AI capex trajectory and, potentially, commentary on financing structures like the reported Nvidia-OpenAI deal.

Confirmation of solid, unhedged capex plans without new financing-structure caveats would likely stabilize the name.
- **Risk note:** This is a headline- and earnings-driven binary setup on a stock that has already moved ~5% in a single session this week — size for volatility, not conviction, and expect the position to be stale within days regardless of outcome.

### WM — Waste Management earnings tonight
- **Catalyst:** Waste Management reports Q2 results after Tuesday's close, with consensus around $1.99 EPS on roughly $6.71 billion revenue. Expected revenue growth of about 4.3% year-over-year is a sharp deceleration from the 19% growth logged a year ago (which included a large acquisition), making the organic growth and margin commentary the real story in the print.
- **Time horizon:** Reaction into Wednesday's session.
- **What would invalidate:** A miss on margins or a soft outlook on the underlying (non-acquisition) growth rate would likely pressure the stock; an in-line-or-better print in a week dominated by AI-capex headlines could see it treated as a defensive safe harbor.
- **Risk note:** This is a single-earnings-event trade in a name whose typical moves are modest — don't expect Nvidia-scale volatility, and size accordingly.
