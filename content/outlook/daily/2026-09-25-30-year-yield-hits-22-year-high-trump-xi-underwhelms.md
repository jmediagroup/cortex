The long end of the Treasury curve keeps breaking multi-decade records: the 30-year yield touched 5.50% intraday Thursday, a level last seen in 2004, and the 10-year closed near 5.20%, its highest since 2007. ## Headlines & analysis

### 1. 30-year Treasury yield hits a 22-year high as the bond selloff deepens
**Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg
**So what:** The 30-year touched 5.50% intraday Thursday — its highest since June 2004 — while the 10-year closed near 5.20%, an 18-19 year high, after a weak Treasury buyback operation failed to meet investor demand. Asian trading Friday saw yields extend further, with the 10-year touching a fresh 19-year peak near 5.23% overnight. This is now squarely a "the Fed isn't done" story: futures markets continue pricing additional hikes after September's 25bp move.

### 2. Trump and Xi extend the trade truce two months, but summit is light on substance
**Source:** Bloomberg, Al Jazeera
**So what:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the existing US-China trade truce would run an additional two months, to January 10, just as Xi's plane landed — shorter than the one-year rollover markets had hoped for. The extension defuses near-term tariff risk but leaves export controls, AI technology restrictions, and critical-minerals access unresolved, which is why Chinese and Hong Kong shares slipped even on the news.

### 3. Oracle sends force majeure notice on $165B Stargate data center, shares fall ~4%
**Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg, TechCrunch
**So what:** Oracle notified the developer of its Project Jupiter data center campus in New Mexico — designed for 2.45 gigawatts and central to the Stargate AI buildout — that delays from a denied energy permit and stalled pipeline financing may push back its 2028 target. Oracle says the project "remains on our planned schedule," but the legal maneuver to shield itself from delay penalties is the kind of crack in AI-infrastructure execution worth watching given how much of this year's capex-driven earnings growth depends on projects like this landing on time.

### 4. Costco beats on EPS, but the beat is lower quality than the headline suggests
**Source:** 24/7 Wall St., Investing.com
**So what:** Costco posted $6.75 EPS on $93.9B in net sales (+11.2% y/y), beating the $6.55 consensus, with comps up 9.4% and digital comps up 19.5%. But roughly $0.15 of that beat came from a non-recurring IEEPA tariff refund, and shares still closed down 0.91% in the regular session before a modest after-hours bounce — a reminder to look past the headline number on this one.

### 5. Oil holds near $105 Brent even as Hormuz shipping hits a 6-month high
**Source:** Bloomberg, Reuters
**So what:** Brent settled near $105.69 (+2.5%) and WTI held around $93 Thursday, even as a US commander reported Hormuz oil and LNG shipments reached their highest level in six months — evidence the naval escort effort is working. The risk premium hasn't cooled because Tehran maintains it will restrict navigation while the blockade and sanctions remain, keeping the inflation-via-energy channel live for the Fed.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### ORCL — Oracle
- **Thesis:** Oracle's core database franchise remains a durable cash generator, and its Oracle Cloud Infrastructure backlog (fueled by AI compute demand) is real — a ~4% single-day drop tied to a financing/permitting delay on one campus, rather than a change in the underlying demand picture, is the kind of dislocation worth researching rather than reacting to.
- **Valuation note:** Oracle now carries a meaningfully AI-capex-dependent earnings profile; the stock's multiple has expanded well ahead of free cash flow in recent quarters, so this is a name to size carefully rather than treat as cheap simply because it fell.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The force majeure notice is a legal instrument to avoid delay penalties, not an admission the project is failing — but it does confirm that permitting, power, and financing risk are real constraints on the AI buildout timeline. Worth tracking whether other hyperscaler-linked projects hit similar snags before adding exposure.
- **Risks / bear case:** Oracle has taken on substantial debt to fund data center buildouts tied to a small number of large AI customers; if Project Jupiter or similar campuses slip meaningfully past 2028, the revenue recognition assumptions embedded in the current valuation come under real pressure, and financing costs are rising alongside every other borrower's in this rate environment.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### MGM — MGM Resorts, weak-consumer setup
- **Catalyst:** Susquehanna cut its price target to $46 from $55 ahead of today's final University of Michigan consumer sentiment read; the preliminary September print fell to 47.8, well below the 51.0 consensus and down for a second straight month, with year-ahead inflation expectations jumping to 4.6%.
- **Time horizon:** Through today's final sentiment print and into next week as Vegas visitation and booking data trickle in.
- **What would invalidate:** A final sentiment reading that reverses higher, or holiday-season booking commentary showing discretionary travel spend holding up despite the headline gloom, would undercut the weak-consumer thesis this setup depends on.
- **Risk note:** Sentiment surveys have diverged from actual spending data repeatedly this cycle — treat this as a research/hedge candidate tied to a specific data print, not a standalone short thesis, and size accordingly.

### TLT — long-duration Treasuries, oversold-bounce watch
- **Catalyst:** Three straight sessions of fresh multi-decade highs on the 10- and 30-year, plus a weak buyback operation, have pushed positioning and sentiment toward extremes; today's Fed speakers (Barkin, Hammack, Paulson) are each a chance to either confirm or temper the ~73%+ hike odds now priced for October.
- **Time horizon:** Days to two weeks — a technical/sentiment bounce trade, not a call that the inflation repricing is over.
- **What would invalidate:** A fresh close above Thursday's yield highs on rising volume, or any Fed speaker today reinforcing rather than tempering hike odds, would say the move still has room to run and this setup is early.
- **Risk note:** This is a mean-reversion trade against a genuine, multi-session repricing — those can and do keep running past the point where they look "oversold." Size small and define the yield level that proves the thesis wrong before entering.
