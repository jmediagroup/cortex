## Top of mind

Sam Altman confirmed over the weekend that OpenAI won't go public in 2026, citing the safety work still needed and pointing to rising "worst-case AI scenario" warnings inside the industry — turning last week's AI-pacing essay debate into an actual, concrete capital-markets decision. Chip stocks are the ones paying for it this morning: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index tumbled roughly 5.9% with Nvidia, AMD and Intel all sharply lower. That's landing on the same morning the 10-year Treasury yield pushed above 5% for the first time since 2023 — its highest level since 2007 — with the Fed's rate decision now less than 24 hours away and odds of a 25bp hike sitting above 90%.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. OpenAI delays its IPO past 2026, citing AI-safety concerns
**Source:** Axios, Fortune
**So what:** Sam Altman told Fortune the company won't go public this year given the safety work still ahead, with internal talk of "tapping the brakes" on frontier model development. This is the first time the AI-pacing debate — until now mostly essays and public statements from lab CEOs — has produced an actual, calendar-moving decision. Anthropic, by contrast, is reportedly still targeting an October 2026 listing at a valuation as high as $2 trillion, setting up a real divergence in how the two labs are reading the room.

### 2. 10-year Treasury yield breaks above 5% for the first time since 2023
**Source:** CNBC
**So what:** The benchmark yield's highest level since 2007 is landing one day before the Fed's decision, not after it — a sign the bond market isn't waiting for confirmation. Higher long-end yields mechanically pressure the valuation of long-duration growth stocks, which is part of why chips are underperforming the broader tape today, not just the AI-pacing headlines.

### 3. Chip stocks slammed again as AI-pacing debate turns concrete
**Source:** Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Nvidia, AMD and Intel are all lower, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down roughly 5.9%. Chip order books reportedly remain strong for now — this is a sentiment and multiple-compression move, not yet a confirmed demand cut. The distinction matters: a hyperscaler actually guiding down AI capex would be a different, more serious story than a lab CEO delaying an IPO.

### 4. Fed hike odds above 90% into Wednesday's decision, dot plot also due
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Futures markets are pricing a roughly 90-92% probability of a 25bp hike to a 3.75%-4.00% target range Wednesday at 2pm ET. This is also a Summary of Economic Projections meeting, so the updated dot plot — not just the hike itself — will shape how markets read the path from here.

### 5. Saudi pipeline outage persists; Iran-Gulf talks on Hormuz shipping postponed
**Source:** Reuters-sourced wire reporting
**So what:** The East-West pipeline shutdown is still trapping an estimated 4 million bpd of Saudi export capacity, and the diplomatic talks that had been scheduled to discuss easing Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions have reportedly been postponed rather than held — removing the one near-term de-escalation catalyst this report had been tracking. Brent remains anchored near $107 as a result.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### BAC — Bank of America
- **Thesis:** A money-center bank with a large, sticky deposit base that benefits mechanically from a higher-for-longer rate regime through net interest income, at a moment when the Fed looks set to hike again Wednesday rather than pause. The board just raised the quarterly dividend 14.3% to $0.32/share, a signal of confidence in the earnings base even as capital-markets activity softens.
- **Valuation note:** Trading near $59.33 with a P/E around 14.4 and a dividend yield in the low-2% range post-hike — not a deep-value setup, but not stretched either relative to the broader market's higher multiple.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a "why now" more than a "why patient" call — a rate-hike backdrop is a genuine near-term tailwind for bank net interest margins, though the read isn't clean, which is exactly why it's a core holding and not a trade.
- **Risks / bear case:** CEO Brian Moynihan flagged this week that trading revenue will likely be "relatively flat" year over year and investment-banking fees could fall more than 10% this quarter — a reminder that capital-markets softness partially offsets the net-interest-income tailwind. A hawkish Fed surprise that dents growth expectations broadly would also hit a bank stock's credit-quality assumptions, not just its margin math.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### INTC — Intel, momentum-unwind dip candidate
- **Catalyst:** Intel is down roughly 5.6% Tuesday, the hardest hit of the major chip names, as the AI-pacing story moved from talk to OpenAI's actual IPO delay. The stock had been one of the sector's biggest gainers this year, which is exactly why it's giving back the most now — momentum unwinds hit the names with the most embedded gains hardest.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, watching for either stabilization in the 10-year yield or a hyperscaler capex signal in either direction.
- **What would invalidate:** Any hyperscaler actually guiding down AI infrastructure spending would turn this from a sentiment-driven pullback into a real demand repricing — at that point this is no longer a dip to buy. Continued acceleration in the 10-year past 5.1%-5.2% without stabilizing would also keep multiple compression working against the name regardless of company-specific news.
- **Risk note:** This is a volatile, high-beta name riding a large year-to-date gain, not a value stock — size accordingly, and treat Wednesday's Fed decision as a bigger near-term driver than anything company-specific.
