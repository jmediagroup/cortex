## Top of mind

Stocks pulled back from records on Wednesday, October 7: the S&P 500 fell about 0.2% to roughly 7,802 and the Dow lost about 0.7%, while the 10-year Treasury yield touched roughly 5.35% intraday, its highest since 2002. The Fed's September minutes were reported to show most officials expecting one more hike this year, which is hard to square with a market that has been paying record prices for AI leaders. Today's 30-year auction and PepsiCo's earnings are the next reads on whether the bond market keeps the upper hand.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Stocks retreat from records as yields climb
**Source:** CNBC, Investing.com (levels differ slightly; direction agrees)
**So what:** Two outlets agree the major indexes closed lower after the prior session's records, with the Dow the weakest of the three. The S&P 500 had closed above 7,800 for the first time on Tuesday, so Wednesday's dip leaves it within a rounding error of that mark. Nvidia slipped from its all-time high.

### 2. Fed minutes lean toward one more hike
**Source:** Investing.com
**So what:** The minutes of the September 15-16 meeting were reported to show that most policymakers saw one more rate increase this year. That is a single-source characterization and I could not corroborate the details, so read it as directional. It matters because data since the meeting (a soft jobs report and a softer PCE print) has pushed market odds of an October hike down sharply, which leaves the committee and the market at odds on where this goes.

### 3. Long-end yields at 2002 highs again
**Source:** CNBC, TheStreet
**So what:** The 10-year and 30-year both sat at levels not seen since 2002 even as hike odds fell. That points to term premium and Treasury supply, not just Fed expectations. A 30-year reopening is scheduled today at 1:00 p.m. ET; the September 30-year sale cleared at about 5.31%, the highest since 2001, so another weak auction is a live risk.

### 4. PepsiCo reports third-quarter results this morning
**Source:** PepsiCo, company timing release; preview coverage
**So what:** PepsiCo said it would publish results around 6:00 a.m. ET, with a call at 8:15. Previews put consensus near $2.30 EPS and about $25 billion in revenue, after estimates were trimmed over the past 90 days. I did not find the actual results, so none are reported here. The stock has traded near its 52-week low in recent weeks, per preview coverage, which makes the volume and pricing commentary the thing to read.
