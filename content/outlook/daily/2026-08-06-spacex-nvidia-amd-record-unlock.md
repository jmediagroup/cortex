## Top of mind

SpaceX told the market Wednesday it will build its entire AI infrastructure — including the new "Starmind" orbital-compute satellites — exclusively on Nvidia's chips, cutting AMD out of a relationship AMD had been counting on to prove its AI-data-center case. AMD shares fell as much as 8% even after a record quarter; Nvidia rose roughly 3-4%. That news lands the same day as SpaceX's first and largest post-IPO lockup expiration — roughly 911.5 million insider shares, worth $116-123 billion, become eligible for trading today, August 6 — a supply event that has already knocked SpaceX shares down double digits this week even as the company reported revenue nearly doubling year-over-year. Layer in a genuine macro tailwind (an Iran-Oman deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz shipping is pushing oil lower and gold to seven-week highs) and today is a test of how much single-stock, single-customer news can move the AI trade versus how much the broader disinflation story can offset it.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. SpaceX goes all-in on Nvidia, cuts AMD out of its AI buildout
**Source:** TechTimes, American Bazaar, Not a Tesla App
**So what:** SpaceX said it will build its terrestrial and orbital AI infrastructure — including "Starmind," an orbital network of compute satellites running Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips — exclusively on Nvidia hardware, ending AMD's prior role in SpaceX's stack. AMD had been pitching its Instinct MI450 line as a credible alternative to Nvidia for hyperscaler-scale buyers; losing SpaceX publicly, on the same day as a record quarter, undercuts that pitch more than the earnings beat helps it. Nvidia shares rose roughly 3-4%; AMD fell as much as 8%.

### 2. SpaceX's record lockup expiration arrives today
**Source:** Motley Fool, Investing.com, ecmsource
**So what:** SpaceX priced its June IPO at $135/share, the largest US IPO on record. Today, August 6, is the first of a staggered unlock schedule — roughly 911.5 million insider shares (about 20% of eligible holdings, worth $116-123 billion at current prices) become tradable. That's a genuine supply-and-demand test independent of the company's fundamentals: shares are already down double digits this week even though Q2 revenue nearly doubled year-over-year and losses narrowed.

### 3. Iran and Oman reach a Hormuz shipping agreement; oil keeps sliding
**Source:** Bloomberg, TradingEconomics
**So what:** An agreement between Iran and Oman on a shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz is the latest step toward normalizing flows through the chokepoint that had been effectively closed since February. WTI is down roughly 11% over the week's first three sessions to the mid-$70s. Markets have scaled back Fed rate-hike expectations to just one increase priced in by year-end, down from two last week, on the disinflation read-through.

### 4. Eli Lilly crushes Q2 on GLP-1 demand, raises full-year revenue guidance
**Source:** FX Leaders, CNBC
**So what:** Lilly reported EPS of $8.38 versus $6.07 expected and revenue of $23.0 billion, up 48% year-over-year, then raised its full-year revenue outlook to $85-87 billion from $82-85 billion. Mounjaro revenue rose 91% to $9.9 billion; Zepbound US revenue rose 44% to $4.9 billion even as net pricing came down — volume is more than offsetting price. Shares rose roughly 4%.

### 5. Disney beats on earnings despite a revenue miss, lifts buyback target
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Motley Fool, Benzinga
**So what:** Disney's fiscal Q3 adjusted EPS of $2.06 beat the $1.86 estimate even as revenue of $25.25 billion came in about $150 million light. Experiences (parks) revenue rose 10% to $9.97 billion on higher attendance and per-capita spending, and total segment operating income jumped 21%. Management reaffirmed double-digit full-year EPS growth and raised the fiscal 2026 buyback target to at least $9 billion from a lower prior level. Shares rose roughly 4% premarket — a reminder that parks and streaming, not the legacy TV business, are now doing the heavy lifting.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### LLY — Eli Lilly
- **Thesis:** Lilly's GLP-1 franchise (Mounjaro, Zepbound) is compounding on both volume and international expansion even as net pricing erodes — a sign of durable demand rather than a one-time launch pop, and management keeps raising guidance rather than just meeting it.
- **Valuation note:** Trading at a rich multiple to the broader market and to pharma peers; the raise-and-beat pattern has been consistent enough to earn some premium, but the bar for the next quarter keeps climbing with it.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. The stock already popped roughly 4% on the news — better to see whether the raised guidance holds through the next print than to chase today's move.
- **Risks / bear case:** Oral GLP-1 competition (from Lilly's own pipeline and rivals like Novo Nordisk) could eventually pressure pricing further; any signal that international volume growth is decelerating would hit a stock priced for continued acceleration.

### NVDA — Nvidia
- **Thesis:** Being named SpaceX's exclusive AI-chip supplier for a first-of-its-kind orbital compute platform reinforces Nvidia's position as the default choice even for the most demanding, best-capitalized AI infrastructure buyers — a data point for the moat, not just a single contract win.
- **Valuation note:** Nvidia's multiple already prices in a dominant position; today's news defends that position rather than creating fresh upside on its own — treat it as thesis confirmation, not a new catalyst to size up on.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. This is a name to own through the cycle rather than trade around single headlines; the more interesting question is whether AMD's competitive setback here is durable or one account among many.
- **Risks / bear case:** Concentration risk cuts both ways — Nvidia's dominance in AI infrastructure means any slowdown in hyperscaler/AI-capex spending, or a genuine second-source alternative gaining share, would hit the stock disproportionately given how much good news is already priced in.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMD — post-SpaceX-news oversold bounce watch
- **Catalyst:** AMD posted a record quarter Wednesday night but fell as much as 8% on the same-day news that SpaceX will exclusively use Nvidia going forward — a sentiment-driven drop on a fundamentally strong print.
- **Time horizon:** Days, through the next 1-2 sessions as the market separates "AMD lost one high-profile account" from "AMD's broader data-center growth story is impaired."
- **What would invalidate:** A continued slide below Wednesday's after-hours low on incremental bad news (another customer loss, a guidance walk-back) would signal the market sees this as more than a single-account issue.
- **Risk note:** This is a bet that the selloff overshot on headline risk, not a fundamentals call — size small and be prepared to be wrong if other hyperscalers follow SpaceX's lead.

### SPCX — record lockup unlock volatility
- **Catalyst:** Roughly 911.5 million insider shares ($116-123 billion) become eligible for trading today, the largest such unlock on record, arriving right after a strong-revenue-but-high-capex earnings report already pressured the stock.
- **Time horizon:** Days to weeks — through the initial selling window and into how much of the unlocked stock actually hits the market versus gets held.
- **What would invalidate:** A stabilization or bounce in the first day or two would suggest the unlock was largely pre-priced; a continued grind lower into next week would suggest real insider selling pressure, not just headline fear.
- **Risk note:** SpaceX has no earnings-reaction or unlock-reaction history to anchor expectations — this is a name that can gap sharply in either direction on flow alone, independent of fundamentals.
