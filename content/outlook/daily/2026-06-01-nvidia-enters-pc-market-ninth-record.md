## Top of mind

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang unveiled the RTX Spark at Computex 2026 — a "superchip" combining the Blackwell GPU with a new ARM-based N1X CPU co-developed with MediaTek, launching fall 2026 inside slim laptops and desktops from Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and Microsoft Surface. The announcement immediately reshuffled the PC semiconductor stack: Qualcomm fell 9.5%, Intel shed 6.5%, ARM surged 12%, Dell gained 10%, HP 8%, and Microsoft 4%. The S&P 500 closed at 7,599.96 (+0.26%) for a ninth consecutive record close, capping what is now the longest unbroken weekly record streak since the rally began in January.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Nvidia unveils RTX Spark: Blackwell GPU + ARM CPU enters the PC market
**Source:** Fast Company, Gurufocus, Yahoo Finance (June 1, 2026)
**So what:** The RTX Spark pairs Nvidia's Blackwell GPU architecture with a new ARM-based N1X CPU co-developed with MediaTek — Nvidia's first entry into the PC processor market. OEM partners include Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and Microsoft Surface for fall 2026 shipments. The competitive implication is direct: Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite PC play and Intel's Core Ultra are now fighting an adversary with the strongest brand in AI compute and a differentiated GPU+CPU integration story that neither can replicate. D.A. Davidson added NVDA to its top-pick list on the news.

### 2. ServiceNow jumps 7.7% as Oppenheimer and BofA argue AI accelerates, not disrupts, enterprise software
**Source:** Oppenheimer / Eciks.org, Benzinga, FinancialContent (June 1, 2026)
**So what:** ServiceNow extended a roughly 40% four-session rally — the strongest in its history — with Oppenheimer reiterating Outperform and BofA reinstating Buy at $130. Both analysts cited accelerating enterprise demand for agentic AI workflows, arguing that AI tools increase ServiceNow's stickiness rather than cannibalizing it. The Boomi Workflow Data Network partnership embeds NOW's AI data fabric deeper into enterprise systems. This session is a direct market rebuke of the "AI kills SaaS" thesis.

### 3. MGM Resorts surges 15.8% on Barry Diller's $18 billion takeover bid
**Source:** Skift, CBS News, Deadline (June 1, 2026)
**So what:** Barry Diller's People Inc. (formerly IAC) — already owning 26.1% of MGM with two board seats — offered $48.30 per share in cash for all remaining shares, valuing MGM at over $18 billion. The bid represents a 24.1% premium to the 30-day VWAP and a 10.6% premium to the most recent closing price. MGM rose 15.8% on the news, with the stock trading above the $48.30 bid price by close — signaling the market expects either a higher offer or a competing bid. Diller's stated rationale: MGM's physical experiences are AI-proof and the company is structurally undervalued in public markets.

### 4. WTI spikes 6% intraday as US-Iran communication fears resurface
**Source:** CNBC, The Hill (June 1, 2026)
**So what:** WTI crude futures surged above $92 per barrel intraday after Iranian media reported suspended communications, raising concerns about the fragile ceasefire framework. Prices pulled back after President Trump confirmed negotiations were continuing. Oil had fallen nearly 17% month-to-date into Friday's close on Iran deal optimism; today's spike is the first live test of how fast the trade can reverse if the ceasefire agreement — still awaiting Trump's final signature — falls apart before it is formalized. The S&P headline summarized it bluntly: "tech rally overpowers oil spike."

### 5. HPE Q2 2026 after hours: $0.79 EPS vs. $0.54 est., revenue +40% YoY, guidance raised 40%+
**Source:** Seeking Alpha, Investing.com, FX Leaders (June 1, 2026, after close)
**So what:** Hewlett Packard Enterprise reported record Q2 revenue of $10.68 billion (+40% YoY) against a $9.79 billion consensus estimate, with EPS of $0.79 versus $0.54 expected. The company raised FY2026 EPS guidance to $3.35–$3.45 from $2.30–$2.50 prior — a 40%+ increase driven by accelerating demand for AI inference servers. Shares surged 30%+ after hours. The print confirms the Dell thesis is not a single-company story: AI server demand is broad enough to lift the entire enterprise hardware stack simultaneously, and HPE may be the cheaper way to own the same thesis.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### NVDA — Nvidia
- **Thesis:** The RTX Spark announcement opens a third vertical for Nvidia — PC chips — alongside data center GPUs and networking. The ARM+Blackwell GPU integration directly threatens Qualcomm's PC strategy while creating an AI-native PC platform that traditional CPU vendors cannot replicate at equivalent performance-per-watt. Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Microsoft Surface are already committed OEM partners. This is TAM expansion: Nvidia is no longer just a data center play, it is now embedded in the device layer.
- **Valuation note:** Nvidia's multiple remains elevated on data center GPU demand; the PC entry is not yet in consensus revenue models. RTX Spark could expand FY2027–FY2028 revenue targets materially if OEM adoption matches the announced partner list. D.A. Davidson's top-pick addition today signals sell-side models are beginning to reprice.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. NVDA is up 6% today on a fall 2026 product announcement — the market is pricing optionality, not current earnings. RTX Spark ships no revenue until Q3–Q4 2026. Enter on consolidation, not news-day momentum. The data center thesis alone likely justifies holding a core position at current levels; the PC optionality is free upside if adoption meets expectations.
- **Risks / bear case:** Obvious: Nvidia is entering an OEM-driven, low-margin PC market in structural unit decline. A narrative catalyst with minimal near-term P&L impact is a real possibility. Non-obvious: The N1X CPU co-developed with MediaTek creates ARM license dependency and supply chain complexity that Dell and HP's procurement teams have more negotiating leverage over than Nvidia's data center team is accustomed to. If OEMs can play Qualcomm against Nvidia on pricing, Nvidia's PC economics get worse before they get better.

### HPE — Hewlett Packard Enterprise
- **Thesis:** HPE's Q2 print confirms it is riding the same AI infrastructure wave as Dell — but trading at a steeper hardware discount that has not fully re-rated. Record Q2 revenue of $10.68 billion (+40% YoY) and a guidance raise to $3.35–$3.45 EPS (from $2.30–$2.50) are the strongest signals yet that demand for HPE's ProLiant AI servers and GreenLake managed infrastructure is accelerating alongside hyperscaler capex. If the new guidance range holds, HPE is likely still cheaper than Dell on an earnings-adjusted basis post-gap.
- **Valuation note:** Pre-announcement HPE traded at a steep discount to Dell on EV/Revenue. The 30%+ AH surge partially closes that gap but the June 2 open price will determine how much of the guidance raise is already priced. The comparison to Dell — which trades at significantly higher multiples for the same underlying AI server demand — is the valuation anchor.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Wait for the gap to settle. Post-earnings gap dynamics on hardware names typically see 2–5 sessions of re-pricing before a clean entry window opens. The guidance raise is real; the thesis doesn't expire while you wait for the gap to clarify.
- **Risks / bear case:** Obvious: HPE is not Nvidia or Dell in the AI hardware stack — it is a meaningful but smaller player, and Dell's direct hyperscaler relationships give it structural advantages HPE cannot fully match. Non-obvious: The FY2026 guidance raise was partially enabled by completing the $3.5 billion H3C exit — a one-time cash event. Sustaining $3.35–$3.45 EPS through FY2026 requires organic server demand to continue at the Q2 pace for two more quarters. If AI inference spending hits a digestion pause in Q3, the gap between guided and delivered EPS closes fast.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### QCOM — Qualcomm (watch for oversold bounce)
- **Catalyst:** Qualcomm fell 9.5% on the RTX Spark announcement — a large one-day reaction to a competitive threat whose first product doesn't ship until fall 2026. The Snapdragon X Elite has real enterprise traction in Windows PCs; today's move prices existential risk that a fall product launch does not yet confirm. A bounce off the -9.5% level is plausible if the market's initial reaction is too aggressive for a product eight months from shipping.
- **Time horizon:** 3–10 days. This is a sentiment-rebound trade against an oversold condition, not a thesis reversal.
- **What would invalidate:** Additional OEM defections from Qualcomm to RTX Spark before the product ships. Any Nvidia pricing announcement suggesting RTX Spark will undercut Snapdragon X Elite for OEMs materially. Intel and Microsoft jointly announcing competing ARM silicon that further fragments the PC market.
- **Risk note:** Trading against a structural narrative shift is high-risk. Qualcomm's PC diversification strategy — built over years to reduce mobile dependence — is under direct competitive pressure. Size at 1% or less of portfolio risk. Define exit at the pre-announcement level approximately 10% above today's close. If the stock doesn't stabilize within 3 sessions, do not add.

### MGM — M&A arb (monitor; stock above initial bid)
- **Catalyst:** People Inc.'s $48.30/share cash offer values MGM at $18 billion. MGM's 15.8% close today appears to have priced through the $48.30 bid, suggesting the market expects a higher offer or a competing bid. If confirmed above $48.30 at close, the classic arb trade shifts from "buy at discount to bid" to "bet on bid increase."
- **Time horizon:** Deal timeline is uncertain — People Inc. bid is non-binding and MGM's board must respond. Weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate:** MGM rejects the bid with no counter and no competing bidder emerges. Regulatory concerns about People Inc.'s existing gaming and media assets create deal risk. Broader market selloff de-risks the bid premium.
- **Risk note:** MGM trading above the initial bid means this is no longer a pure arb — it requires a higher offer to resolve profitably from current levels. Size appropriately for M&A uncertainty. Diller's 26.1% stake gives him leverage but also limits the universe of competing bidders willing to negotiate with an activist block holder.
