## Top of mind

Monday's close was a study in internals over headlines: the S&P 500 fell 0.28% and the Nasdaq dropped 0.76% as memory chips (Micron, SanDisk, Western Digital) sold off again, while the Dow gained 0.26% on a rotation into consumer staples, communication services, and financials. That defensive tilt landed the same day Washington opened two new fronts at once — a sweeping global sanctions campaign against Iran and the collapse of US-Canada trade talks into 50% tariffs — yet the VIX only ticked up to 15.89, still inside its "normal" range. The obvious read is that markets are treating both stories as contained; the less obvious one is that Wednesday's Nvidia earnings now has to answer two questions simultaneously — is AI capex still accelerating, and can risk appetite hold up through a week that also includes Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote Friday.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Treasury unveils "Operation Economic Outcast" — a global sanctions campaign against Iran
**Source:** Al Jazeera, CNBC
**So what:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced sanctions targeting Iran's aviation, digital-assets, gold, technology, and shipping sectors, plus 60 individuals and vessels, aiming to cut off all Iranian revenue sources including oil. Oil prices still fell Monday (WTI -2.5% to $84.89, Brent -2.5% to $92.06) — a sign markets are skeptical the sanctions bite hard enough to tighten global supply near-term, or are simply booking profits after last week's run-up.

### 2. US-Canada trade talks collapse; 50% tariffs on $20 billion of Canadian goods now in effect
**Source:** Al Jazeera, NBC News, Global News (Canada)
**So what:** Talks broke down Friday night after Prime Minister Mark Carney recalled negotiators over what he called "uneconomic" last-minute US demands. Canada plans dollar-for-dollar retaliation starting September 8, targeting steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural machinery, paper, and electronics. This is a fresh, un-hedged supply-chain and margin risk for any US company with meaningful Canadian input costs or cross-border manufacturing — one the market hasn't fully priced given Monday's muted VIX reaction.

### 3. Memory chips slide again on reports Apple may shift sourcing to Chinese suppliers
**Source:** 24/7 Wall St., Invezz, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** SanDisk fell as much as 9%, Micron and Western Digital around 7%, on reports the administration may let Apple source memory from Chinese suppliers ahead of a planned Xi visit — compounded by Samsung's shareholder-return plan disappointing investors in the same session. Analysts framed it as a positioning unwind after this year's extraordinary AI-memory gains (SanDisk was up over 600% year-to-date entering the month) rather than a fundamental demand problem, but the size of the moves shows how crowded the trade has become.

### 4. Consumer sentiment weakens as Conference Board data lands today
**Source:** University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** The University of Michigan's sentiment index fell to 51.0 in August from 55.2 in July, missing both Reuters' (54.5) and Bloomberg's (55) consensus, with respondents citing inflation worries tied to the Middle East conflict. The Conference Board's own Consumer Confidence Index — a different, closely watched gauge — is due out today at 10 a.m. ET after missing expectations in July (90.8 vs. an upwardly revised 92.2 in June); a second consecutive miss would reinforce the Michigan read that consumers are more nervous than the mixed equity tape suggests.

### 5. Nvidia reports Wednesday, Marvell Thursday, in the summer's highest-stakes earnings week
**Source:** Intellectia.ai earnings previews, TradingKey
**So what:** Consensus centers on roughly $92-95 billion in Nvidia quarterly revenue (up sharply year-over-year) driven by continued Blackwell demand; sell-side price targets cluster $275-325. Coming right after Monday's memory-chip unwind and alongside two live trade-policy fronts, Nvidia's commentary on hyperscaler capex durability will be read as the tell for whether this week's chip weakness is sector-specific profit-taking or an early signal of broader AI-spending caution.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### MU — Micron Technology
- **Thesis:** Micron sits at the center of the AI-memory supercycle — HBM and high-capacity DRAM demand from hyperscalers has been a structural, multi-year growth driver, not a one-quarter phenomenon, even though Monday's move traded it like the latter.
- **Valuation note:** After 2026's run-up (peers like SanDisk were up over 600% year-to-date before this month's pullbacks), the stock is not statistically cheap on trailing multiples, but forward estimates have been revised up repeatedly through the AI-memory cycle — the relevant question is whether guidance keeps pace with the stock, not whether the multiple looks low in isolation.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a "buy the unwind, not the story" setup at best — Monday's 7% drop was driven by a single sourcing report (Apple potentially shifting to Chinese suppliers) that analysts have characterized as positioning-driven, not confirmed fundamental change. Patient entries around further weakness make more sense than chasing a bounce.
- **Risks / bear case:** If the Apple-sourcing report proves directionally accurate, or if AI-capex spending decelerates (a live question given this week's Nvidia print), memory pricing power — which drove most of the 2026 re-rating — could reverse quickly. The sector is also inherently cyclical with a history of overbuilding into demand peaks.
