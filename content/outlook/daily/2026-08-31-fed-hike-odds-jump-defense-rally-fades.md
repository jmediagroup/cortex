## Top of mind

The morning's Hormuz-strike trade — defense stocks up 5-10%, oil sharply higher — mostly reversed by the close: Lockheed Martin finished down 0.5%, RTX down 1.9%, even as Brent held near $88-90 and WTI settled up 2.6% at roughly $85.50. The more durable move was in rates — CME FedWatch's odds of a September hike climbed to about 66% (from Friday's near-coin-flip), and the 10-year Treasury touched 4.75% intraday, its highest since January 2025, before easing back to close little changed near 4.72%. Two idiosyncratic stories did more damage than the geopolitics: the FTC sued Amazon over ad-pricing practices (shares -2.7%), and a California wildfire bill that stripped out liability protection sent Edison International down roughly 24% — its worst day since 2001 — and PG&E down about 19%.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Defense stocks give back the premarket Hormuz pop
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Benzinga
**So what:** Lockheed (-0.5%) and RTX (-1.9%) closed lower after opening up 5-10% on the weekend US-Iran exchange near the Strait of Hormuz. The fade suggests markets aren't yet pricing a sustained shipping disruption — worth remembering before chasing the next headline spike in this group.

### 2. CME FedWatch hike odds climb to ~66%
**Source:** Forbes, Bloomberg
**So what:** Odds of a 25bp September hike rose intraday from Friday's near-50/50 read to roughly 66%, with the 10-year hitting 4.75% intraday — its highest since January 2025 — before settling back near 4.72%. Higher-for-longer is now the market's base case, not the tail case.

### 3. FTC sues Amazon over ad-pricing practices
**Source:** Bloomberg, Forbes
**So what:** The FTC and 20+ state AGs allege Amazon covertly raised the minimum spend advertisers paid for "sponsored" placement, allegedly generating tens of billions in extra profit over seven years. Shares fell about 2.7-3%. This is a multi-year litigation overhang, not a near-term earnings hit.

### 4. California wildfire bill craters PG&E and Edison International
**Source:** Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Benzinga
**So what:** State lawmakers stripped liability-cap language from SB 492, leaving investor-owned utilities exposed to open-ended wildfire costs. Edison International fell roughly 24% (worst day since 2001) and PG&E about 19%; Mizuho cut EIX to Neutral and slashed its target from $86 to $70.

### 5. August closes out as a fifth straight winning month for the Dow
**Source:** CNBC, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Despite Monday's pullback, the S&P gained roughly 2.6-3.0% and the Nasdaq 3.5-4.1% in August — a reminder that daily headline risk (Hormuz, FTC, wildfire bill) is being absorbed by a market still in an uptrend, not one cracking under it.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMZN — Post-lawsuit dip
- **Catalyst:** FTC/state-AG ad-pricing suit knocked shares down ~2.7-3%; litigation of this type typically takes years to resolve and rarely produces an near-term earnings shock.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, watching for stabilization.
- **What would invalidate:** A broader amendment to the complaint targeting core marketplace-fee practices (not just ad pricing), or a second regulator (DOJ) joining, which would signal this is bigger than a single-issue suit.
- **Risk note:** Litigation headline risk can resurface unpredictably over quarters; size for a multi-year overhang, not a one-week trade.

### RTX/LMT — Hormuz-headline mean reversion
- **Catalyst:** Today's fade shows the market isn't yet pricing sustained disruption; any confirmed attack on tanker traffic or mine-laying in the Strait would likely reprice the group sharply higher again.
- **Time horizon:** Days — this is a headline-reactive trade, not a thesis.
- **What would invalidate:** A de-escalation statement from either side, or several sessions without follow-through violence.
- **Risk note:** Whipsaw risk is high; today's reversal is itself the evidence that this group overshoots on the way up.
