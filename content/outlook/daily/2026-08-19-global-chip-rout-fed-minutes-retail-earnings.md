## Headlines & analysis

### 1. South Korea's KOSPI plunges 5.8%, triggers sidecar circuit breaker as Samsung and SK Hynix lead a global chip rout
**Source:** Investing.com, CNBC, Seoul Economic Daily
**So what:** The Korea Exchange halted program sell orders for five minutes after the index's steepest drop in months; Samsung fell roughly 7.8% and SK Hynix nearly 10%. Both are among the clearest plays on AI-memory demand, which makes them the first to get hit when investors pull back from the broader AI trade — and this move followed a six-session KOSPI winning streak, so some of it is profit-taking rather than a fresh negative catalyst. Still, the speed and size of the reversal is the story: this spread from Wall Street's Tuesday session into a forced-selling event in Asia within 24 hours.

### 2. 30-year Treasury yield holds near 5.33%, a fresh 19-year high, pressuring duration-sensitive assets globally
**Source:** CNBC, TheStreet
**So what:** Heavy government debt issuance, tariff-driven inflation that's proven stickier than hoped, and market recalibration around the new Fed chair's approach are all cited as drivers. It's not confined to the US — Japan's 10-year hit a three-decade high, Germany's 30-year the highest since 2011, and France's 30-year the highest since 2008. That's a global repricing of long-duration risk, and it's the backdrop against which the chip selloff is happening, not a separate story.

### 3. Trump rules out Iran talks, reaffirms the Hormuz-area naval blockade "remains in force"
**Source:** Truth Social (via wire reports)
**So what:** Trump stated flatly there are "no talks or conversations going on, or scheduled" with Iran, closing the door on the negotiated-resolution scenario that would deflate the oil and defense risk premium. WTI climbed toward $84.61 and Brent to $91.55 on the comments. The binary flagged in this report yesterday hasn't resolved — it's now leaning toward the escalation side.

### 4. TJX, Target, and Lowe's report before Wednesday's open, the busiest retail print of the week
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Schaeffer's, 24/7 Wall St
**So what:** TJX is expected to post EPS near $1.18 on $15.14B revenue (7.3% EPS growth, 5.1% revenue growth), with options pricing a 5.1% post-earnings move — nearly double its 2.6% average reaction over the last eight quarters. Analyst sentiment diverges sharply on the other two: Lowe's carries a decisively bullish 24-buy/10-hold/1-sell split, while Target sits at a far more cautious 12-buy/23-hold/3-sell. Together with last week's soft retail-sales print, this is the clearest read yet on whether the stretched-consumer narrative is broadening or staying contained to a few names.

### 5. July FOMC minutes due 2:00 p.m. ET — the tiebreaker on three weeks of conflicting rate-path signals
**Source:** CNBC, CME FedWatch-based trackers

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### LOW — Lowe's Companies
- **Thesis:** A durable, two-player (with Home Depot) moat in home-improvement retail, with a decisively bullish analyst base (24 buy, 10 hold, just 1 sell) heading into today's print. The category has structural tailwinds from an aging US housing stock that needs ongoing repair and renovation spend regardless of the rate cycle's exact path.
- **Valuation note:** Trailing P/E around 18.9x, below its own 3-year (19.3x) and 5-year (19.2x) averages and roughly 11% below one model's estimated fair value (~$252 vs. a ~$223 share price) — modestly undervalued rather than stretched, unlike much of the market right now.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Let today's print clear first. A clean beat that supports the bullish analyst consensus is the more attractive entry than chasing pre-earnings; if the stock sells off on a soft housing-related outlook despite an in-line print, that's arguably the better opportunistic entry into the same long-term thesis.
- **Risks / bear case:** Housing turnover remains historically low with mortgage rates elevated, which caps the DIY/renovation catalyst even if the long-term repair-spend thesis holds. A miss today that confirms consumer softness (echoing Friday's retail-sales data) would be a read-through risk for the whole home-improvement category, not just this name.
