## Headlines & analysis

### 1. CoreWeave jumps 24.7% after a beat and raised guidance
**Source:** Reuters (via MarketScreener), stock-data recaps
**So what:** CoreWeave (CRWV) closed at $60.77, up from $48.73, its biggest one-day percentage gain in more than three months, after posting Q3 revenue of about $3.66 billion, raising full-year guidance and saying it expects adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028. It is the clearest evidence yet that demand for debt-funded AI capacity is holding up. Shares were reported back near $57 after hours as Nvidia fell.

### 2. Nvidia falls about 8.6% after hours on reports its board may remove the CEO
**Source:** The Wall Street Journal (as cited by CNN, CNBC and others)
**So what:** Nvidia closed the regular session at $173.73. It then dropped after the Journal reported the board was preparing to remove Huang, following a separate New York Times report related to the ongoing personal-conduct investigation. No leadership change has been announced, and reported after-hours losses range from about 7% to 9%. I am not repeating the allegations. For investors, the issue is key-person risk at the largest single holding in many index funds, days before earnings that one source lists for November 17.

### 3. Chip stocks rally across the board; the SOX rises 5.7%
**Source:** Reuters, same-day market recaps
**So what:** Every SOX component reportedly closed higher, a sign that investors are once again willing to pay for AI hardware exposure after last week's sell-off. That rally was priced before the Nvidia news, so expect at least part of it to reverse today.

### 4. Apple closes at a record, about a $5.16 trillion market cap
**Source:** TheStreet, Stocktitan
**So what:** Apple rose about 2% to a record above $355, and one report puts it roughly $1 trillion ahead of Nvidia in market value. Large-cap tech leadership is starting to shift away from AI chips toward cash-rich platform companies, which matters if Nvidia's problems keep going.

### 5. Applied Materials and Cisco report tonight, and PPI comes Friday
**Source:** Benzinga, Investing.com, Zerohedge, Stocktitan
**So what:** Applied Materials (consensus about $10.30 billion in sales and $4.05 adjusted EPS, with options pricing about a 10% move) and Cisco (consensus about $18.1 billion in revenue and $1.32–$1.33 EPS, with options pricing about 7%–9%) report after the close today. They test AI demand outside Nvidia. October PPI on Friday is the next inflation check before the December 8–9 FOMC meeting.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMAT — Earnings as the read on chip demand outside Nvidia
- **Catalyst:** Fiscal Q4 results after the close today, with consensus for sales up about 51% to about $10.30 billion. The stock reportedly rose about 11% on November 9 ahead of the print.
- **Time horizon:** Days, through the earnings reaction.
- **What would invalidate:** Guidance below consensus, or any sign that customers are pausing equipment orders. A move against you larger than the roughly 10% options-implied range means the thesis is wrong. Exit.
- **Risk note:** The run-up before earnings raises the bar for the results, and Nvidia's headlines could swamp a clean print. Size small, or wait for the reaction instead of holding through the report.

### NVDA — Watch, don't catch
- **Catalyst:** Board action on CEO leadership, and earnings reportedly due November 17. Confirm the date with Nvidia investor relations.
- **Time horizon:** Days to weeks. Wait for an official board statement before taking any position.
- **What would invalidate:** For anyone considering a buy into the drop: a confirmed leadership change without a credible succession plan, or a further drop below the after-hours low of about $159 reported Wednesday. That would be the lowest close since July 2025.
- **Risk note:** This is headline risk, not a valuation call. Governance stories often take longer to resolve than the first reports suggest. A clear succession plan could produce a sharp bounce, and more reports could push the stock lower. If you act at all, use very small size.
