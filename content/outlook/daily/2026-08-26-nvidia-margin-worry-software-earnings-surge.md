## Top of mind

Nvidia beat Wall Street on every headline number after Wednesday's close — $96.22 billion in revenue (up 106% year-over-year), $2.22 in adjusted EPS, and a Q3 guide of $108 billion that tops $100 billion for the first time in company history — yet shares whipsawed through the earnings call and struggled to hold early gains once management guided Q3 gross margin down a point to 74%. The regular session itself was a non-event, with all three major indices closing marginally lower ahead of the print, so tonight's real story is what happened after the bell: Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Okta all posted double-digit after-hours pops on clean beats-and-raises, while the market's most important AI bellwether got a much more contested reception. Read together, it looks like investors are starting to price AI margin durability as hard as they price AI growth — a subtle but real shift from earlier in this earnings cycle.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Nvidia beats on revenue and EPS, guides Q3 above $100 billion, but the margin outlook gets sold
**Source:** CNBC, Investing.com, company release
**So what:** Nvidia's Q2 FY27 revenue of $96.22 billion (vs. roughly $92 billion consensus) and adjusted EPS of $2.22 (vs. roughly $2.09 consensus) both beat comfortably, with data-center revenue up 117% year-over-year to $89.0 billion. The Q3 guide of $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, is the first time the company has ever guided above $100 billion in a single quarter and topped the roughly $105 billion Street estimate — but a cut to adjusted gross-margin guidance (74% for Q3, down from an actual 75% in Q2) sent shares on a volatile, two-sided after-hours ride that hadn't fully settled as of this writing. At roughly 8% of the S&P 500's weight, how this resolves by Thursday's open matters well beyond the stock itself.

### 2. Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Okta all surge double digits on clean beats
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, StockStory/FinancialContent
**So what:** Salesforce jumped about 12% after hours on Q2 revenue of $11.35 billion (vs. $11.32 billion expected); CrowdStrike rose roughly 11.7% after revenue of $1.47 billion (vs. $1.44 billion expected, up 25.8% year-over-year) and a raised full-year guide to $5.99-$6.01 billion; and Okta surged about 19% on adjusted EPS of $1.05 (vs. $0.97 expected) and revenue of $805 million (vs. $795 million expected). All three came with margin expansion rather than margin compression — the opposite setup to Nvidia's print the same night, and a reminder that "AI theme" exposure is not monolithic right now.

### 3. Durable goods orders beat expectations, another data point against a hard landing
**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** July durable goods orders rose 1.1% versus a 0.4% consensus estimate, an acceleration from June's 0.3% gain, with orders excluding defense up 1.3%. It's a lagging, volatile series, but it adds to a picture of manufacturing demand holding up better than feared even with tariff uncertainty still unresolved.

### 4. Jackson Hole opens Thursday with Warsh's keynote looming Friday
**Source:** Kansas City Fed, multiple outlets
**So what:** The Fed's Jackson Hole Economic Symposium runs Thursday through Saturday, with Chair Kevin Warsh delivering his first keynote as chair Friday morning under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Markets are pricing roughly one-in-three odds of a September rate hike, so Warsh's framing of inflation risk — not just tonight's earnings — is likely to be the bigger swing factor for rates into next month's FOMC.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### CRWD — CrowdStrike
- **Thesis:** The Falcon platform keeps consolidating point-security tools onto a single cloud agent, and tonight's print showed that translating into real operating leverage — net new ARR up 32% year-over-year to a $5.51 billion ARR base, with non-GAAP operating margin expanding to 24% from 18% a year ago. This is a business scaling profitably, not just growing revenue.
- **Valuation note:** Even before tonight's roughly 12% pop, CrowdStrike traded at a premium multiple to most software peers on P/S and free-cash-flow-yield terms; this is a pay-up-for-quality holding, not a value screen name, and the after-hours gain makes that more true, not less.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The raised full-year guide ($5.99-$6.01 billion vs. roughly $5.94 billion consensus) is real evidence the AI-driven security-threat cycle is turning into wallet share, but a name this richly valued rewards patience on pullbacks over chasing tonight's move.
- **Risks / bear case:** Premium-multiple software names are exactly what gets hit hardest if rates re-accelerate or growth decelerates even modestly, and Microsoft's bundled security offerings remain the standing structural competitive risk to the standalone-platform thesis.
