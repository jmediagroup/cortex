## Top of mind

President Trump said over the weekend he'll "pretty soon" declare the Strait of Hormuz a U.S. territory once the war with Iran is over; Iran's deputy foreign minister called it "delusions" and vowed the blockade continues on Tehran's terms alone, and a fresh tanker strike kept the six-month standoff live into Monday. Brent held a better-than-5% weekly gain near $88 even as shipping through the strait fell to roughly six vessels a day from eleven — yet U.S. equity futures point modestly higher, with traders seemingly more focused on September rate-hike odds sliding to roughly a third and a loaded retail-earnings week (Home Depot reports Tuesday) than on the geopolitical headline risk.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Trump vows to declare the Strait of Hormuz "a territory of the United States"; Iran rejects the claim as ships keep getting hit
**Source:** Al Jazeera, NBC News, CNBC, Gulf News
**So what:** Trump said that once the U.S. "defeats" Iran he'll declare the strait U.S. territory; Iran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi called it "delusions" and said the strait "has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian." Separately, Iran and Oman are reportedly edging closer to a deal on managing strait access — a genuine binary outcome (deal vs. further escalation) that's the single biggest swing factor for oil and shipping risk this week, not something to fade in either direction.

### 2. July retail sales fall 0.6%, a much sharper miss than the +0.1% consensus
**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau (Commerce Department), Motley Fool, BigGo Finance
**So what:** The drop knocked the S&P off Thursday's record and dragged the Dow and Nasdaq lower Friday. Paired with a soft University of Michigan sentiment read, it's the clearest crack yet in the "resilient consumer" narrative that's underpinned the market's better-than-feared read on tariffs and rates all summer.

### 3. September rate-hike odds slide to roughly a third as the dollar extends its slump
**Source:** FXStreet, Mitrade
**So what:** CME-tracked odds of a 25bp September hike fell from around 55% a week ago into the mid-30s% after last week's flat PPI and Friday's retail-sales miss. DXY is pinned near the bottom of its tight 99.5-100 range. Cheaper dollar plus falling hike odds is typically a tailwind for risk assets and commodities, but the softness driving it (a weakening consumer) isn't a clean positive.

### 4. Home Depot reports Tuesday with CEO Ted Decker out on medical leave
**Source:** TIKR, Yahoo Finance, ad-hoc-news
**So what:** Consensus calls for fiscal Q2 EPS around $4.71-4.73 on roughly $47B in revenue, with Wall Street watching same-store sales and pro-customer demand more than the headline number. Decker's August 12 medical leave and a shared interim-leadership setup add uncertainty heading into a print that will also be the first hard read on whether Friday's retail-sales miss shows up in an actual retailer's results.

### 5. Oil majors and defense primes remain the structural beneficiaries of an unresolved war — but some names are cooling off their highs
**Source:** Forbes, Yahoo Finance, MacroTrends
**So what:** Exxon and Chevron posted their best quarterly profits in four and six years, respectively, on higher realized oil and gas prices. Lockheed Martin, despite a strong 2026, has pulled back to around $609 from a March record near $672 even as the war itself hasn't ended — a reminder that "war trade" positioning isn't a one-way bet, and headline escalation doesn't automatically mean new highs for every name in the theme.

### XOM — ExxonMobil
- **Thesis:** Record Q2 profit ($14.5B, its best in four years) on higher realized oil and gas prices, plus structurally growing Permian and Guyana volumes and an expanding LNG portfolio — a name whose earnings are directly levered to the Hormuz risk premium, unlike more speculative "war trade" stocks.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E near 13x, a premium to the ~11x oil & gas sector median but well inside Exxon's own historical range — the market isn't pricing this as a war-risk options trade.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Brent held a 5%+ weekly gain and Hormuz shipping traffic is down to roughly six vessels a day; if the standoff drags on, Q3 realized prices should stay elevated. But a lot of the recent oil strength is already in the tape, so this reads more as a core-position hold than a fresh-money entry point.
- **Risks / bear case:** Iran and Oman are reportedly nearing a deal on strait management — a real resolution would deflate the war-risk premium in oil quickly and pressure Exxon's near-term earnings comps. The position is also exposed to any US-brokered ceasefire that outright collapses crude prices.
