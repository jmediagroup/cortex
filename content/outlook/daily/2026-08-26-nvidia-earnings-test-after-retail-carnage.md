## Top of mind

Tuesday's tape read calm on the surface — the S&P 500 rose 0.3% to 7,677.28, the Nasdaq gained 0.7% to 26,151.30, and the Dow added 0.3% to 53,577.40, all within reach of record territory — but the internals told a rougher story. Dick's Sporting Goods posted its worst single-day loss on record (down roughly 31%) as its Foot Locker acquisition keeps bleeding, Intuit fell about 7% after-hours on soft fiscal 2027 growth guidance despite beating estimates, and Canada finalized 50% retaliatory tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods effective September 8. None of that will matter much once markets close today: Nvidia reports Q2 results after the bell with Wall Street pricing in roughly $92 billion in revenue, and the reaction will likely decide whether this year's AI trade gets another leg or its first real gut-check.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Nvidia reports Q2 after Wednesday's close — the week's defining catalyst
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, The Motley Fool, Kiplinger
**So what:** Consensus calls for roughly $92 billion in revenue (up about 97% year-over-year) and adjusted EPS near $2.09, both slightly above the company's own guidance of $91 billion. Nvidia is roughly 8% of the S&P 500's weight, so the market's reaction to guidance on hyperscaler capex — not just the headline beat — will set the tone for AI-adjacent stocks into September.

### 2. Dick's Sporting Goods crashes on Foot Locker drag, worst day on record
**Source:** CNBC, Forbes, The Motley Fool
**So what:** Core Dick's comparable sales actually grew 4.9%, but the Foot Locker business it acquired last year saw comps fall 3.6%, and bloated sneaker inventory plus heavy discounting forced full-year adjusted EPS guidance down to $11.00-$12.00 from $13.50-$14.50 three months ago. The stock's ~31% drop shows how much execution risk that acquisition still carries, and the footwear-market weakness dragged Nike and Lululemon shares lower in sympathy the same session.

### 3. Intuit beats on Q4 but guides fiscal 2027 growth down sharply
**Source:** Seeking Alpha, GuruFocus
**So what:** Intuit's fiscal Q4 adjusted EPS of $4.03 and revenue of roughly $4.35 billion both beat estimates, but fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of just 9%-10% growth — down from 14% in fiscal 2026 — knocked about $6.9 billion off its market value after hours. Management attributed part of the deceleration to a revised stock-based-compensation accounting method rather than deteriorating fundamentals, a claim worth testing against the next quarter's actual print rather than taking at face value.

### 4. Canada finalizes 50% retaliatory tariffs on $27.6 billion of US goods
**Source:** Washington Post, Insurance Journal
**So what:** Effective September 8, Canada will impose tariffs of 15%, 25%, and 50% across roughly 700 US products — steel, aluminum, furniture, and clothing hit hardest at 50%, with cheese and appliances at 25% and electronics/tools at 15% — in response to the US's own 50% duties on Canadian goods. Ottawa paired the move with a C$7.5 billion domestic support package, signaling this is a multi-month standoff rather than a one-off skirmish; companies with meaningful cross-border input costs face a real, still-unpriced margin risk once the tariffs land.

### 5. Moderna's cancer-vaccine rally cools but the stock trades well above formal price targets
**Source:** BioPharma Dive, Yahoo Finance, Forbes
**So what:** Moderna and Merck's personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine (intismeran autogene) met its primary Phase 3 endpoint, and even after round-tripping some of an initial 177% spike, the stock is still up sharply and trading near $159 — well above the ~$89-91 average analyst price target compiled before the trial news. Wolfe Research upgraded the stock to Peer Perform, but most formal models simply haven't caught up to the news yet, which cuts both ways: upgrades are likely still coming, but so is a real gap-down risk if any post-hoc data scrutiny disappoints.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### INTU — Intuit
- **Thesis:** TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma, and Mailchimp give Intuit a durable, high-margin franchise across consumer tax, small-business software, and fintech — a moat that a single soft guide doesn't undo, especially with Q4 beating on both revenue and EPS.
- **Valuation note:** The stock is already down roughly 47% from its 2026 peak per pre-earnings coverage, and the fresh 7% after-hours drop pushes the multiple lower still; whether that's now "cheap" depends on how much of the FY27 growth deceleration is real versus the accounting artifact management described.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This reads as a name to watch through the next print, not to average into blindly — the guide is fresh, unconfirmed as purely technical, and the stock hasn't stabilized yet. Patient entries on further weakness, once the SBC-accounting explanation can be checked against Q1 FY27 actuals, make more sense than buying the initial drop.
- **Risks / bear case:** If the FY27 deceleration to 9%-10% growth reflects real competitive or demand pressure rather than an accounting reclassification, the stock could re-rate lower for longer; software multiples broadly remain sensitive to any growth disappointment in this market.
