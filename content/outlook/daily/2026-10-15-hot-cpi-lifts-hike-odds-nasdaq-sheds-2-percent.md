## Top of mind

A hotter-than-expected September CPI (3.7% year over year versus about 3.6% expected, with gasoline up 9.1% on the month) sent the S&P 500 down 1.1% and the Nasdaq down 1.9% on Wednesday, October 14, the worst day for U.S. stocks since July. The 10-year yield spiked toward 5.3% before settling near 5.2%, and traders now price a real chance of a Fed hike at the late-October meeting. The index is about 1.6% below its record, but chips, small caps and AI-linked debt are doing the damage.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. September CPI beats on energy, core slightly hot
**Source:** BLS release via CNBC, Fox Business, The Hill
**So what:** Headline CPI rose 0.6% on the month and 3.7% over the year, up from 3.4% in August, with gasoline accounting for about half the increase. Core rose 0.3% on the month and 2.6% year over year, a touch above the 2.5% expected. Energy-driven inflation is hard for the Fed to look through when it keeps recurring, which is why the market reacted so sharply.

### 2. Fed hike odds jump
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, Schwab
**So what:** Accounts differ on the numbers: one has October-hike pricing rising from about 21% to a peak above 50% and settling near 37%; CNBC reported odds of a hike by year-end above 96%. Check CME FedWatch for current values. Higher-for-longer pressure lands hardest on long-duration growth stocks and small caps.

### 3. Big banks: mixed prints, cautious tone
**Source:** CNBC, Fortune, Reuters/LSEG-based reports
**So what:** JPMorgan missed on EPS ($5.57 vs. about $5.93) on weaker fixed-income trading, while Goldman Sachs beat on equities and record advisory revenue and Citigroup beat. On Wednesday, Bank of America fell on a revenue miss and a $1.2 billion penalty tied to compliance failures, Morgan Stanley beat but slid on weaker fixed-income trading, and BlackRock rose on strong results. Banks flagged sticky inflation and higher rates as risks.

### 4. AI-debt concerns widen the tech selloff
**Source:** AP, CNBC
**So what:** Worries about companies outside the largest tech names borrowing heavily to fund AI buildouts joined the chip weakness. With yields above 5%, financing that looked easy at lower rates gets more expensive.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### BAC — Post-penalty reaction
- **Catalyst:** Shares fell roughly 3-5% (sources differ) after a Q3 revenue miss and a $1.2 billion penalty; reported EPS of $0.91 compares with about $1.08 excluding the penalty per one report.
- **Time horizon:** Days to weeks.
- **What would invalidate:** Evidence that the shortfall is operating weakness rather than a one-time charge, or further regulatory action.
- **Risk note:** Bank stocks trade on rates and credit as much as on one quarter; a hike or credit-loss surprise can override the earnings story. Research prompt, not a recommendation.

### MS — Beat, but trading revenue disappointed
- **Catalyst:** EPS of $3.03 and revenue of $20.34 billion topped LSEG expectations, yet shares ended roughly 2.75% lower on weaker fixed-income trading.
- **Time horizon:** Through the rest of earnings season.
- **What would invalidate:** Peers confirming a broad slowdown in fixed-income trading, or a deteriorating deal pipeline.
- **Risk note:** A market that sells a beat is telling you expectations were high. Keep size small and define the exit first.
