## Top of mind

Iran-backed Houthis fired missiles and drones at Riyadh and Saudi Aramco's Yanbu export hub over the weekend — the first air-raid alert in the Saudi capital since the spring US-Iran fighting, with a fuel depot burning near King Khalid International Airport. Yet oil is trading lower Monday morning (WTI ~$99.41, Brent ~$103.06) as Aramco reroutes exports through the Strait of Hormuz, and US equity futures are up (S&P +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.7%) ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit in Washington. That's a market pricing this as a contained supply story rather than a chokepoint crisis — a read worth watching closely if Yemen-Saudi hostilities escalate again before the summit.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Houthis strike Riyadh and Aramco's Yanbu hub; oil falls anyway
**Source:** Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, Reuters (via Investing.com)
**So what:** Saudi air defenses intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at the capital while a fuel tank fire burned near King Khalid airport — the first such alert in Riyadh since the spring war. The market reaction (oil down, not up) suggests traders are betting Aramco's rerouting through Hormuz holds; if it doesn't, this is a supply story that hasn't been priced in at all.

### 2. US and China open trade talks in New York ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** Treasury Secretary Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng are laying groundwork for Thursday's Washington meeting between Trump and Xi, centered on extending a trade truce due to expire in November, plus AI, critical minerals and Taiwan. This is likely the single biggest scheduled catalyst of the week — bigger than any one data print — and it's why futures are shrugging off the weekend's Gulf headlines.

### 3. Wall Street closes a hawkish Fed week mixed on a $7 trillion triple-witching day

The stock fell more than 3% Friday on a name that's typically treated as a durable long-term holding — a reminder that "quality compounder" theses still need fresh engagement data, not just subscriber counts.

### 5. A heavy Fedspeak week follows Wednesday's hike

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### AVGO — Broadcom
- **Thesis:** Broadcom's custom AI silicon and networking business is the clearest evidence that hyperscaler AI capex hasn't slowed — fiscal Q3 revenue hit $29.59B (+85.5% YoY), beating estimates, and management guided to roughly $56B of AI revenue for 2026. Friday's action reinforced the pattern: Broadcom was one of two sectors' worth of gains in an otherwise red session.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E estimates range roughly 19x-24x depending on source (GuruFocus puts it ~16% below the semiconductor industry median), with a consensus "Strong Buy" and an average analyst target near $532 against a recent price around $350 — a wide enough gap that it says as much about Street optimism as it does about the stock being cheap.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a name to build on weakness, not chase Friday's strength — the AI-infrastructure decoupling-from-rates story has now survived two central bank hikes in one week (Fed and Bank of Japan) without cracking.
- **Risks / bear case:** 85%+ revenue growth this concentrated in AI hyperscaler capex is a bet on a handful of customers (custom silicon programs, networking gear) continuing to spend at an extraordinary pace. A capex pause, an in-sourcing decision by any major customer, or a broader AI-spending air pocket would hit Broadcom disproportionately, and the 48%-ish gap to the average price target could just as easily mean the Street hasn't caught down to a slower-capex world as it means the stock is undervalued.

Size this as a small tactical add on a specific news catalyst, not a fresh long-term defense thesis.

That verdict can flip fast on a single follow-up headline; don't treat "oil didn't move" as a permanent signal rather than a snapshot that needs re-checking through the week.
