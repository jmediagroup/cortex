## Top of mind

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired multiple ballistic missiles at US forces in the Middle East overnight — US Central Command says all were intercepted, and the US and Saudi Arabia responded with joint strikes on Iran-aligned targets in Iraq. The attack ends a brief lull in the conflict and sent WTI crude up nearly 4% to around $82, unwinding two days of decline in a single move. That would be the lead story on any normal day; instead it's landing hours before the Fed's 2pm ET rate decision and tonight's earnings from Microsoft and Meta, stacking a geopolitical shock, a (now near-certain) policy decision, and two mega-cap prints into one session.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Iran fires ballistic missiles at US forces; all intercepted; US and Saudi Arabia strike back in Iraq
**Source:** CNN, CNBC, The Hill
**So what:** The attack breaks a short pause in the conflict that began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran back in February. CENTCOM says every missile was intercepted, and the US response — joint strikes with Saudi Arabia on Iran-aligned targets in Iraq — is notable because Saudi Arabia has otherwise avoided being pulled directly into the fighting. The direct market read is oil: WTI jumped nearly 4% on the news, reviving the inflation-and-energy-supply risk that had been fading over the past two sessions.

### 2. Fed decision due 2pm ET; hold odds have converged hard to near-certain
**Source:** CME FedWatch via multiple outlets, Federal Reserve
**So what:** As recently as a week ago, priced hold odds sat around 62-68%, with a meaningful minority pricing a surprise hike. By Tuesday, CME FedWatch had that hold probability up at roughly 97%. The FOMC is expected to leave the target range at 3.50%-3.75% for a fifth straight meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh, but the overnight oil spike is a fresh, last-minute variable — not enough to move today's decision, but worth watching in the press conference for any comment on energy-driven inflation risk into the September meeting, where nine of eighteen officials reportedly lean toward a hike.

### 3. Microsoft and Meta report after today's close; Apple and Amazon follow Thursday
**Source:** TipRanks, company guidance, consensus estimates
**So what:** Microsoft is expected to post adjusted EPS around $4.24 (up ~16% year-over-year) on revenue near $87.6 billion, with Azure growth and AI-linked capital spending the focus. Meta's consensus sits near $7.18-$7.24 EPS on revenue up roughly 27% to about $60.2 billion, with investors watching whether ad growth (up 33% last quarter) can justify capex guided to $125-145 billion for 2026. Both prints extend this week's core question — whether AI infrastructure spending is being matched by real returns — that first flared Monday on the Nvidia-OpenAI financing report.

### 4. Procter & Gamble reports before the open; a read on the consumer
**Source:** Company guidance, TipRanks, Alphastreet
**So what:** P&G reports fiscal Q4 results at 8:30am ET, with consensus EPS estimates clustered near $1.41-$1.56 and revenue around $21.4 billion. Full-year guidance has pointed to 1-5% sales growth. As a bellwether staples name, the print is a useful, lower-drama cross-check on consumer demand the same morning oil and geopolitics are dominating headlines.

### 5. Tuesday's earnings winners and losers set the tone for today
**Source:** CNBC, TheStreet
**So what:** Sherwin-Williams (+8.5%) and Coca-Cola (+5%) both beat and raised guidance, reinforcing the rotation into quality/defensive names. Corning fell nearly 20% on soft revenue guidance and signs wireless carriers are pulling back capex — a small but real data point that not every capex story this earnings season is one-way bullish.

### XLE — energy sector, geopolitical risk premium
- **Catalyst:** Wednesday's ~4% WTI spike on the Iran missile interception reintroduces a geopolitical risk premium into oil and, by extension, energy equities, after two sessions of sharp declines on ceasefire-hold optimism.
- **Time horizon:** Days — this is a headline-reactive trade, not a structural energy call.
- **What would invalidate:** Any de-escalation signal — and this conflict has already reversed direction more than once this year — would likely give back today's oil gain quickly, as it did earlier this week.
- **Risk note:** Whipsaw risk is high; this setup has failed to hold in prior instances this year when pauses re-emerged within days. Use a basket (XLE) rather than a single name to reduce idiosyncratic exposure, and size small.
