## Top of mind

South Korea's Kospi opened down more than 4%, at one point sliding close to 5%, deepening a slide that's now taken the index roughly 25% below its June peak and erased about $1 trillion in value — the AI-stock unwind that started in US chipmakers three weeks ago has gone fully global, with Samsung and SK Hynix both opening down more than 5%. Yet US futures were only modestly higher to flat overnight, shrugging off Asia's capitulation even as oil pushed toward $90 a barrel on a ninth consecutive night of US strikes against Iran. That disconnect — Asia panicking, Wall Street calm — is this week's central question, and Wednesday's Alphabet and Tesla earnings are the first real test of which market has it right.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Kospi enters a technical bear market as the AI-stock unwind goes global
**Source:** Bloomberg; US News; Fortune
**So what:** South Korea's Kospi has fallen roughly 25% from its June peak, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each down 30-40% from their highs — both opened down more than 5% Monday before paring losses. Korea's chip-heavy index has effectively become a pre-market proxy for US AI/semiconductor risk; a nearly $1 trillion wipeout there is the clearest signal yet that the "AI capex is unsustainable" worry has moved from a US-tech story to a global one.

### 2. Iran war enters a ninth night as American casualties mount and oil approaches $90
**Source:** CNN; CNBC; NPR
**So what:** The US has struck Iran for a ninth consecutive night, with CENTCOM confirming 16 US service members killed and more than 430 wounded since the conflict began — including two killed and one missing after an Iranian attack in Jordan announced Saturday. Hormuz shipping traffic remains severely disrupted, and Brent has risen more than 14% over the past week alone. This is now a live, escalating input to markets, not a background risk.

### 3. TSMC's $100 billion US pledge cushions Taiwan even as Korea craters
**Source:** Yahoo Finance; Fortune; ABC News
**So what:** TSMC raised its 2026 revenue outlook, lifted capex guidance to $60-64 billion, and committed another $100 billion to US fabs (bringing its total US pledge to $265 billion) — news that helped Taiwan's Taiex hold roughly flat Monday (TSMC itself up 2%) even as Korea's memory-chip names got crushed. The divergence suggests investors are distinguishing between AI's logic/foundry leader and the more commoditized memory suppliers, rather than treating all "AI stocks" the same.

### 4. Alphabet and Tesla headline a pivotal earnings week for the AI trade
**Source:** CNBC; Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Alphabet and Tesla both report Wednesday after the close, with Intel following Thursday. Alphabet's Cloud growth rate and its 2027 capex guidance are the single clearest read this week on whether hyperscaler AI spending is decelerating (the fear driving Korea's selloff) or still accelerating — arguably more important to the broader market right now than Tesla's own numbers.

### 5. Fed enters blackout ahead of the July 28-29 decision
**Source:** Federal Reserve; CME FedWatch (via Forbes, FedRateCalc)
**So what:** With no Fed speakers this week ahead of the blackout period, and CME FedWatch pricing roughly 89% odds of a hold at 3.50%-3.75%, monetary policy is a non-factor for the next several sessions — leaving earnings and the Iran war as the dominant market drivers until July 29.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### MU — Micron Technology
- **Thesis:** Micron has quietly become one of the more indispensable names in the AI buildout — its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) product line is reported sold out for all of calendar 2026, fiscal Q3 revenue more than quadrupled year-over-year to $41.46 billion, and Q4 guidance points to $50 billion. Unlike pure-play AI software or chip-design names, Micron sits at a physical capacity bottleneck (memory fabs take years to build), giving it real pricing power in the current cycle.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E sits around 5.5-6x — roughly 80% below the semiconductor industry median near 32x — a gap that reflects the market pricing Micron as a boom-bust cyclical rather than crediting the structural HBM demand shift. Consensus analyst price targets cluster well above the current price.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Micron has been swept into the same selloff dragging down Samsung and SK Hynix, even though its own order book and guidance haven't changed. That's the kind of price/fundamentals gap long-term investors watch for — but memory is genuinely cyclical, so this is a name to build into patiently, not chase into a single down week.
- **Risks / bear case:** Memory pricing has a long history of boom-bust cycles, and a real slowdown in AI capex commitments — the exact fear driving this week's Kospi rout — would hit Micron's HBM backlog and margins directly. A cheap-looking forward multiple can also be a warning sign right before a cyclical peak, not just an opportunity.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### GOOGL — Wednesday's earnings as the AI-capex referendum
- **Catalyst:** Alphabet reports Q2 results Wednesday, July 22 after the close. Consensus is looking for accelerating Cloud growth and an updated 2027 capex figure — arguably the single clearest data point this week on whether hyperscaler AI spending is decelerating (Korea's fear) or still accelerating (the case for Wall Street's calm).
- **Time horizon:** Days — the immediate reaction Wednesday night into Thursday, with Intel's Thursday print adding a second data point on the semiconductor side.
- **What would invalidate:** Decelerating Cloud growth or cautious 2027 capex commentary would validate this week's Asia-led selloff and likely pressure the broader AI trade further; an accelerating number with a confident capex guide would support the read that Korea overreacted.
- **Risk note:** Single-print, binary-feeling reaction — Alphabet has moved sharply on capex commentary multiple times this year. Size for a gap in either direction.

### XOM — Hormuz risk premium, not a fundamentals trade
- **Catalyst:** Brent is up more than 14% over the past week as the Strait of Hormuz conflict escalates — a ninth straight night of US strikes, mounting American casualties, and severely disrupted tanker traffic are being priced into crude in real time.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a couple of weeks, tracked against war headlines rather than a scheduled event.
- **What would invalidate:** Any credible ceasefire or de-escalation signal would likely unwind the risk premium quickly — oil has given back gains fast after past lulls in this same conflict, and a name that's rallied on the premium would give it back too.
- **Risk note:** This is a geopolitical-headline trade, not an earnings or fundamentals trade. News flow drives it, and reversals can be sharp with little warning.
