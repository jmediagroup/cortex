## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Alphabet reports 63% cloud growth, backlog nearly doubles to $460B
**Source:** 9to5Google, Yahoo Finance (April 29–30, 2026)
**So what:** Alphabet's Q1 beat was comprehensive — revenue $109.9B (+22%), EPS $5.11 vs. $2.62 estimated, Google Cloud $20.02B (+63%), with enterprise AI solutions driving cloud for the first time as its primary growth catalyst (gen AI product revenue +~800% YoY). The backlog nearly doubling to over $460B quarter-on-quarter is the most significant forward-revenue signal any hyperscaler has produced this cycle. The bear case matters: $460B in backlog commits future capex without guaranteed margin, and cloud contracts convert to cash slowly. But the directional signal — that enterprise AI has moved from experiment to budget line — is now difficult to dispute.

### 2. Meta falls ~7% on $145B capex raise and user-growth miss
**Source:** CNBC, Invezz, Fortune, SiliconAngle (April 29–30, 2026)
**So what:** Meta beat on revenue ($56.3B, +33%) and net income ($26.8B, +61%), but raised 2026 capex guidance to $125–145B from $115–135B, and daily active people (DAP) of 3.56B missed the 3.62B estimate — the company cited "internet disruptions in Iran" for the shortfall. The selloff is rational: investors are not penalizing Meta's current fundamentals, they are repricing the risk that AI infrastructure spend has no visible ceiling and ROI evidence remains confined to advertising yield data. That is a different risk profile than Alphabet's cloud revenue backlog or Amazon's AWS growth.

### 3. FOMC holds 3.5%–3.75% with unprecedented four-member dissent
**Source:** CNBC, Federal Reserve press release (April 29–30, 2026)
**So what:** The 8-4 vote is the first four-member dissent since October 1992. Three of the four dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) didn't disagree with the hold — they wanted the statement's easing bias removed, explicitly signaling the next move could be a hike. With Kevin Warsh replacing Powell on May 15, and three senior hawks on record for a more aggressive framing, the Fed's internal consensus has broken from the "patient, cut-leaning" framework that has supported equity multiples since early 2025. The June 17 meeting is now a live event in both directions.

### 4. Q1 GDP advance estimate +2.3%, March PCE at 2.6% headline — both better than feared
**Source:** BEA advance estimate; multiple pre-release forecasters confirmed publication April 30, 2026
**So what:** Q1 GDP at +2.3% annualized substantially beat the Atlanta Fed's 1.2% GDPNow tracker. March PCE came in at 2.6% headline, 2.8% core (single-source figure from the BEA release; not yet cross-confirmed) — not the 4%+ stagflation scenario the prior session flagged as the bear case. Business investment drove the GDP beat; consumer spending softened, which is worth watching into Q2. These two numbers together remove the near-term stagflation scare — but do not resolve it: oil at $107–110 is a Q2 PCE input, not a Q1 one.

### 5. Amazon AWS +28%, trailing FCF collapses to $1.2B on AI capex surge
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC (April 29–30, 2026)
**So what:** Amazon's headline beat was strong — $181.5B revenue (+17%), net income nearly doubled to $30.3B, AWS at $37.6B (+28%). But trailing-twelve-month free cash flow fell to $1.2B from $25.9B a year ago as capital expenditures surged into AI infrastructure. Amazon's FCF compression is a different flavor of the Meta capex story: the business is deliberately compressing current-period cash returns to fund AI capacity. Unlike Meta, Amazon's AWS revenue is directly and measurably linked to the infrastructure investment. The FCF recovery timeline — not the revenue growth — is the underappreciated variable for AMZN's multiple.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### GOOGL — Alphabet
- **Thesis:** Alphabet's Q1 2026 results resolve the "is AI commercial?" debate for this company. Google Cloud growing 63% with enterprise AI as the primary growth driver for the first time — not just a contributor — is a structural shift in Alphabet's revenue mix. A backlog of $460B nearly doubling quarter-on-quarter is the strongest forward-revenue signal in the hyperscaler group. Search advertising at $77.2B+ and YouTube subscriptions at 350M paying members provide a durable floor beneath the growth story.
- **Valuation note:** Q1 EPS of $5.11 vs. $2.62 estimated represents a material earnings re-rate. Cloud alone is approaching an $80B annual run rate at 63% growth — a segment that barely registered five years ago. The forward multiple math has shifted significantly from consensus entering this week.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The proof is freshest right now. Patient holders capture both the AI cloud re-rating and the advertising base as a floor. Any multiple compression from near-term macro uncertainty (hawks, oil, consumer softness) is a long-term accumulation context, not a thesis break.
- **Risks / bear case:** The $460B backlog is a forward commitment, not revenue — conversion timing is uncertain and cloud margins at scale are not yet demonstrated. Search faces structural disruption risk from AI-native query tools that could erode the $77B+ advertising moat over 3–5 years. EU AI Act compliance costs are unquantified. And the capex required to sustain 63% cloud growth is enormous — any slowdown in bookings compresses the multiple sharply.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMZN — AWS re-acceleration into Q2 earnings
- **Catalyst:** AWS at $37.6B (+28%) beat cleanly. If Q2 AWS growth holds above 25% and FCF begins recovering as the capex cycle matures, AMZN could see meaningful multiple re-expansion. The FCF collapse to $1.2B is a transient capex timing effect if AWS revenue growth continues to compound — not a structural impairment.
- **Time horizon:** Through Q2 2026 earnings (July).
- **What would invalidate:** AWS growth below 20%, FCF remaining near zero with no management guidance for normalization, or a broad macro deterioration from oil/Iran-driven inflation that compresses enterprise IT budgets. Any of these conditions would eliminate the re-rating catalyst.
- **Risk note:** The market is already pricing strong execution — no hidden value here. This is a momentum-and-confidence trade on AI cloud dominance. Any execution miss, guidance miss, or macro rotation amplifies the downside materially. Size it with the understanding that today's bid is partly post-earnings euphoria.

## Portfolio-level guidance

*Allocation and risk observations. Not specific buy/sell calls — those depend on a full picture this report doesn't see.*

- **Meta's capex signal is not idiosyncratic.** The market is treating the $145B capex raise as Meta-specific. The variant view: it's a leading indicator for all hyperscalers. Amazon FCF is near zero, Alphabet's backlog commits future capex, and Microsoft's AI run rate is accelerating. Every hyperscaler is compressing near-term cash returns for AI infrastructure. The difference is that some (GOOGL, AMZN) have cloud revenue directly attributable to the investment; Meta's ROI is visible only in advertising yield. One of those narratives is structurally more fragile under investor pressure.
- **FOMC four-dissenter scenario deserves more weight than markets gave it.** The 8-4 vote was absorbed as noise. The structural implication: Warsh inherits a hawkish internal base already on record. His first FOMC meeting framing is pre-set by three dissenters. If June's PCE data is elevated by oil price pass-through, Warsh's inaugural meeting could produce the first credible "hike risk" signal in over a year. That is not currently priced in duration or equity multiples.
- **GDP and PCE removed the near-term stagflation tail, not the medium-term one.** The 2.3% GDP beat and 2.6% PCE print is the best-case scenario for today's macro read. But it is Q1 data — oil at $107–110 is a Q2 input. Consumer spending already softened in Q1. The medium-term stagflation risk is deferred, not dismissed.

Key questions: China manufacturing tariff exposure, AI iPhone upgrade cycle traction, and whether supply chain commentary addresses Iran/oil logistics costs.
