## Top of mind

Asia tech opened the week under pressure: Alibaba fell as much as 10% in Hong Kong after pricing a $10.2 billion share placement to fund AI infrastructure, and Samsung Electronics dropped 8.35% after investors judged its up-to-$80 billion shareholder-return plan insufficient — even as SK Hynix, which announced its own $28.6 billion buyback days earlier, rose 2.4% in the same session. The Kospi fell 3% and US futures were only modestly lower (Dow -0.03%, S&P 500 -0.1%, Nasdaq -100 futures -0.3%) heading into the open, meaning the damage is concentrated in specific names rather than a broad risk-off move. The obvious read is dilution aversion — investors punishing a capital raise that dilutes existing holders by roughly 3.7-3.8%; the less obvious one is that the market is now actively discriminating between how AI capex gets funded (equity dilution vs. buybacks funded by AI-driven cash flow), a distinction that will matter a great deal once Nvidia and Salesforce report Wednesday and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote Friday.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Alibaba shares sink up to 10% in Hong Kong after $10.2 billion AI share placement
**Source:** Reuters (via Investing.com), Yahoo Finance, ts2.tech
**So what:** Alibaba priced its first share placement since its 2019 Hong Kong listing — 710 million new shares at HK$112.70, raising HK$80 billion ($10.2 billion), with 100% of proceeds earmarked for AI infrastructure. The offering was oversubscribed within an hour and drew roughly $28 billion in demand from sovereign wealth funds and long-term investors, but the stock still opened down about 8% and touched a near-10% intraday decline as existing holders priced in ~3.7-3.8% dilution. The tension is real: strong institutional demand for the *capital raise* doesn't mean the market likes what dilution does to existing shareholders' claim on Alibaba Cloud, which grew revenue 45% year-over-year with AI product revenue up triple digits for a 12th straight quarter.

### 2. Samsung falls 8.35% as its up-to-$80 billion shareholder-return plan disappoints
**Source:** CNBC, TradingKey, Sunday Guardian (KRX data)
**So what:** Samsung outlined plans to return 90-110 trillion won (roughly $65-80 billion) to shareholders in 2026, funded by record AI-memory profits — days after SK Hynix announced its own 40 trillion won ($28.6 billion) buyback. Instead of rallying on the headline size, Samsung fell 8.35% as investors apparently wanted more, or a different mix (buyback vs. dividend), while SK Hynix rose 2.4% in the same session. The Kospi dropped 3.03% on the divergence. This is a useful tell: after a run where "AI memory boom" was priced almost uniformly across Korean chipmakers, the market is starting to price the two companies' capital-return credibility separately.

### 3. Nvidia and Salesforce headline the summer's busiest earnings week, both reporting Wednesday
**Source:** TipRanks, AskTraders, Kiplinger
**So what:** Nvidia and Salesforce both report Wednesday, August 26, with consensus looking for Nvidia revenue in the $92-95 billion range on continued Data Center and Blackwell-platform strength. Coming one day after the Alibaba/Samsung capital-structure story, Nvidia's own commentary on customer capex sustainability (hyperscaler spending plans, Blackwell demand) will be read as the read-through for whether this week's Asia dilution/buyback story is idiosyncratic or an early tell on AI capex funding stress more broadly.

### 4. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote Friday
**Source:** MNI Markets, Newsquawk, TechTimes
**So what:** The Kansas City Fed's Jackson Hole Economic Symposium runs August 27-29 under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Warsh speaks at 10:00 a.m. ET Friday, August 28 — his first Jackson Hole appearance since taking the Fed chair in May and landing just 19 days before the September 16 FOMC decision. With the 10-year yield sitting near 20-month highs at 4.74%, markets will parse the speech closely for any signal on how Warsh's Fed weighs sticky yields against the AI-capex-driven growth story.

### NVDA — Nvidia
- **Thesis:** Nvidia remains the clearest infrastructure toll-taker in the AI buildout — Data Center revenue and the Blackwell ramp are the demand signal every hyperscaler capex plan (and, increasingly, every memory-chip buyback or dilution decision in Asia) ultimately traces back to.
- **Valuation note:** Wall Street consensus centers on $92-95 billion in quarterly revenue for Wednesday's print; valuation should be judged against the growth rate actually delivered and guided, not the headline beat/miss alone — Walmart's August reaction was a fresh reminder that a beat with decelerating underlying growth can still sell off.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a "wait for the print" setup, not an entry call — Wednesday's report and guidance will reset the multiple either direction, and buying ahead of a binary event is a different risk profile than buying the long-term thesis on a normal day.
- **Risks / bear case:** Nvidia's results are now a proxy for the entire AI capex cycle; a guidance disappointment doesn't just hit NVDA, it validates the capex-funding-stress read from this week's Alibaba/Samsung moves and could compress multiples across the group. Customer concentration (a handful of hyperscalers) remains a structural risk.

### BABA — Alibaba, post-dilution stabilization watch
- **Catalyst:** Monday's up-to-10% Hong Kong decline on a $10.2 billion AI-infrastructure share placement that was oversubscribed by institutional demand (~$28B) even as existing holders priced in the dilution — a setup where the capital-raise fundamentals (funding a 45%-YoY-growing cloud business) and the near-term price action are pointing in different directions.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a couple of weeks, watching whether the stock stabilizes once the new shares are absorbed or continues lower as more sell-side notes digest the dilution math.
- **What would invalidate:** A break meaningfully below Monday's low without signs of institutional buying support would argue the market is pricing more than just the mechanical dilution — e.g., renewed concern about China tech regulatory risk or AI-capex ROI skepticism.
- **Risk note:** This is a China ADR/H-share exposure with the usual variant-perception, regulatory, and currency risks layered on top of the dilution story — size accordingly, and don't treat institutional oversubscription on the placement as a signal about near-term share-price direction.
