## Top of mind

Regional mediators (Qatar, Egypt, Pakistan) have floated a 10-day ceasefire to halt the US-Iran war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz — and even though neither side has accepted it, the mere prospect pulled oil back from near $90 over the weekend toward the low $80s and sent Asian equities sharply higher overnight, led by chipmakers. That's a hopeful signal, but it's a proposal, not a deal — both sides are reportedly still trying to improve their leverage with further strikes first. Wednesday's Alphabet and Tesla earnings, the first Magnificent Seven prints of the season, are this week's other pivot point.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Mediators push a 10-day Iran ceasefire proposal; oil slides off its highs
**Source:** Axios
**So what:** Qatar, Egypt, Pakistan and other regional mediators have presented Washington and Tehran with a proposal to halt fighting and reopen both Hormuz shipping lanes for 10 days, giving room to salvage the collapsed memorandum of understanding. Neither side has accepted it yet, and both appear to be pressing for more leverage through additional strikes first — so this is a real de-escalation signal, but an unconfirmed one. Oil's slide from near $90 toward $82-83 is pricing in real odds it holds; a rejection or a fresh attack on shipping would likely send it right back.

### 2. Asian markets rally as the chip-stock bounce goes global ahead of earnings
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** The MSCI Asia Pacific Index climbed 1.7% overnight, with South Korea's Kospi and Taiwan's Taiex both up more than 2.5% and Japan's Nikkei up 2.2%, led by Samsung, SK Hynix, and TSMC. That's the mirror image of last week's Kospi bear-market rout — a signal that at least part of the AI-capex-fear selloff was overdone, though Wednesday's Alphabet print is the real test of whether the bounce has fundamentals behind it.

### 3. US chipmakers rebound Monday, but Apple and Tesla keep the broader market in the red
**Source:** Bloomberg
**So what:** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 2.5% after entering bear-market territory Friday, with Micron up as much as 5.7%, AMD up 4%, and Nvidia and Intel both higher. But the S&P 500 erased its early gains to close lower as declines in Apple and Tesla — both large index weights — offset the chip-sector strength, a reminder that "AI trade" and "big tech" aren't moving as one block right now.

### 4. Rosenblatt names AMD a "top pick," lifts price target to $665 from $490
**Source:** TipRanks
**So what:** Rosenblatt's Kevin Cassidy raised his AMD target more than 35% ahead of the company's August 4 earnings, citing EPYC server-CPU share gains (he's modeling 70%+ year-over-year growth) and rival delays at Intel. It's a single analyst call, not a consensus shift, but it's part of the same narrative driving Monday's chip rebound — that the AI-capex fear hit sentiment harder than actual order books.

### 5. Alphabet and Tesla headline a pivotal earnings week; Fed enters blackout
**Source:** CNBC; CME FedWatch (via Octagon, Polymarket)
**So what:** Alphabet and Tesla both report Wednesday after the close — the first Magnificent Seven prints of the season, with Intel following Thursday. Alphabet's Cloud growth rate and updated capex guidance (current 2026 guide: $180-190 billion) are the clearest read yet on whether hyperscaler AI spending is decelerating or still accelerating. Separately, the Fed has entered its blackout period ahead of the July 28-29 meeting, where futures price roughly 84% odds of a hold at 3.50%-3.75% — rates are a non-factor for markets until then.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor
- **Thesis:** TSMC is the foundry every major AI chip designer — Nvidia, AMD, Apple, and others — ultimately depends on, which gives it exposure to the AI buildout without the single-customer concentration risk of a pure memory or GPU name. The company guided full-year 2026 revenue growth of "slightly more than 40%," up from an earlier 30%+ forecast, and raised 2026 capex to $60-64 billion while committing an additional $100 billion to US fabs (bringing its total US pledge to $265 billion).
- **Valuation note:** Trading around 20x forward earnings — a discount to the broader semiconductor sector's AI-driven multiples, and arguably cheap against 40%+ guided growth for a company with structural pricing power at the leading edge.
- **Why now (or why patient):** TSMC's stock actually fell after its July 16 earnings beat and raised guidance, and it got swept into the broader Kospi-led Asia selloff last week despite its own numbers improving — that gap between fundamentals and price is exactly the kind of setup long-term investors watch for, though the stock's own rebound (part of Tuesday's Asia rally) means some of that gap has already started closing.
- **Risks / bear case:** Geopolitical risk around Taiwan is structural and can't be diversified away by fundamentals. A real slowdown in hyperscaler AI capex — which this week's Alphabet print will start to answer — would hit TSMC's growth guidance directly, and its own capex ramp pressures near-term free cash flow even as revenue grows.

## Ideas — opportunistic

### GOOGL — Wednesday's Cloud print as the AI-capex referendum
- **Catalyst:** Alphabet reports Q2 results Wednesday, July 22 after the close. Consensus is looking for continued acceleration in Google Cloud growth (63% year-over-year in Q1, with backlog nearly doubling to $462 billion) and confirmation of the $180-190 billion 2026 capex guide.
- **Time horizon:** Days — the immediate reaction Wednesday night into Thursday, with Intel's Thursday print adding a second data point.
- **What would invalidate:** Decelerating Cloud growth or cautious capex commentary would validate last week's Asia-led AI selloff and could pressure the broader trade again; an accelerating number with confident capex guidance supports the case that Korea overreacted.
- **Risk note:** Single-print, binary-feeling reaction — Alphabet has moved sharply on capex commentary multiple times this year. Size for a gap in either direction.

### TSLA — robotaxi update and a Bitcoin wildcard, same night as Alphabet
- **Catalyst:** Tesla also reports Wednesday after the close; consensus is $0.52 EPS (+30% year-over-year) on ~$25.99 billion revenue (+15%). Beyond the headline numbers, investors will press for robotaxi expansion updates and an update on Tesla's roughly 11,509 BTC holding, which carried an unrealized loss in the $170-220 million range last quarter.
- **Time horizon:** Days — the Wednesday-night reaction, though TSLA's volatility means moves can extend for sessions afterward.
- **What would invalidate:** A weak delivery/margin update or a vague robotaxi timeline would extend TSLA's recent underperformance (it was one of Monday's two biggest S&P point decliners); a concrete robotaxi expansion or FSD milestone could spark a sharp reversal given how beaten-down sentiment already is.
- **Risk note:** High-volatility, headline- and social-media-driven name with an unusual crypto-marks wildcard layered onto the print. Size conservatively into a binary event.
