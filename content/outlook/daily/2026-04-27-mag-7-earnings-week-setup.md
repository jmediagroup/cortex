## Lead

Five of the Magnificent Seven report Wednesday and Thursday. Together they're roughly a third of the S&P 500 by market cap, and the tape today doesn't tell you any of that — the S&P slipped 0.2%, the Dow fell 176 points, the Nasdaq finished down 0.3%. But the *internals* tell a different story. NVIDIA closed up 4.45% to $208.43 on reporting around sustained next-gen chip demand; Verizon ran 3.5% on a raised 2026 outlook. Meanwhile the bond market is sending a third message altogether: the 10-year yield rose for a fifth straight session to about 4.33%, with stalled Iran peace talks and Strait of Hormuz disruptions keeping a risk premium in long rates. Three different tapes, one trading day. Pick which one you trust before Wednesday.

## What we're watching

- **Mag 7 earnings concentration.** MSFT, META, AMZN, and GOOGL report Wednesday; AAPL on Thursday. Consensus has Microsoft at roughly $4.04 EPS (+17% YoY) on $81.4B revenue, with Azure tracking ~38% growth — the AI-capex monetization read everyone is waiting on. Meta is set up at $7.51 EPS on $55.5B (revenue +31% YoY); Alphabet at $2.64 on $92.2B (revenue +20.6%, EPS *down* 6.1% as investments hit). Apple is expected to deliver ~20% earnings growth. Five binary catalysts in 72 hours, on the names that drove most of the index's 2026 advance.
- **Bond market is not in the equity story.** 10Y at 4.33%, up five sessions, while equity vol (VIX 19.03) sits well below its trailing-month average of 21.68. Either rates are wrong about an Iran-driven inflation impulse, or stocks are about to find out about it. It's hard to be bullish duration here even on the dip.
- **Single-name dispersion is back.** Domino's down 10.5% on a Q1 miss. Veradermics (MANE) up roughly 45% on a Phase 2/3 minoxidil trial. POET Technologies cut nearly in half after Celestial AI — now part of Marvell — cancelled all of its outstanding purchase orders. Tapes where individual catalysts move stocks 10–50% in a session are tapes where stock-picking is paying again, even when the index isn't.
- **Energy still bid, but quietly.** Goldman upgraded Williams (WMB) to Buy with an $82 target; BNP Paribas turned constructive on Exxon (XOM), lifting it to Neutral with a target of $165. Brent has stayed firm on Hormuz headlines and Energy held leadership through last week. The cleaner expression remains midstream over upstream — same demand, less commodity beta.

## The decision worth making today

**NVIDIA (NVDA)** as a setup — not a position call. The stock closed up 4.45% to $208.43 on chip-demand reporting, in a tape where every major index slipped. That's the variant view: the AI-capex narrative is being repriced *upward* in the two sessions before MSFT and GOOGL print. The question worth researching is whether NVDA's pre-print strength is a leading indicator — hyperscalers leaking favorable capex commentary into their own quarter — or a sell-the-news risk into the most concentrated earnings 72 hours of the year. Define exit before entry: a Microsoft Azure print below ~35% (consensus is ~38%) would invalidate the bull thesis fastest, since NVDA's terminal demand sits downstream of every CSP capex line. Opportunistic only, sized accordingly.

## Risks to the call

The obvious risk: any one of the four Wednesday prints disappoints on AI revenue or guides capex lower, and the whole AI-infra complex — including NVDA's pre-print bid — unwinds in a single session. The less obvious risk: an Iran or Hormuz escalation pushes oil and the 10Y high enough to compress multiples regardless of what the prints say. Worth flagging on data quality, too: sources for today's tape disagreed on the index direction, with one summary describing a record close and multiple others describing a slip. We've gone with the multi-source read, but treat any single-day read-through as soft. The setup matters this week; the single day doesn't.
