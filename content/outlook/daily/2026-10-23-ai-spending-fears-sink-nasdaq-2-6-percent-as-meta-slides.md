## Top of mind

The Nasdaq Composite fell about 2.6% on Thursday, October 22, and the S&P 500 lost about 1.2% while the Dow finished roughly flat, as Meta (down about 7%), Tesla (down about 3%) and IBM all traded lower after earnings they had mostly beaten. The common thread was the cost and financing of AI spending, with a jump in oil prices adding to the pressure. For a long-term plus opportunistic investor, the signal is that beats are no longer enough when free cash flow and funding plans are in question.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Meta falls about 7% as capex climbs and free cash flow turns negative
**Source:** Kiplinger, Investing.com, BigGo Finance recaps
**So what:** Meta reportedly raised its capex floor to $135 billion and announced a new non-voting Class C stock to help fund AI work. Revenue topped expectations, yet the stock had its worst day since July. The market is now asking who bears the funding burden, and dilution of voting power is a new part of that conversation.

### 2. Tesla beats but still drops about 3%
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, Investing.com
**So what:** Adjusted EPS of $0.48 and free cash flow of about $1.5 billion were better than expected. Attention went to a lower 2026 capex outlook of around $20 billion alongside higher planned spending in 2027 and 2028, which muddies the near-term picture for AI and robotics suppliers.

### 3. IBM beats, then slides on AI-return doubts
**Source:** Yahoo Finance premarket coverage, Investing.com
**So what:** Gross margin of 58.5% came in below the 59.1% consensus, full-year constant-currency growth guidance stayed at 4%-5%, and the CEO said clients are asking whether AI spending is producing measurable returns. IBM was down as much as about 5.7% before the open; I could not confirm its closing move, so I am not quoting one.

### 4. Oil and Treasuries add a macro layer
**Source:** Benzinga, Schwab commentary, Techflow
**So what:** Reports that Houthi forces attacked Saudi energy facilities pushed Brent up roughly 4%, while the 10-year yield dropped back to about 5%, one source saying it was the largest one-day fall in over two years. Falling yields on rising oil is a flight-to-safety signature, not a growth-optimism one. Several of these figures are single-source, so treat the magnitudes as approximate.

## Portfolio-level guidance

*Allocation and risk observations. Not specific buy/sell calls — those depend on a full picture this report doesn't see.*

- **Concentration check:** A single day with the Nasdaq down 2.6% and the Dow flat is a reminder of how much index exposure is really AI-capex exposure. Check how much of your equity risk rides on the same handful of megacaps and chip names.
- **Rates positioning:** A 10-year near 5% that can move this much in a day argues for knowing your duration. Falling yields helped bonds on Thursday, but the same oil shock could reverse that quickly.
- **Cash & dry powder:** Having cash lets you wait for the earnings dust to settle instead of acting on a headline. There is no urgency signal in one session.
- **Risk regime read:** Risk-off in growth, steady in value. This is a rotation, not a panic, until breadth or credit say otherwise, and I could not verify VIX to cross-check that.
