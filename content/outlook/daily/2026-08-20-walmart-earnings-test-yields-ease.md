## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Treasury doubles long-bond buyback operations, yields retreat from multi-year highs
**Source:** CNBC, Bloomberg
**So what:** Treasury said it will raise the maximum size of its liquidity-support buybacks for 10-to-30-year debt from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, starting September 9. This isn't debt paydown — Treasury still issues new bonds at auction — but pulling illiquid older bonds off dealer balance sheets narrows bid-ask spreads and can ease borrowing-cost pressure. It came after a selloff pushed the 30-year to its highest yield since 2007 on fiscal and inflation concerns; the announcement, not new economic data, is what moved the tape.

### 2. Moderna closes up ~177% after Merck-partnered melanoma vaccine succeeds in Phase 3
**Source:** Bloomberg, Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance
**So what:** The Interpath-001 trial showed Moderna's personalized mRNA vaccine (intismeran, alongside Merck's Keytruda) reduced melanoma recurrence risk versus Keytruda alone — the first positive late-stage readout for an mRNA cancer vaccine and a data point that could reframe Moderna as an oncology platform rather than a shrinking COVID-vaccine business. Bloomberg put short-seller losses from the move near $5.5 billion, meaning a meaningful chunk of the gain is short-covering mechanics on top of the fundamental news. No FDA filing timeline has been announced; the companies said they'll present fuller data at an upcoming medical meeting.

### 3. Retail earnings week nearly complete: Target, TJX beat; Estée Lauder jumps on turnaround guidance; Walmart reports Thursday
**Source:** AP wire, StockStory, company releases
**So what:** Target and TJX both topped estimates Wednesday, and Estée Lauder rose roughly 8% premarket after Q4 organic sales grew 5% (a fourth straight quarter of growth) and FY27 guidance came in above consensus. That leaves Walmart, reporting before Thursday's open, as the read on whether the "resilient but selective" consumer narrative holds at the largest retailer. Options imply a 5.1% move, and the put/call ratio (1.56x) skews more bearish than bullish — a signal some positioning is hedged against a miss even with 26 of 29 analysts at Buy.

### 4. July FOMC minutes show a fractured, 9-3 vote with three regional presidents dissenting for a hike
**Source:** Federal Reserve, Bloomberg
**So what:** Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari, and Dallas's Lorie Logan all preferred a quarter-point hike at the July 28-29 meeting that ultimately held rates at 3.5%-3.75%. The minutes describe broader hawkish sentiment beyond the three dissenters, with participants noting financial conditions might not be tight enough to bring inflation to target. That's a notably hawkish document to have landed the same day stocks rallied on falling yields — the two stories are pulling in different directions.

### 5. Weekly jobless claims due Thursday after last week's rise to 209,000
**Source:** BLS, Bloomberg, Trading Economics
**So what:** Initial claims for the week ending August 8 came in at 209,000, above the 202,000 consensus and up from 200,000 the prior week. Thursday's release (week ending August 15) is the next data point on whether that uptick was noise or the start of a labor-cooling trend — relevant context heading into Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech next week.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### EL — Estée Lauder Companies
- **Thesis:** A multi-quarter growth inflection rather than a one-off beat — Q4 organic sales growth marked a fourth consecutive quarter of improvement, and management raised both FY27 EPS guidance ($3.10-$3.35, above the $3.18 Street estimate) and its adjusted operating-margin outlook (12.7%-13.5%, up from a preliminary 12.5%-13.0%). That's evidence a genuine cost and brand-portfolio turnaround is gaining traction, not just easy comps.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E around 26.7x, in the cheaper quartile of its peer set per GuruFocus/YCharts data; one discounted-cash-flow model (Simply Wall St) puts fair value near $91 against a roughly $69 share price pre-print — treat that gap as one model's estimate, not a target, but it's directionally consistent with "still cheap relative to the turnaround story."
- **Why now (or why patient):** The stock is already up roughly 8% premarket on the print, so some of this is priced. The longer thesis is whether four consecutive quarters of organic growth and a raised margin outlook continue for another year — that's a multi-quarter confirmation process, not a single trade.
- **Risks / bear case:** Net margin is still negative on a trailing basis, so this remains a story about the path to profitability, not current profitability. Prestige beauty's historical soft spots — China and travel retail — aren't guaranteed to stay fixed, and a premium multiple on an unprofitable trailing base gives little cushion if the turnaround stalls.
