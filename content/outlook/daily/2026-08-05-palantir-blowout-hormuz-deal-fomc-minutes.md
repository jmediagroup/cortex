## Top of mind

The S&P 500 and Dow closed at fresh record highs Tuesday — the Dow's first close above 54,000 — powered by Palantir's ~27% post-earnings surge and a broad tech/industrials rally, while oil kept sliding on optimism that the US and Iran are close to a deal reopening the Strait of Hormuz. That optimism gets tested today: Treasury Secretary Bessent said an agreement could land "within a day or two," the Fed releases July FOMC minutes this afternoon, and markets are still digesting a genuinely split reaction to Tuesday night's two marquee earnings — AMD beat and guided above consensus but whipsawed from a post-close drop to a premarket gain, while SpaceX beat on revenue but sold off on ballooning AI capital spending. Three straight sessions of records built substantially on AI-infrastructure names means today's mixed earnings reactions are worth watching closely, not waving off.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Palantir smashes Q2, stock has best day since 2024
**Source:** Benzinga, TradingKey
**So what:** Palantir reported Q2 revenue of $1.94 billion (vs. $1.80B consensus) and adjusted EPS of $0.41 (vs. $0.35 est.), then raised full-year revenue guidance to $8.15-8.16 billion versus a prior Street estimate of $7.69 billion. Shares jumped roughly 27-29%, the stock's best single-day move since February 2024. This is a genuine beat-and-raise, not just a beat — but it comes after the stock had already fallen ~40% from its highs, so the move is as much a valuation reset as a fundamentals story.

### 2. AMD beats and guides up, but the stock can't decide how to react
**Source:** Benzinga, Investing.com, StockTitan
**So what:** AMD posted Q2 revenue of $11.54 billion (vs. $11.28B est.) and EPS of $1.66 (vs. $1.61 est.), with data-center revenue more than doubling year-over-year to $6.7 billion — now 58% of total revenue. Q3 guidance of ~$13 billion topped the ~$12.52 billion consensus. Despite that, shares fell as much as 8.8% in after-hours trading Tuesday night before recovering to a premarket gain of roughly 5% Wednesday. The whipsaw suggests investors wanted more specificity on the Helios rack-scale AI platform than management gave — a reminder that "beat and raise" isn't automatically enough when expectations are this elevated.

### 3. SpaceX's first-ever public earnings: strong growth, bigger spending worries
**Source:** CNBC, CNN Business, Bloomberg
**So what:** In its debut quarterly report as a public company, SpaceX posted $7.8 billion in revenue (up 92% y/y, beating the ~$6.9B consensus) and a smaller-than-expected loss. But capital spending jumped to roughly $18.4 billion for the quarter, and shares fell about 7-7.5% in postmarket trading as investors weighed AI-unit spending against the growth. Cash on hand rose to $93.5 billion from $24.7 billion after the IPO. Management guided toward $100 billion in annualized recurring revenue by year-end — an ambitious marker the market will now hold them to.

### 4. Bessent: US-Iran deal on the Strait of Hormuz could land "within a day or two"
**Source:** Washington Times, The National
**So what:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US and Iran could reach an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to normal shipping within a day or two, with drafts reportedly circulating. Oil has already priced in a chunk of this optimism — WTI is down to the mid-$70s from over $100 at the height of the conflict — meaning a confirmed deal may be a "sell the news" event for crude, while a collapse in talks would be a sharper-than-expected shock given how much good news is already priced in.

### 5. Micron rides the AI-memory supercycle to a $1 trillion-plus valuation
**Source:** Benzinga, FX Leaders
**So what:** Micron shares rose roughly 4-5% Tuesday and again in Wednesday's premarket, testing the $900 level, as tight global DRAM supply and surging HBM demand from AI infrastructure buildouts (evidenced by Microsoft, Google, and Palantir's own capex commentary) keep the memory-pricing cycle running. The stock is up roughly 68% year-to-date. This is now a crowded, high-multiple trade — worth distinguishing conviction in the multi-year memory cycle from chasing a stock that's already tripled off its lows.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### MU — Micron Technology
- **Thesis:** A multi-year AI-driven memory supercycle — tight DRAM/HBM supply against surging AI-infrastructure demand — gives Micron a structurally better pricing environment than the boom-bust cycles that have historically capped memory-maker returns.
- **Valuation note:** Up ~68% year-to-date and testing a $900 share price with a $1 trillion-plus market cap, the easy money in this re-rating has likely been made; compare current DRAM-cycle pricing power against Micron's own history of overbuilding into strong demand before treating this as cheap.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. This is a name to build into on a pullback tied to a cyclical DRAM pricing wobble, not to chase after a run this large.
- **Risks / bear case:** Memory is a commodity business at its core — a supply response from Micron, SK Hynix, or Samsung, a slowdown in AI capex, or renewed China competitive pressure could compress margins quickly, and insider selling into the rally is worth tracking.

### AMD — Advanced Micro Devices
- **Thesis:** Data center now drives 58% of AMD's revenue (up from 42% a year ago) and more than doubled year-over-year, giving the company a credible second horse in the AI-compute race behind Nvidia, with a diversified base (client, gaming, embedded) that Nvidia lacks.
- **Valuation note:** Tuesday night's post-earnings whipsaw — an 8.8% after-hours drop despite a beat-and-raise, followed by a premarket recovery — suggests the market is pricing near-perfection into AMD's data-center trajectory; check forward P/E against the growth rate before assuming today's price is a discount.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patient. Better to see how the stock settles over the next few sessions once the market fully digests the Helios roadmap commentary than to buy into the volatility.
- **Risks / bear case:** A single quarter of "good but not blowout enough" data-center growth showed the stock can drop nearly 9% in an instant — any real deceleration in AI-chip demand would hit AMD alongside the rest of the semiconductor complex.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### AMD — post-earnings volatility trade
- **Catalyst:** Tuesday's Q2 beat-and-raise triggered an 8.8% after-hours drop that reversed into a ~5% premarket gain Wednesday — an unusually wide, unresolved reaction that today's full session should help settle.
- **Time horizon:** Through Wednesday's close and Thursday's session.
- **What would invalidate:** A fade back below Tuesday's after-hours low would signal the market's real verdict is negative despite the premarket bounce; a close near or above Tuesday's regular-session close would suggest the after-hours drop was overdone.
- **Risk note:** Implied volatility is elevated after a swing this size in under 24 hours — this is a name that can move 5%+ in either direction on sentiment alone right now.

### SPCX — SpaceX capex-versus-growth debate
- **Catalyst:** First-ever public earnings report beat on revenue and narrowed losses, but a jump to $18.4 billion in quarterly capex spooked the market into a ~7-7.5% postmarket decline.
- **Time horizon:** Weeks — through follow-on analyst commentary and the next update on the path to management's stated $100 billion ARR target.
- **What would invalidate:** Commentary suggesting AI-unit and Starship capex is running structurally ahead of revenue generation, without a clearer near-term monetization timeline, would extend the selloff.
- **Risk note:** This is the company's first quarter as a public entity with no earnings-reaction history to anchor expectations, and the float is still thin post-IPO — size for outsized volatility in either direction.

### PLTR — Palantir momentum post-beat-and-raise
- **Catalyst:** Q2 revenue and EPS beat, plus a full-year guidance raise to $8.15-8.16 billion, drove the stock's best single-day gain since February 2024.
- **Time horizon:** Days to weeks — through the initial momentum window and any follow-on analyst price-target revisions.
- **What would invalidate:** The stock still carries a rich multiple (forward P/E in the 75-140x range depending on methodology) even after its recent pullback — any broader AI-sentiment wobble or a disappointing follow-through in commercial bookings would unwind the move quickly.
- **Risk note:** A 27%+ single-day move is itself a risk signal — chasing it without a defined exit is a bet on continued momentum, not on the fundamentals alone.
