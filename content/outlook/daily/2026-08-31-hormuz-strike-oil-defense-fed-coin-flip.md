## Top of mind

The US military struck Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday — the first such strike in a month — after Tehran's Revolutionary Guard was seen preparing to send mines into the waterway, sending Brent crude above $90/barrel (+2.9%) and lighting up defense stocks premarket: Kratos Defense +10.1%, Lockheed Martin +6.7%, RTX +6.6%. That jolt lands on a market still digesting Friday's hawkish Jackson Hole keynote from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, which pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.73% and sent CME's September rate-hike odds to 57% (from 40% a week earlier) — even as Kalshi and Polymarket price the opposite outcome, a hold, at roughly 52%, leaving the Fed's next move a genuine coin flip heading into a data-heavy week.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. US strikes Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC, NBC News, Axios
**So what:** US Central Command said forces struck two Iranian launchers on Larak Island after IRGC forces were observed preparing to fire rockets carrying sea mines into the strait — the first acknowledged US strike on Iran in about a month, and a reversal from the administration's stated pivot toward economic rather than military pressure just days earlier. Iran's Revolutionary Guard acknowledged casualties and said it would respond, which is the acute tail risk for oil markets this week.

### 2. Brent tops $90 as roughly 6-8 million barrels a day keep flowing through Hormuz — for now
**Source:** Bloomberg, CNBC
**So what:** Brent rose above $90/barrel and WTI traded near $86 Monday morning on the renewed mine threat. Reporting notes that meaningful crude volumes are still transiting the strait despite the absence of a lasting US-Iran deal — the risk premium is about a potential future disruption, not a current one, which matters for how durable this move is.

### 3. Defense stocks jump premarket on renewed Hormuz tension
**Source:** Finviz, Seeking Alpha
**So what:** Kratos Defense led with a 10.1% premarket gain; Lockheed Martin (+6.7%), RTX (+6.6%), L3Harris (+5.6%), and Northrop Grumman (+5.2%) all followed. The move reflects investors positioning for prolonged regional instability and higher defense outlays rather than a single contract or event — but a premarket pop of this size on a single weekend headline is also exactly the kind of move that can partially reverse once the cash session opens.

### 4. Warsh's Jackson Hole speech turns the September Fed decision into a coin flip
**Source:** CNBC, Kalshi Research
**So what:** Warsh's Friday remarks — "I stand here today committed to a discipline, not to a decision" — were read as hawkish on inflation, and flagged PCE running near 3.7%, well above target. CME's FedWatch tool now prices a 57% chance of a 25bp hike at the September 16 meeting, up from 40% a week ago, while Kalshi and Polymarket price a hold at roughly 52% — a real divergence between futures positioning and prediction-market sentiment worth watching rather than picking a side on.

### 5. Gold sells off as the dollar rallies into the Fed repricing
**Source:** Trading Economics, CNBC
**So what:** Gold fell 3.2% Friday to roughly $4,454/oz, its sharpest one-day drop in over a week, as the dollar index rallied to a two-week high (99.65, +0.5%) on Warsh's hawkish tone. It's a reminder that gold's 2026 rally has been a rates-and-dollar story as much as a safe-haven one — Sunday's Iran strike hasn't (yet) reversed Friday's gold weakness, which is itself informative about which risk factor markets are pricing harder right now.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### LMT — Lockheed Martin
- **Thesis:** A prolonged, multi-front period of Middle East instability plus a US administration signaling it will keep striking Iranian military targets argues for a structurally higher, multi-year floor under Western defense budgets — not just a one-day headline trade. Lockheed's diversified platform mix (F-35, missile defense, space) gives it broader exposure to that theme than single-program peers.
- **Valuation note:** Worth checking Lockheed's forward P/E against its own five-year average and against RTX and Northrop before buying into today's pop — a name that's already up ~7% premarket on one weekend headline has, by definition, pulled some of that valuation re-rate forward.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Patience is the more defensible stance here — today's premarket move is a geopolitical headline reaction, not a new contract or budget line. A pullback once the immediate Hormuz news is digested would be a better research entry than chasing the gap-up.
- **Risks / bear case:** Defense budgets are still subject to congressional appropriations and political risk; a de-escalation or negotiated resolution with Iran (which the administration was reportedly favoring just days before this strike) would remove the acute catalyst and could see today's gains partly unwind.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### KTOS — Kratos Defense, Hormuz-headline momentum
- **Catalyst:** Monday's premarket leader among defense names (+10.1%) on the weekend Iran strike; as a smaller, higher-beta defense name, Kratos tends to move more on headline-driven sentiment than the primes do.
- **Time horizon:** Days — this is a momentum/follow-through question, not a new thesis on the company.
- **What would invalidate:** A giveback of more than half the premarket gain once the cash session opens and volume normalizes would suggest the move was a headline overreaction rather than durable repositioning; any sign of Iran-US de-escalation this week would remove the catalyst entirely.
- **Risk note:** Smaller-cap defense names are thinly traded relative to the primes — size accordingly, and don't mistake a premarket gap for a confirmed, liquid move until the opening print.

### XLE — Energy sector, Hormuz risk-premium trade
- **Catalyst:** Brent's move above $90 is a direct function of the mine threat to a strait that carries an estimated 6-8 million barrels a day; further Iranian retaliation or any actual disruption to shipping would extend the move, while any sign of the strait staying open would fade it fast.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a couple of weeks — tied to how the US-Iran standoff evolves this week, not a structural energy call.
- **What would invalidate:** Confirmation that tanker traffic through Hormuz is continuing unimpeded, or any US-Iran de-escalation signal, would likely reverse most of the risk-premium component of this move.
- **Risk note:** Geopolitical risk premiums in oil are notoriously fast to build and fast to unwind — this is a trade with an exit plan, not a long-term energy allocation decision.
