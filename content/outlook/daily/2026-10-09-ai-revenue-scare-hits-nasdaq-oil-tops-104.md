## Top of mind

Stocks fell for a second day on Thursday, October 8: the S&P 500 slid about 0.5% to 7,765.36 and the Nasdaq fell about 1.3%, while the Dow edged up about 0.1%. The trigger was a Financial Times report, as relayed by other outlets, that OpenAI's annualized revenue is $20 billion lower than previously estimated, which hit chipmakers, with Brent above $104 adding to inflation worries. The 30-year auction at 5.618% was decent but far above September's result, so the AI trade and the long end of the curve are now both pushing on valuations.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. OpenAI revenue report hits the AI trade
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, EconoTimes (citing a Financial Times report)
**So what:** The report that OpenAI's annualized revenue is $20 billion below earlier estimates questions the demand behind AI capex, which has carried the index records. Nvidia, Micron, and Intel fell in the afternoon. This is a secondhand characterization of the FT piece, so treat the exact figure as unverified.

### 2. 30-year auction clears at 5.618%
**Source:** CNBC and market commentary via search summaries
**So what:** Treasury sold $22 billion of 30-year bonds at a high yield of 5.618%, up 31 bps from September's 5.308%, with indirect bidders taking 72.3%. Commentators called it decent but weaker than Wednesday's 10-year sale. A steeper curve means long-duration assets, including growth stocks, still face a rising discount rate.

### 3. Oil above $104 keeps inflation worries alive
**Source:** Yahoo Finance, EconoTimes
**So what:** Higher crude feeds airline fuel bills and headline inflation at the moment the Fed is debating one more hike. Reports of geopolitical headlines around Iran moved yields intraday; I could not corroborate the specifics, so I am not relying on them.

### 4. PepsiCo beats, but trims its profit outlook
**Source:** LSEG consensus via search summaries
**So what:** Adjusted EPS of $2.34 on $25.27 billion revenue topped consensus of about $2.29 and $24.96 billion. Management still lowered its profit outlook as it works to regain US momentum. Shares rose roughly 2% to 3.7%, depending on the source and time, so the close is not pinned down.
