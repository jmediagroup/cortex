## Top of mind

July CPI matched consensus Wednesday (headline +0.1% m/m, 3.4% y/y; core +0.2% m/m, 2.5% y/y, the coolest core reading since 2021), and September rate-hike odds on CME FedWatch fell hard — from roughly 48% pre-release to somewhere in the 33–36% range afterward, depending on the tracker. That should be an unambiguously bullish setup, but Cisco's after-hours reaction complicates the picture: shares fell 4–5% despite record Q4 revenue ($17.3B), an EPS beat ($1.22 vs. $1.17 consensus), and AI-order guidance raised above $9B for the year. If beat-and-raise isn't enough for Cisco, tonight's Applied Materials report — the next AI-capex bellwether — is a real test of whether that pattern holds. PPI lands at 8:30am ET as the CPI read's last word before markets can fully exhale.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. July CPI matches forecasts; September hike odds tumble
**Source:** CNBC, Motley Fool, Forbes, Barchart
**So what:** Headline CPI rose 0.1% m/m (3.4% y/y) and core CPI rose 0.2% m/m (2.5% y/y, the softest annual core print since March 2021), both in line with Dow Jones consensus. Combined with last week's weak July jobs report, CME FedWatch showed September hike probability falling from around 48% pre-release into the mid-30s%, with hold-rate odds climbing toward 60–67% depending on the tracker. Directionally consistent across sources even where the exact percentage varies.

### 2. Cisco beats on every headline number, stock falls anyway
**Source:** SiliconANGLE, Investing.com, Blockonomi, TipRanks
**So what:** Cisco posted record Q4 revenue of $17.3B (+18% y/y) and adjusted EPS of $1.22 versus $1.17 consensus, with Q4 AI-infrastructure orders of $4B pushing the full-year AI order tally to $9.3B — above the raised $9B guide. Shares still fell roughly 4–5% after hours. Cisco trades around a 25x forward P/E (versus a ~40x trailing multiple), suggesting the market had already priced in the beat and is now parsing forward commentary and valuation rather than rewarding the headline print.

### 3. Applied Materials reports tonight — the next AI-capex bellwether
**Source:** Barchart, TradingKey, Yahoo Finance
**So what:** AMAT reports fiscal Q3 results after today's close (call at 4:30pm ET), with consensus at roughly $3.39 EPS on ~$9B revenue, against company guidance of $3.36 EPS ±$0.20 and $8.95B revenue ±$500M. As a semiconductor-equipment name, it's a read on whether AI-infrastructure capex is actually showing up in equipment orders — and after Cisco's beat-but-sell-off, whether "in line or better" is even enough to move a stock that's already pulled back roughly 25%+ from its 2026 high on China-export-restriction overhang.

### 4. Oil's six-day Hormuz rally finally cracks
**Source:** Bloomberg, Trading Economics
**So what:** Brent slipped toward $88/bbl and WTI below $83/bbl Thursday, each down roughly 1.2–1.4%, snapping a six-session advance built on the stalled Strait of Hormuz standoff. Trump's claim of "total control" over the waterway and ongoing Iran-US friction mean the rally could resume on any fresh escalation — this looks like profit-taking into a still-unresolved standoff, not a resolution.

### 5. Wendy's jumps 15% on report Trian is preparing a take-private bid
**Source:** Forbes, Motley Fool, Reuters (via wire)
**So what:** Wendy's shares surged as much as 15% Wednesday (closing near $8.66) after reports that Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management — which holds roughly 8% of the company, with Peltz personally holding about 16% — is assembling a consortium with BlueFive Capital and franchisee Flynn Group to take the burger chain private. No price has been set, and this is the second time in three months this idea has resurfaced; Wendy's said it would review any formal proposal under its fiduciary duties.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### CSCO — Cisco Systems
- **Thesis:** A rare case where a legacy networking incumbent is compounding AI-order growth (full-year AI orders raised above $9B, up from a $5B initial target) on top of a record, cash-generative core business — a lower-beta way to own enterprise AI-infrastructure spend than pure-play data-center names.
- **Valuation note:** Forward P/E around 25x, roughly in line with the hardware-industry median (~23x) — not cheap, but not the stretched multiple its ~40x trailing P/E might suggest, since that gap reflects expected earnings growth.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The after-hours sell-off despite a genuine beat-and-raise is the kind of disconnect worth watching rather than chasing — if the stock stabilizes over the next few sessions near current levels, it's a reasonable entry into a name that just proved out its AI-order thesis; if it keeps sliding, wait for a clearer valuation reset.
- **Risks / bear case:** If AI-infrastructure order growth decelerates from its recent pace, or the enterprise-networking business outside AI stays sluggish, the stock has no beat-and-raise catalyst left to lean on for several quarters.

## Ideas — opportunistic

### AMAT — Applied Materials, pre-earnings event
- **Catalyst:** Fiscal Q3 results tonight after the close (call at 4:30pm ET), consensus ~$3.39 EPS / ~$9B revenue against company guidance of $3.36 EPS / $8.95B revenue. - **Risk note:** AMAT already trades at an elevated multiple (mid-40s to mid-50s P/E depending on the measure) after a sharp pullback from its 2026 high — this is a volatile, defined-risk trade, not a valuation-support setup.

### WEN — Wendy's, M&A-driven trade
- **Catalyst:** Reports that Trian Fund Management (with BlueFive Capital and Flynn Group) is preparing a take-private bid, no price disclosed yet. A formal offer, a leaked price, or a company statement confirming/denying talks would each move the stock independently of any operating news.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, until a formal bid materializes or the story fades (as it did three months ago).
- **What would invalidate:** A company statement definitively rejecting a deal, or reporting suggesting the consortium has walked away, would remove the takeout premium currently in the stock.
- **Risk note:** This is speculation on unconfirmed deal talks with no price attached — a classic case where the stock can give back double-digit gains just as fast if the report doesn't turn into a real offer. Small size only.
