## Lead

The March CPI print landed hot — 3.2% headline versus the 2.9% consensus, with core sticky at 3.6%. The reflexive read was "no cuts in 2026," but the bond market disagreed almost immediately: 2-year yields gave back their entire intraday spike by the close, and Fed funds futures still price 1.75 cuts before December. The takeaway isn't that inflation is whipped — it's that the market believes the Fed has lost the appetite to fight a single quarter of stickier shelter prices.

## What we're watching

- **Energy unwind**: XLE has rolled over despite the inflation surprise. Brent is back below $78 and refinery margins are compressing. The setup is constructive for transports and discretionary, less so for the inflation-hedge trade.
- **Tech breadth**: The Mag-7 carried the index again Friday, but small-cap tech (the IGV ex-mega-cap basket) outperformed by 80bps. If breadth holds through earnings, the "AI bubble" narrative gets harder to argue.
- **Dollar weakness**: DXY broke below the 200-day for the first time since November. That's a tailwind for emerging markets and US multinationals — and a quiet headwind for anyone short EM credit.

## The decision worth making today

If you've been waiting for a "clean" entry into long-duration tech, the post-CPI dip is doing more work than the headline suggests. The bond market is telling you the Fed is on hold, not hiking. That's the regime where duration works — even at these multiples.

## Risks to the call

The obvious one: a hot April PCE on May 9th invalidates everything above. The less obvious one: a credit event from a regional bank exposed to office CRE could force the Fed's hand the *other* way and crater the cut probability. Position size accordingly.
