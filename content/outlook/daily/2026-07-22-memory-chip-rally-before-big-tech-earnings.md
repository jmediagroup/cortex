## Top of mind

Wall Street snapped a three-day losing streak Tuesday after Morgan Stanley reignited the memory-chip supercycle thesis, sending Micron, Western Digital and Seagate each roughly 12% higher and lifting the Nasdaq 1.29%; the move carried into Asia overnight as the Kospi jumped 5.95% to cross 7,000 for the first time. It's a real fundamentals story — Micron just posted a record $41.46 billion quarterly revenue, up from $9.3 billion a year ago — but it's also a narrow, one-subsector rally riding into the week's actual test: Alphabet and Tesla report tonight after the close, the first Big Tech earnings of the season and the clearest read yet on whether AI-capex optimism is broadening or just rotating between corners of the trade.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Morgan Stanley reignites the memory-chip trade with steep target hikes
**Source:** TipRanks / Yahoo Finance
**So what:** Analyst Joseph Moore lifted his SanDisk target to $1,750 (from $1,100) and Micron to $1,050 (from $520), arguing the DRAM/NAND supply crunch has no quick fix and could persist two to three years on relentless hyperscaler AI demand. It's one analyst's call, but it lines up with Micron's own record quarter reported in June — the kind of fundamentals-plus-sentiment combination that can extend a squeeze rather than just cause a one-day pop.

### 2. Micron, Western Digital, Seagate rally lifts the Nasdaq off a three-day slide
**Source:** CNBC
**So what:** Traders bid up memory-chip names ahead of this week's Big Tech reports, with Micron, Western Digital and Seagate each adding roughly 12% and the Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) up 11%. All three major indexes broke a three-session losing streak, but the gains were concentrated in one corner of tech rather than spread broadly — a distinction that matters for how durable the bounce is.

### 3. Kospi crosses 7,000, Nikkei rallies as the chip trade goes global
**Source:** Invezz; The Asia Business Daily
**So what:** South Korea's Kospi jumped 5.95% to reclaim the 7,000 level and Japan's Nikkei rose 1.76%, led by Samsung, SK Hynix and Kioxia, as the US memory-chip rally and unwinding of leveraged short positions fed through to Asian semiconductor names. It's the mirror image of the Kospi's bear-market rout two weeks ago — a reminder of how quickly sentiment is swinging on the same underlying AI-memory story.

### 4. TSMC finalizes 5-10% chip price hikes for 2027
**Source:** Bloomberg (via Nikkei)
**So what:** Taiwan Semiconductor has finalized base-price increases of 5-10% across both advanced and mature nodes, effective next year, to cover rising manufacturing and overseas-fab costs. TSMC itself has said it's holding back relative to memory makers' margins because its customers "would not survive" proportionally larger hikes — a sign the supply/demand imbalance in chips is broader than just memory, even if the pricing power isn't distributed evenly.

### 5. Alphabet and Tesla headline the week's real test; Tesla already posted record deliveries
**Source:** CNBC; Alphastreet; Electrek
**So what:** Alphabet and Tesla both report Q2 results tonight after the close — the first Magnificent Seven prints of the season. Consensus for Alphabet is $2.89 EPS and $116.8 billion revenue (+21% year-over-year), with Google Cloud growth of roughly 63% the number to watch as an AI-capex-payoff signal. Tesla already reported record Q2 deliveries of 480,126 vehicles (+25% year-over-year, well above the ~406,000 estimate) two weeks ago; tonight's print will show whether margins and cash flow kept pace with that delivery beat.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### MU — Micron Technology
- **Thesis:** Micron sits at the center of the AI-memory bottleneck — DRAM and high-bandwidth memory are now widely cited as the binding constraint on AI data-center buildouts, not GPUs. The company just posted fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $41.46 billion (a fifth consecutive quarterly record), up from $23.86 billion the prior quarter and $9.3 billion a year ago, with GAAP gross margin of 84.6% and guidance for roughly 86% next quarter.
- **Valuation note:** Today's rally (~+12%) is layered on top of that June earnings beat and Morgan Stanley's fresh $1,050 target — the stock has re-rated hard in a short window, so much of the "cheap relative to growth" gap that existed a month ago has already closed.
- **Why now (or why patient):** The bull case (2-3 years of tight supply, per Morgan Stanley) argues for treating dips as buying opportunities rather than chasing the day after a 12% pop; patient accumulation on pullbacks fits the long-term thesis better than buying into today's move specifically.
- **Risks / bear case:** Memory has historically been one of the most brutal boom-bust cycles in semis — today's shortage narrative is exactly the kind of consensus view that has reversed sharply before. Micron's own capex is ramping to meet demand, which pressures near-term free cash flow, and any sign hyperscalers are over-ordering (a "bullwhip" risk) would hit the stock hard given how much good news is now priced in.

### GOOGL — tonight's Cloud print as the AI-capex referendum
- **Catalyst:** Alphabet reports Q2 results tonight, July 22, after the close. Consensus is $2.89 EPS (+25% year-over-year) on $116.8 billion revenue (+21%), with Google Cloud growth of roughly 63% the figure markets will react to hardest, alongside any update to the $180-190 billion 2026 capex guide.
- **Time horizon:** Days — the immediate reaction tonight into Thursday, with Intel's print adding a second AI-capex data point.
- **What would invalidate:** A Cloud deceleration or cautious capex commentary would suggest today's chip-led optimism got ahead of the fundamentals; an accelerating number with confident capex guidance would support the case that the rally has legs beyond memory chips.
- **Risk note:** Single-print, binary-feeling reaction — Alphabet has moved sharply on capex commentary multiple times this year. Size for a gap in either direction.

### TSLA — margins and robotaxi commentary after an already-confirmed delivery beat
- **Catalyst:** Tesla also reports tonight after the close. The delivery number is already known (480,126 vehicles, +25% year-over-year, beating the ~406,000 estimate), so tonight's print is about whether margins (consensus sees automotive gross margin ex-credits just above 18%) and cash flow (analysts model roughly negative $3.25 billion free cash flow on ~$6.7 billion of capex) kept pace, plus any robotaxi and Optimus deployment update.
- **Time horizon:** Days — the Wednesday-night reaction, though TSLA's volatility means moves can extend for sessions afterward.
- **What would invalidate:** A margin miss or another vague robotaxi timeline (the company has missed its own short-term robotaxi targets in each of the last three earnings reports) would undercut the delivery beat's read-through; a concrete robotaxi expansion update could spark a sharp re-rating given how much scrutiny that specific question is getting on the earnings call.
- **Risk note:** High-volatility, headline-driven name reporting the same night as Alphabet — expect the two reactions to compete for attention and liquidity. Size conservatively into a binary event.
