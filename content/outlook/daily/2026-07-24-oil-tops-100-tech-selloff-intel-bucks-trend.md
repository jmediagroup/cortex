## Top of mind

Thursday was the S&P 500's worst day in a month — down 1.21% — as Alphabet (-7%) and Tesla (roughly -14% to -15%, sources vary slightly) both got hammered on their earnings reports and Brent crude broke above $100 a barrel for the first time in two months on an escalating Iran conflict. The obvious risk is that oil-driven inflation forces the Fed's hand at next week's meeting; the less obvious one is that Intel's after-hours beat-and-raise — its fastest revenue growth in 15 years — shows the "AI capex is a risk" narrative from Alphabet's report isn't universal. It's a dispersion story as much as a selloff, and next week's FOMC decision plus Microsoft, Meta, and Apple earnings will test which read is right.

## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Alphabet and Tesla earnings reactions turn into a broad tech selloff
**Source:** Washington Post; Yahoo Finance; CNBC
**So what:** Alphabet fell about 7% and Tesla roughly 14-15% as Thursday's session played out, confirming Wednesday night's after-hours moves and then some. The Nasdaq 100 lost about 1.9% and the S&P posted its worst day in a month. Two idiosyncratic stories (a capex guide hike, a margin miss) turned into a market-wide risk-off day once oil piled on.

### 2. Brent crude breaks $100 for the first time in two months
**Source:** CNN; Trading Economics; SpecialEurasia intel brief
**So what:** Brent rose roughly 7% Thursday to about $100.65 and held above $100 into Friday morning. CENTCOM has conducted 13 consecutive nights of strikes on Iranian military infrastructure, and Houthi forces hit two Saudi-flagged tankers in the Red Sea this week — the primary alternative export route if Hormuz transit stays constrained. Iran's army spokesman said Wednesday that "preparations for secondary retaliatory operational scenarios are complete," a rhetorical escalation worth tracking into the weekend.

### 3. Intel's beat-and-raise is the week's contrarian data point
**Source:** CNBC; Bloomberg; TradingKey
**So what:** Intel posted $16.1 billion in revenue (+25% year-over-year, its fastest growth in roughly 15 years) against a $14.42 billion estimate, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 beating the $0.21 consensus, and guided Q3 revenue to $15.8-16.8 billion versus $15.1 billion expected. Shares jumped as much as 12-13% after hours. The GAAP headline — a roughly $11 billion net loss — was driven almost entirely by a $12.5 billion non-cash mark-to-market charge tied to the CHIPS Act equity stake, not operations; restructuring charges actually fell sharply from a year ago. Foundry revenue grew 31% and management said the company is now supply-constrained on data center demand, with 10 new long-term customer agreements signed.

### 4. 10-year yield hits its highest level since January 2025
**Source:** Trading Economics; multiple yield aggregators
**So what:** The 10-year climbed to 4.71% Friday, a fourth consecutive up session, as oil's spike revives the inflation debate right as the Fed enters its blackout period ahead of next week's meeting. Estimates of the odds of a hold at the July 28-29 FOMC meeting range widely across sources — from roughly 89% (CME FedWatch-style pricing cited by one outlet) to a "1 in 3" chance of a hike (per another) — a real disagreement worth resolving before leaning on any single probability.

### 5. Asia absorbs Friday's contagion as sidecar curbs trigger in Seoul
**Source:** investingLive Asia-Pacific wrap
**So what:** South Korea's KOSPI fall was sharp enough to trigger sidecar trading curbs, with KOSDAQ futures down more than 6% triggering a similar halt, and Japan's Nikkei fell around 2.5% in sympathy. The regional reaction was considerably sharper than Friday morning's modest US futures dip (S&P futures -0.13%, Nasdaq 100 futures -0.44%), suggesting Asian markets are pricing more capex-cycle risk into chip-adjacent names than US futures currently reflect.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### INTC — Intel
- **Thesis:** 25% revenue growth (fastest in roughly 15 years), a foundry business growing 31% and now supply-constrained, and 10 new long-term customer agreements point to the 18A turnaround finally showing up in the numbers rather than just the roadmap. Data center demand reportedly exceeds what Intel can currently produce.
- **Valuation note:** The stock has already had an enormous run into this print (one source cites 317% year-over-year), so a lot of good news is priced in; the after-hours pop faded from an intraday high near $110 back toward the $100 area, suggesting the market isn't fully convinced yet either.
- **Why now (or why patient):** This is a "why now" setup only for investors who believe the yield improvement (85% on 18A, up from 65% last quarter) is durable and not a one-quarter data point — patient investors may prefer to wait for a second confirming quarter before sizing up.
- **Risks / bear case:** The GAAP net loss, even though CHIPS-Act-driven and non-operational, is a reminder of how much dilution and government entanglement is baked into Intel's turnaround financing. Foundry customer concentration and the difficulty of scaling 18A yields beyond current levels remain real execution risks, and the stock's run has left little room for a stumble.

## Ideas — opportunistic

*Catalyst-driven, time-bound, sized smaller. Hold horizon: days to months. Define exit before entry.*

### XLE — energy on a $100 oil breakout
- **Catalyst:** Brent's break above $100 for the first time in two months, on CENTCOM's continuing strikes on Iran and fresh Houthi tanker attacks in the Red Sea. Energy was Thursday's second-best sector.
- **Time horizon:** Days to a few weeks, tracking headline flow out of the Persian Gulf and Red Sea.
- **What would invalidate:** Any ceasefire or de-escalation signal would likely reverse the oil bid quickly — this conflict has round-tripped on diplomatic headlines multiple times already this year. Iran's stated preparation for "secondary retaliatory scenarios" also cuts both ways: it could mean further escalation or posturing ahead of talks.
- **Risk note:** This remains a geopolitical-headline trade, not a fundamentals-driven one. Energy names can gap violently in either direction on a single headline — size small and set an exit level before entering.

### TSLA — post-selloff stabilization watch
- **Catalyst:** Tesla's roughly 14-15% Thursday drop on the margin miss (operating margin near 1.4% versus 4.1% a year ago) was one of its worst single-day moves in years; a stock that falls this hard this fast sometimes finds a short-term bounce as forced-selling pressure exhausts itself.
- **Time horizon:** Days — this is about whether Friday and early next week show signs of stabilization, not a multi-month thesis.
- **What would invalidate:** Continued selling into Friday's close without any volume climax, or fresh negative headlines on robotaxi timelines or delivery guidance, would argue the selloff isn't done. No confirmed robotaxi deployment timeline remains a standing overhang.
- **Risk note:** This is a mean-reversion research candidate, not a fundamentals call — the margin deterioration is real and unresolved. Treat any bounce as tradeable, not investable, until margins show a clear inflection.
