## Headlines & analysis

### 1. Powell holds at 3.5%–3.75% in likely final FOMC meeting as chair
**Source:** CNBC, Kiplinger, PBS NewsHour, NBC News (April 29, 2026)
**So what:** The third consecutive hold was never in doubt (100% market-implied probability). What matters more is the transition: Powell's term ends May 15, and Kevin Warsh — a known hawk who opposed QE and has been critical of the Fed's post-pandemic framework — is the nominated successor. Markets have barely blinked at the changeover, but Warsh's historical stance implies fewer cuts, a longer hold, and less forward-guidance transparency. His first signals between now and June's meeting will be the real rates catalyst.

### 2. Four Mag 7 companies report in an "80-second window" after the close tonight
**Source:** Bloomberg, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance, Morningstar (April 28–29, 2026)
**So what:** Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta report Q1 results tonight. Combined, these four names represent roughly $8T in market cap and ~25% of the S&P 500. Options markets priced 5–7% moves in individual names. Polymarket put beat probability at 92–96% for all four — the question is not whether they beat, it is whether guidance language convinces investors that $600–700B in combined 2026 AI capex is yielding returns. Beat-and-guide-higher is the bull thesis; beat-and-hold with vague AI ROI language is the "sell the news" scenario.

### 3. Q1 GDP advance estimate lands tomorrow — Atlanta Fed tracking at 1.2%
**Source:** Atlanta Fed GDPNow, Polymarket, CEPR, Kraken Economic Brief (April 22–29, 2026)
**So what:** The BEA releases the advance Q1 2026 GDP estimate at 8:30 a.m. ET on April 30, alongside March PCE — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. The Atlanta Fed's GDPNow model tracked Q1 at 1.2% annualized; Polymarket consensus clusters around 2.0–3.0%. A print near 1.2% while PCE remains elevated would be the textbook stagflation signal — and it arrives the morning after four Mag 7 earnings reactions are still being digested. The calendar compression is unusually dangerous.

### 4. OpenAI revenue miss continues to shadow AI hardware names
**Source:** WSJ (April 28, 2026, via TheStreet, CNBC)
**So what:** The WSJ reported that OpenAI missed its own 2025 revenue and user growth targets, with CFO Sarah Friar flagging concerns about the company's ability to meet computing contracts if top-line growth doesn't accelerate. This hit chip stocks hard on April 28 (Nvidia -1.5%, Broadcom -4%, AMD -3%) and frames tonight's Mag 7 prints in an uncomfortable light: if the world's leading AI consumer is struggling to convert compute into revenue, the hyperscalers' AI capex stories need to work harder tonight.

### 5. Gas prices hit $4.18/gallon — highest since August 2022
**Source:** CBS News, CNBC (April 28–29, 2026)
**So what:** The national average for regular gasoline hit $4.176 per gallon, driven by WTI crude near $99 and the Iran war disruption to Strait of Hormuz traffic. If tomorrow's March PCE print comes in above 4%, the Fed's "patient" framing becomes structurally harder to defend post-Powell.

## Ideas — long-term core

*Quality businesses, durable competitive advantages, reasonable valuation. Hold horizon: years.*

### META — Meta Platforms
- **Thesis:** Meta's AI advertising engine is the most commercially validated AI application among public companies today. Advantage+ and Llama-powered ad targeting have driven 14 consecutive quarters of revenue beats; Q1 2026 consensus calls for +31% YoY revenue growth to $55.5B. The $115–135B capex guided for 2026 resolves as a long-term hold if AI ROI continues showing up in advertising yield improvements — and it has, every quarter for 3.5 years.
- **Valuation note:** META trades at a premium to communication services peers, justified by the AI monetization story. The premium is defensible as long as revenue growth stays above 25% YoY; it compresses rapidly if tonight's guidance signals deceleration or capex creep without revenue matching.
- **Why now (or why patient):** Tonight's Q1 2026 print is the latest validation point. A 14-quarter beat streak makes the base case clear. The long-term hold doesn't require tonight to be perfect — it requires the AI ad flywheel to remain intact over a 3–5 year horizon. Any post-earnings dip driven by capex-guidance noise (not fundamental deterioration) is an accumulation opportunity.
- **Risks / bear case:** EU AI Act compliance costs could be material and unquantified. FTC antitrust risk is ongoing. Any upward capex revision tonight without matching revenue guidance expansion spooks the multiple immediately — that's the near-term bear case in a single sentence. Longer-term: advertiser concentration makes the business structurally cyclical, and any macro downturn disproportionately hits discretionary ad budgets.

- **Catalyst:** WTI near $99 with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively closed. Trump canceled the Pakistan-mediated Iran talks trip, signaling no resolution is imminent. - **Risk note:** The Hormuz long is the single most crowded macro trade in the market. When the Strait reopens — and it will — the exit will be fast and deep. This is a momentum setup with a hard stop, not a thesis trade.
